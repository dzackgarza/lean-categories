### Definition/convention — Riemannian metric {#fc08-c13-u001}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC08-C13-U001` · Ch. 13, § Riemannian Manifolds; source L9788 · [not formalized]{.route-unmatched}
:::

Transferring these ideas to manifolds, we obtain one of the most important applications of tensors to differential geometry. Let M be a smooth manifold with or without boundary. A Riemannian metric on M is a smooth symmetric covariant 2-tensor field on M that is positive definite at each point. A Riemannian manifold is a pair $( M , g )$ , where M is a smooth manifold and $g$ is a Riemannian metric on M . One sometimes simply says “M is a Riemannian manifold” if M is understood to be endowed with a specific Riemannian metric. A Riemannian manifold with boundary is defined similarly.

An important generalization of Riemannian metrics is obtained by relaxing the positivity requirement. A symmetric 2-tensor $g$ on a vector space V is said to be nondegenerate if the linear map $\widehat { g } \colon V \to V ^ { * }$ defined by $\widehat g ( v ) ( w ) = g ( v , w )$ is an isomorphism, or equivalently if for every nonzero $v \in V$ there exists $w \in V$ such that $g ( v , w ) \neq 0$ .
