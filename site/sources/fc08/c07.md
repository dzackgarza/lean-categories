### Definition/convention — Lie group {#fc08-c07-u001}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC08-C07-U001` · Ch. 7, § Basic Definitions; source L4100 · [in Mathlib]{.route-mathlib}
:::

A Lie group is a smooth manifold $G$ (without boundary) that is also a group in the algebraic sense, such that the multiplication map $m \colon G \times G \to G$ and the inversion map $i \colon G \to G$, given by $$ m(g,h) = gh, \qquad i(g) = g^{-1}, $$ are both smooth. A Lie group is in particular a topological group.

::: {.unit-lean}
**Formalized.** [`Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Algebra/LieGroup.lean::LieGroup`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Algebra/LieGroup.html) — `LieGroup I ∞ G` extends `ContMDiffMul`, which itself extends `IsManifold`, and adds smooth inversion; together with `Group G` this is exactly Lee’s group-plus-smooth-manifold compatibility. Mathlib permits models with corners, so Lee’s boundaryless finite-dimensional real convention is the corresponding specialization rather than an extra theorem.
:::

### Example 7.3 {#fc08-c07-u004}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC08-C07-U004` · Ch. 7, § Basic Definitions; source L4130 · after [`FC08-C01-U038`](c01.html#fc08-c01-u038) · [not formalized]{.route-unmatched}

(Lie Groups). Each of the following is a Lie group with the indicated operation. (a) The general linear group $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})$, the invertible real $n \times n$ matrices: multiplication is smooth because the entries of $AB$ are polynomials in those of $A$ and $B$. (b) $\mathrm{GL}^{+}(n,\mathbb{R})$, the matrices of positive determinant: since $\det(AB) = (\det A)(\det B)$ and $\det(A^{-1}) = 1/\det A$ it is a subgroup, and as the preimage of $(0,\infty)$ under $\det$ it is open, hence an $n^{2}$-dimensional manifold. (c) Any open subgroup $H \subseteq G$ of a Lie group, by the argument of (b). (d) The complex general linear group $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})$, an open submanifold of $M(n,\mathbb{C})$ and so a $2n^{2}$-dimensional manifold, since products and inverses are smooth in the real and imaginary parts of the entries. (e) $\mathrm{GL}(V)$, the invertible linear maps of a real or complex vector space $V$ to itself; its smooth structure is independent of the choice of basis. (f) $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ under addition, the coordinates of $x - y$ being linear in $(x,y)$. (g) Likewise $\mathbb{C}$ and $\mathbb{C}^{n}$ under addition. (h) $\mathbb{R}^{*}$, the nonzero reals under multiplication, a 1-dimensional Lie group — indeed $\mathrm{GL}(1,\mathbb{R})$ — with $\mathbb{R}^{+}$ an open subgroup. (i) $\mathbb{C}^{*}$, the nonzero complex numbers, 2-dimensional, identified with $\mathrm{GL}(1,\mathbb{C})$. (j) The circle $\mathbb{S}^{1} \subseteq \mathbb{C}^{*}$ under complex multiplication: in angle coordinates multiplication and inversion read $(\theta_{1},\theta_{2}) \mapsto \theta_{1} + \theta_{2}$ and $\theta \mapsto -\theta$, so $\mathbb{S}^{1}$ is a Lie group, the circle group. (k) The direct product $G_{1} \times \cdots \times G_{k}$ of Lie groups, with componentwise multiplication $(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})(g'_{1},\dots,g'_{k}) = (g_{1}g'_{1},\dots,g_{k}g'_{k})$. (l) The $n$-torus $\mathbb{T}^{n} = \mathbb{S}^{1} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{S}^{1}$, an $n$-dimensional abelian Lie group. (m) Any group with the discrete topology is a topological group, and if finite or countable it is a zero-dimensional Lie group, a discrete Lie group.

### Definition/convention — Lie group isomorphism {#fc08-c07-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC08-C07-U005` · Ch. 7, § Lie Group Homomorphisms; source L4166 · [in Mathlib]{.route-mathlib}
:::

If G and H are Lie groups, a Lie group homomorphism from G to H is a smooth map $F \colon G \to H$ that is also a group homomorphism. It is called a Lie group isomorphism if it is also a diffeomorphism, which implies that it has an inverse that is also a Lie group homomorphism. In this case we say that G and H are isomorphic Lie groups. ##

::: {.unit-lean}
**Formalized.** [`Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Algebra/Monoid.lean::ContMDiffMonoidMorphism`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Algebra/Monoid.html); [`Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Diffeomorph.lean::Diffeomorph`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Diffeomorph.html); [`Mathlib/Algebra/Group/Hom/Defs.lean::MonoidHom.inverse`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Group/Hom/Defs.html#MonoidHom.inverse) — For groups, `ContMDiffMonoidMorphism I I′ ∞ G H` is precisely a group homomorphism whose underlying map is smooth. Requiring the same underlying map to be a `Diffeomorph` is Lee’s Lie-group-isomorphism condition; [`MonoidHom.inverse`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/find/?pattern=MonoidHom.inverse#doc) records that the inverse of the resulting bijective group homomorphism is again a homomorphism.
