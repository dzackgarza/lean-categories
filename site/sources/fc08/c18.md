For any topological space $M$ and abelian group $G$ one also defines the singular cohomology groups $H^{p}(M;G)$ with coefficients in $G$. Only $G = \mathbb{R}$ matters here, and then $H^{p}(M;\mathbb{R})$ is a real vector space naturally isomorphic to $\operatorname{Hom}(H_{p}(M),\mathbb{R})$, which may be taken as the definition. A continuous map $F \colon M \to N$ induces $F^{*} \colon H^{p}(N;\mathbb{R}) \to H^{p}(M;\mathbb{R})$ by $(F^{*}\gamma)[c] = \gamma(F_{*}[c])$, and the functorial properties carry over: $(G \circ F)^{*} = F^{*} \circ G^{*}$ and $(\operatorname{Id}_{M})^{*} = \operatorname{Id}$. So $p$th singular cohomology with real coefficients is a contravariant functor from the topological category to real vector spaces and linear maps.
