Definition/convention — map

FC08-C02-U001 · Ch. 2, § Smooth Functions and Smooth Maps; source L983 · not formalized

Although the terms function and map are technically synonymous, in studying smooth manifolds it is often convenient to make a slight distinction between them. Throughout this book we generally reserve the term function for a map whose codomain is R (a real-valued function) or \(\mathbb { R } ^ { k }\) for some \(k > 1\) (a vector-valued function). Either of the words map or mapping can mean any type of map, such as a map between arbitrary manifolds. ##

Proposition 2.5

FC08-C02-U007 · Ch. 2, § Smooth Maps Between Manifolds; source L1025 · in Mathlib

(Equivalent Characterizations of Smoothness). Suppose \(M\) and \(N\) are smooth manifolds with or without boundary and \(F \colon M \to N\) is a map. Then \(F\) is smooth if and only if either of the following holds. (a) For every \(p \in M\) there exist smooth charts \((U,\varphi)\) containing \(p\) and \((V,\psi)\) containing \(F(p)\) such that \(U \cap F^{-1}(V)\) is open in \(M\) and the composite \(\psi \circ F \circ \varphi^{-1}\) is smooth from \(\varphi(U \cap F^{-1}(V))\) to \(\psi(V)\).
