### Definition/convention — multilinear {#fc08-c12-u001}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC08-C12-U001` · Ch. 12, § Multilinear Algebra; source L8889 · [in Mathlib]{.route-mathlib}
:::

Suppose $V_{1},\dots,V_{k}$ and $W$ are vector spaces. A map $F \colon V_{1} \times \cdots \times V_{k} \to W$ is multilinear if it is linear in each variable separately when the others are held fixed: for each $i$, $$ F(v_{1},\dots,av_{i} + a'v_{i}',\dots,v_{k}) = aF(v_{1},\dots,v_{i},\dots,v_{k}) + a'F(v_{1},\dots,v_{i}',\dots,v_{k}). $$ The multilinear maps form a vector space $\operatorname{L}(V_{1},\dots,V_{k};W)$ under pointwise addition and scalar multiplication.

::: {.unit-lean}

Its dependent-domain form covers Lee’s possibly different vector spaces `V₁,…,Vₖ`.

(Tensor Products of Covectors). Suppose V is a vector space, and ω , η ∈ V * . Define a function ω ⊗ η : V × V → ℝ by ω ⊗ η (v _1, v _2) = ω (v _1) η (v _2), where the product on the right is just ordinary multiplication of real numbers. The linearity of ! and η guarantees that ω ⊗ η is a bilinear function of v _1 and v _2 , so it is an element of L ( V , V ; ℝ ) . For example, if ( e ^1 , e ^2 ) denotes the standard dual basis for ( ℝ ^2 ) * , then e ^1 ⊗ e ^2 : ℝ ^2 × ℝ ^2 → ℝ is the bilinear function e ^1 ⊗ e ^2 ((w, x), (y, z)) = w z. ### Definition/convention — formal linear combination of elements of S {#fc08-c12-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC08-C12-U005` · Ch. 12, § Abstract Tensor Products of Vector Spaces; source L8999 · [in Mathlib]{.route-mathlib}
:::

To begin, we need to make sense of “formal linear combinations.” Let S be a set. Roughly speaking, a formal linear combination of elements of S is an expression of the form scriptstyle sum _i = 1 ^m a _i x _i , where a _1 , … , a _m are real numbers and x _1 , … , x _m are elements of S. Of course, since we are not assuming that S has any algebraic structure, we cannot literally add elements of S together or multiply them by numbers. But the essential feature of such an expression is that it is completely determined by which elements of S appear in the sum, and what coefficients appear with them. Thus, we make the following definition: for any set S, a formal linear combination of elements of S is a function f : S → ℝ such that f ( s ) = 0 for all but finitely many s ∈ S . The free (real) vector space on S , denoted by F ( S ) , is the set of all formal linear combinations of elements of S. Under pointwise addition and scalar multiplication, F ( S ) becomes a vector space over R.

::: {.unit-lean}
**Formalized.** [`Mathlib/Data/Finsupp/Defs.lean::Finsupp`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Data/Finsupp/Defs.html); [`Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Finsupp/Defs.lean::Finsupp.instModule`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Finsupp/Defs.html#Finsupp.instModule) — `S →₀ ℝ` is exactly the type of finitely supported coefficient functions on `S`, with pointwise additive and real-module structure: Lee’s free real vector space on the set `S`.
