Convention/notation — orbit space of the action

FC08-C21-U001 · Ch. 21, § Quotients of Manifolds by Group Actions; source L16299 · in Mathlib

are given an action of a group G on a topological space M; which we write either as \(\theta \colon G \times M \to M\) or as \(( g , p ) \mapsto g \cdot p\) . (For definiteness, let us assume that \(G\) acts on the left; similar considerations apply to right actions.) Recall that the orbit of a point \(p \in M\) is the set of images of \(p\) under all elements of the group: \[ G \cdot p = \{g \cdot p: g \in G \}. \] Define a relation on M by setting \(p \sim q\) if there exists \(g \in G\) such that \(g \cdot p = q\) This is an equivalence relation, whose equivalence classes are exactly the orbits of G in M . The set of orbits is denoted by \(M / G\) ; with the quotient topology it is called the orbit space of the action. It is of great importance to determine conditions under which an orbit space is a smooth manifold. It is important to be aware that some authors use the notation \(G \backslash M\) for an orbit space determined by a left action of G on M; reserving \(M / G\) for one determined by a right action. We use only the latter notation, relying on the context, not the notation, to distinguish between the two cases.

Formalized. Example 21.2

FC08-C21-U003 · Ch. 21, § Quotients of Manifolds by Group Actions; source L16323 · after FC08-C05-U043 · not formalized

(Orbit Spaces of Smooth Lie Group Actions). (a) For the trivial action of a group \(G\) on a smooth manifold \(M\), the orbits are points and \(M/G = M\). (b) \(\mathbb{R}^{k}\) acts on \(\mathbb{R}^{k} \times \mathbb{R}^{n}\) by translation in the first factor, \(v \cdot (x,y) = (v+x,\ y)\), and the quotient map \(\pi \colon \mathbb{R}^{k} \times \mathbb{R}^{n} \to \mathbb{R}^{n}\) is a smooth submersion. (c) The circle group \(\mathbb{S}^{1}\) acts on \(\mathbb{C}\) by complex multiplication, \(z \cdot w = zw\); the orbits are the circles centered at the origin together with \(\{0\}\), and the orbit space is not a manifold. (d) The natural action of \(\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})\) on \(\mathbb{R}^{n}\) has an orbit space that is not even Hausdorff. (e) Restricting that action gives a smooth action of \(\mathrm{O}(n)\) on \(\mathbb{R}^{n}\) whose orbit space is homeomorphic to \([0,\infty)\): for nonzero \(v, v'\) of equal norm, completing \(v/|v|\) and \(v'/|v'|\) to orthonormal bases and taking the orthogonal matrices \(A\), \(A'\) with those columns, \(A'A^{-1}\) carries \(v\) to \(v'\). (f) Deleting the origin makes all three orbit spaces manifolds: \((\mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\})/\mathbb{S}^{1}\) and \((\mathbb{R}^{n} \setminus \{0\})/\mathrm{O}(n)\) are homeomorphic to \(\mathbb{R}^{+}\), and \((\mathbb{R}^{n} \setminus \{0\})/\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})\) is a point. (g) Restricting further gives an action of \(\mathrm{O}(n)\) on \(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\), smooth by Corollary 5.30 since \(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\) is an embedded submanifold of \(\mathbb{R}^{n}\).

Definition/convention — a proper action

FC08-C21-U005 · Ch. 21, § Quotients of Manifolds by Group Actions; source L16349 · in Mathlib

To avoid pathological cases such as these, we need to introduce one more restriction on our group actions. A continuous left action of a Lie group G on a manifold M is said to be a proper action if the map \(G \times M \to M \times M\) given by \(( g , p ) \mapsto ( g \cdot p , p )\) is a proper map. Note that this is generally a weaker condition than requiring that the map \(G \times M \to M\) defining the action be a proper map (see Problem 21-1).

Formalized. Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/ProperAction/Basic.lean::ProperSMul, ProperSMul.isProperMap_smul_pair — ProperSMul G X is defined exactly by properness of (g,x) ↦ (g • x,x), Lee’s definition of a proper action.
