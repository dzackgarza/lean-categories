Chapter II. Semisimple Lie Algebras

FC14 — Humphreys, Introduction to Lie Algebras and Representation Theory.

This chapter contains 17 definitions, 9 theorems, 6 propositions, 10 lemmas, 9 corollaries, 2 examples, 7 constructions, 2 conventions, 1 formula and 1 criterion.

Nobody has checked this chapter against Lean yet, so the statements below say nothing about whether they are formalized.

Convention: characteristic-zero algebraically closed base field

FC14-C02-U001 · Chapter II opening; p. 15

Except where explicitly stated otherwise, F is algebraically closed of characteristic zero throughout the remaining semisimple theory.

Theorem: common eigenvector theorem for solvable linear Lie algebras

FC14-C02-U002 · §4.1; pp. 15–16 · after FC14-C01-U041

If L⊆gl(V) is solvable, V finite-dimensional and nonzero, then V contains a common eigenvector for all elements of L.

Corollary: Lie’s Theorem

FC14-C02-U003 · §4.1; p. 16 · after FC14-C01-U050, FC14-C02-U002

A solvable linear Lie algebra L⊆gl(V) stabilizes a complete flag; equivalently it is simultaneously upper triangularizable.

Corollary: ideal flag in a solvable Lie algebra

FC14-C02-U004 · §4.1; p. 16 · after FC14-C01-U041, FC14-C02-U003

Every solvable Lie algebra L has a chain of ideals 0=L_0⊂L_1⊂⋯⊂L_n=L with dim L_i=i.

Corollary: nilpotence of the derived algebra of a solvable Lie algebra

FC14-C02-U005 · §4.1; pp. 16–17 · after FC14-C01-U023, FC14-C01-U047, FC14-C02-U004

If L is solvable and x∈[L,L], then ad_L x is nilpotent; in particular [L,L] is nilpotent.

Definition: semisimple endomorphism

FC14-C02-U006 · §4.2; p. 17

For finite-dimensional V, x∈End(V) is semisimple if its minimal polynomial has distinct roots; over algebraically closed F this is equivalent to diagonalizability.

Proposition: Jordan–Chevalley decomposition

FC14-C02-U007 · §4.2; pp. 17–18 · after FC14-C02-U006

Every x∈End(V) has unique commuting semisimple and nilpotent parts x_s,x_n with x=x_s+x_n. They are polynomials in x without constant term, commute with every endomorphism commuting with x, and preserve the source/target condition x(B)⊆A between invariant subspaces.

Definition: additive Jordan–Chevalley decomposition

FC14-C02-U008 · §4.2; p. 17 · after FC14-C02-U007

The decomposition x=x_s+x_n of the proposition is the additive Jordan–Chevalley (Jordan) decomposition; x_s and x_n are the semisimple and nilpotent parts.

Lemma: Jordan decomposition under the adjoint representation

FC14-C02-U009 · §4.2; p. 18 · after FC14-C01-U016, FC14-C02-U007

If x=x_s+x_n in End(V), then ad x=ad x_s+ad x_n is the Jordan decomposition of ad x on End(V).

Lemma: derivation algebras contain Jordan parts

FC14-C02-U010 · §4.2; pp. 18–19 · after FC14-C01-U014, FC14-C02-U007

For a finite-dimensional F-algebra A, Der(A) contains the semisimple and nilpotent parts in End(A) of each derivation.

Lemma: trace criterion for nilpotence

FC14-C02-U011 · §4.3; pp. 19–20 · after FC14-C02-U007

Let A⊆B⊆gl(V) and M={x∈gl(V) | [x,B]⊆A}. If x∈M satisfies Tr(xy)=0 for every y∈M, then x is nilpotent.

Formula: trace commutator identity

FC14-C02-U012 · §4.3; p. 19 · after FC14-C01-U005

For endomorphisms x,y,z of a finite-dimensional vector space, Tr([x,y]z)=Tr(x[y,z]).

Theorem: Cartan’s solvability criterion

FC14-C02-U013 · §4.3; p. 20 · after FC14-C01-U023, FC14-C01-U047, FC14-C02-U011, FC14-C02-U012

If L⊆gl(V) and Tr(xy)=0 for all x∈[L,L], y∈L, then L is solvable.

Corollary: Cartan criterion via adjoint traces

FC14-C02-U014 · §4.3; p. 20 · after FC14-C01-U016, FC14-C02-U013

If Tr(ad x ad y)=0 for all x∈[L,L], y∈L, then L is solvable.

Definition: Killing form

FC14-C02-U015 · §5.1; p. 21 · after FC14-C01-U016, FC14-C02-U012

The Killing form of L is κ(x,y)=Tr(ad x ad y). It is symmetric bilinear and associative: κ([x,y],z)=κ(x,[y,z]).

Lemma: Killing form on an ideal

FC14-C02-U016 · §5.1; pp. 21–22 · after FC14-C01-U021, FC14-C02-U015

If I is an ideal of L, the Killing form of I equals the restriction of the Killing form of L to I×I.

Definition: nondegenerate symmetric form and radical

FC14-C02-U017 · §5.1; p. 22 · after FC14-C01-U021

A symmetric bilinear form B is nondegenerate when its radical {x | B(x,y)=0 for all y} is zero. For the Killing form the radical is an ideal.

Example: Killing form of sl(2,F)

FC14-C02-U018 · §5.1; p. 22 · after FC14-C01-U026, FC14-C02-U015

For char F≠2, the Killing form of sl(2,F) has the displayed nonsingular matrix in the standard basis, hence is nondegenerate.

Criterion: semisimplicity via absence of abelian ideals

FC14-C02-U019 · §5.1; p. 22 · after FC14-C01-U018, FC14-C01-U042

L is semisimple iff it has no nonzero abelian ideals.

Theorem: Cartan criterion for semisimplicity

FC14-C02-U020 · §5.1; p. 22 · after FC14-C01-U042, FC14-C02-U013, FC14-C02-U015, FC14-C02-U017

A Lie algebra L is semisimple iff its Killing form is nondegenerate.

Definition: direct sum of ideals

FC14-C02-U021 · §5.2; p. 22 · after FC14-C01-U021

L is the direct sum of ideals I_1,…,I_t when L=I_1⊕⋯⊕I_t as vector spaces; then [I_i,I_j]=0 for i≠j and brackets are componentwise.

Theorem: decomposition of a semisimple Lie algebra into simple ideals

FC14-C02-U022 · §5.2; pp. 22–23 · after FC14-C01-U025, FC14-C02-U016, FC14-C02-U020, FC14-C02-U021

Every semisimple L decomposes as a direct sum of simple ideals L_1⊕⋯⊕L_t. These are exactly the simple ideals of L, and each Killing form is the restriction of that of L.

Corollary: ideals and quotients of a semisimple Lie algebra

FC14-C02-U023 · §5.2; p. 23 · after FC14-C01-U023, FC14-C01-U030, FC14-C02-U022

For semisimple L, L=[L,L]; every ideal and homomorphic image is semisimple, and every ideal is a sum of some simple summands.

Theorem: all derivations of a semisimple Lie algebra are inner

FC14-C02-U024 · §5.3; pp. 23–24 · after FC14-C01-U015, FC14-C02-U020, FC14-C02-U022

If L is semisimple, Der(L)=ad(L).

Definition: abstract Jordan decomposition in a semisimple Lie algebra

FC14-C02-U025 · §5.4; p. 24 · after FC14-C02-U009, FC14-C02-U010, FC14-C02-U024

For x∈L semisimple, unique s,n∈L satisfy x=s+n, [s,n]=0 and ad x=ad s+ad n is the ordinary Jordan decomposition. These are the ad-semisimple and ad-nilpotent parts of x.

Convention: finite-dimensional representations in §6

FC14-C02-U026 · §6 opening; p. 25

All representations in §6 are finite-dimensional unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Definition: L-module

FC14-C02-U027 · §6.1; p. 25 · after FC14-C01-U032

An L-module is a vector space V with a bilinear action satisfying [x,y]v=x(yv)−y(xv); equivalently it is a representation L→gl(V).

Definition: homomorphism and isomorphism of L-modules

FC14-C02-U028 · §6.1; p. 25 · after FC14-C02-U027

An L-module homomorphism is a linear map commuting with the L-action; an isomorphism is a vector-space isomorphism of this kind, and isomorphic modules afford equivalent representations.

Definition: irreducible L-module

FC14-C02-U029 · §6.1; p. 25 · after FC14-C02-U027

A nonzero L-module is irreducible if its only L-submodules are 0 and itself.

Definition: completely reducible L-module

FC14-C02-U030 · §6.1; pp. 25–26 · after FC14-C02-U029

V is completely reducible if it is a direct sum of irreducible L-submodules; equivalently every submodule has an L-stable complement.

Construction: direct sum of L-modules

FC14-C02-U031 · §6.1; p. 26 · after FC14-C02-U027

For L-modules W,W′, the vector-space direct sum becomes an L-module componentwise.

Lemma: Schur’s Lemma

FC14-C02-U032 · §6.1; p. 26 · after FC14-C02-U029

For an irreducible representation φ:L→gl(V), every endomorphism of V commuting with φ(L) is scalar.

Construction: dual/contragredient module

FC14-C02-U033 · §6.1; p. 26 · after FC14-C02-U027

For an L-module V, the dual V* is an L-module by (x·f)(v)=−f(x·v).

Construction: tensor product of L-modules

FC14-C02-U034 · §6.1; p. 26 · after FC14-C02-U027

For L-modules V,W, V⊗W is an L-module via x·(v⊗w)=xv⊗w+v⊗xw.

Construction: Hom and End as L-modules

FC14-C02-U035 · §6.1; pp. 26–27 · after FC14-C02-U033, FC14-C02-U034

Hom(V,W)≅V*⊗W carries the action (x·f)(v)=x f(v)−f(xv); in particular End(V) is an L-module.

Definition/proposition: faithful representation and its trace form

FC14-C02-U036 · §6.2; p. 27 · after FC14-C02-U013, FC14-C02-U020

A representation is faithful when it is injective. If L is semisimple and φ:L→gl(V) faithful, B(x,y)=Tr(φ(x)φ(y)) is a nondegenerate symmetric associative bilinear form on L.

Definition: Casimir element

FC14-C02-U037 · §6.2; pp. 27–28 · after FC14-C02-U032, FC14-C02-U036

For semisimple L, a representation φ, and dual bases x_i,y_i relative to a nondegenerate associative trace form on the faithful part, c_φ=Σφ(x_i)φ(y_i) commutes with φ(L). For faithful irreducible φ it is scalar with trace dim L and value dim L/dim V.

Example: Casimir element for the natural sl(2,F)-module

FC14-C02-U038 · §6.2; p. 28 · after FC14-C01-U026, FC14-C02-U037

For the natural two-dimensional representation of sl(2,F), the Casimir element is xy+(1/2)h²+yx=(3/2)I.

Construction: Casimir element for a nonfaithful representation

FC14-C02-U039 · §6.2; p. 28 · after FC14-C02-U022, FC14-C02-U037

For nonfaithful φ, decompose semisimple L into simple ideals; restrict to the sum complementary to Kerφ where φ is faithful and perform the Casimir construction there.

Lemma: semisimple representations have trace-zero image

FC14-C02-U040 · §6.3; p. 28 · after FC14-C01-U007, FC14-C02-U023

For a representation φ of semisimple L, φ(L)⊆sl(V). Hence L acts trivially on every one-dimensional module.

Theorem: Weyl’s complete reducibility theorem

FC14-C02-U041 · §6.3; pp. 28–29 · after FC14-C02-U030, FC14-C02-U032, FC14-C02-U037, FC14-C02-U040

Every finite-dimensional representation of a semisimple Lie algebra is completely reducible.

Theorem: semisimple linear Lie algebras contain Jordan parts

FC14-C02-U042 · §6.4; pp. 29–30 · after FC14-C02-U007, FC14-C02-U025, FC14-C02-U041

If L⊆gl(V) is semisimple, it contains the ordinary semisimple and nilpotent parts of every x∈L; hence abstract and ordinary Jordan decompositions coincide.

Corollary: representations preserve Jordan decomposition

FC14-C02-U043 · §6.4; p. 30 · after FC14-C02-U025, FC14-C02-U042

For any finite-dimensional representation φ of semisimple L, if x=s+n is the abstract Jordan decomposition, then φ(x)=φ(s)+φ(n) is the ordinary Jordan decomposition.

Definition: weights and weight spaces for sl(2,F)

FC14-C02-U044 · §7.1; p. 31 · after FC14-C02-U027

For an sl(2,F)-module V, V_λ={v | hv=λv}; λ is a weight when V_λ≠0, and V_λ is its weight space.

Lemma: raising and lowering weights

FC14-C02-U045 · §7.1; pp. 31–32 · after FC14-C02-U044

If v∈V_λ, then xv∈V_{λ+2} and yv∈V_{λ−2}.

Definition: maximal vector

FC14-C02-U046 · §7.1; p. 32 · after FC14-C02-U044, FC14-C02-U045

A nonzero vector in a weight space V_λ with V_{λ+2}=0 is a maximal vector of weight λ.

Lemma: sl(2) highest-weight basis formulas

FC14-C02-U047 · §7.2; p. 32 · after FC14-C01-U026, FC14-C02-U046

For maximal v_0 of weight λ and v_i=(1/i!)y^i v_0: h v_i=(λ−2i)v_i, yv_i=(i+1)v_{i+1}, and xv_i=(λ−i+1)v_{i−1}.

Theorem: classification of finite-dimensional irreducible sl(2,F)-modules

FC14-C02-U048 · §7.2; p. 33 · after FC14-C02-U047

An irreducible sl(2,F)-module of dimension m+1 has weights m,m−2,…,−m, each multiplicity one, a unique maximal vector up to scalar of highest weight m, and the explicit action of the preceding lemma; at most one irreducible module exists in each dimension.

Corollary: weights in arbitrary finite-dimensional sl(2,F)-modules

FC14-C02-U049 · §7.2; p. 33 · after FC14-C02-U041, FC14-C02-U048

All h-eigenvalues are integers and occur with their negatives with equal multiplicity; in any irreducible decomposition, the number of summands is dim V_0+dim V_1.

Construction: irreducible module V(m)

FC14-C02-U050 · §7.2; pp. 33–34 · after FC14-C02-U047, FC14-C02-U048

For every m≥0, the highest-weight formulas define an irreducible (m+1)-dimensional sl(2,F)-module V(m).

Proposition: Weyl-element symmetry on V(m)

FC14-C02-U051 · §7.2; pp. 33–34 · after FC14-C01-U037, FC14-C02-U050

For τ=expφ(x)expφ(−y)expφ(x), τφ(h)τ^{-1}=−φ(h); hence τ exchanges the weight spaces of weights λ and −λ.

Definition: toral and maximal toral subalgebra

FC14-C02-U052 · §8.1; p. 35 · after FC14-C02-U025

A toral subalgebra consists entirely of semisimple elements; it is maximal toral if not properly contained in another toral subalgebra.
