In §17 the field F is arbitrary; Lie algebras may be infinite-dimensional where explicitly allowed.

### Definition/construction: tensor algebra {#fc14-c05-u002}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U002` · §17.1; pp. 89–90
:::

For a vector space V, T(V)=⊕_{m≥0}T^mV with T^0V=F, T^1V=V and T^mV=V^{⊗m}; concatenation of tensors makes T(V) an associative graded F-algebra with 1, universal for linear maps V→A into associative unital F-algebras.

### Definition/construction: symmetric algebra {#fc14-c05-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U003` · §17.1; pp. 89–90 · after [`FC14-C05-U002`](#fc14-c05-u002)
:::

S(V)=T(V)/I where I is generated by x⊗y−y⊗x. It is the universal commutative associative unital algebra receiving V, is graded, and for a basis of V identifies canonically with a polynomial algebra.

### Definition/result: homogeneous symmetric tensors {#fc14-c05-u004}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U004` · §17.1; p. 90 · after [`FC14-C05-U002`](#fc14-c05-u002), [`FC14-C05-U003`](#fc14-c05-u003)
:::

In characteristic zero, the symmetric group acts on T^mV by permuting tensor factors. Fixed tensors are homogeneous symmetric tensors of order m; averaging over permutations gives a subspace canonically isomorphic to S^mV and hence a graded vector-space copy of S(V) inside T(V).

### Definition: universal enveloping algebra {#fc14-c05-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U005` · §17.2; pp. 90–91
:::

A universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra L is an associative unital algebra U with a linear map i:L→U satisfying i([x,y])=i(x)i(y)−i(y)i(x), universal among such pairs. It is unique up to unique isomorphism.

### Construction: U(L) as a quotient of T(L) {#fc14-c05-u006}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U006` · §17.2; p. 91 · after [`FC14-C05-U002`](#fc14-c05-u002), [`FC14-C05-U005`](#fc14-c05-u005)
:::

Let J be the two-sided ideal of T(L) generated by x⊗y−y⊗x−[x,y]. Then U(L)=T(L)/J with the induced map i:L→U(L) satisfies the universal property.

### Example: enveloping algebra of an abelian Lie algebra {#fc14-c05-u007}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U007` · §17.2; p. 91 · after [`FC14-C01-U018`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u018), [`FC14-C05-U003`](#fc14-c05-u003), [`FC14-C05-U006`](#fc14-c05-u006)
:::

If L is abelian, U(L) is canonically the symmetric algebra S(L).

### Construction: PBW filtration and associated graded algebra {#fc14-c05-u008}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U008` · §17.3; p. 91 · after [`FC14-C05-U003`](#fc14-c05-u003), [`FC14-C05-U006`](#fc14-c05-u006)
:::

Filter T(L) by tensor degree T_m and U(L) by U_m=π(T_m). The quotients G^m=U_m/U_{m−1} form the associated graded algebra G; the canonical T(L)→G kills the commutativity ideal and induces a surjective graded algebra map w:S(L)→G.

### Theorem: Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem {#fc14-c05-u009}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U009` · §17.3; p. 92 · after [`FC14-C05-U008`](#fc14-c05-u008)
:::

The canonical graded algebra homomorphism w:S(L)→gr U(L) is an isomorphism.

### Corollary: degree-m complements in the PBW filtration {#fc14-c05-u010}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U010` · §17.3; p. 92 · after [`FC14-C05-U009`](#fc14-c05-u009)
:::

If W⊆T^mL maps isomorphically to S^mL, then its image in U_m is a vector-space complement of U_{m−1}.

### Corollary: canonical embedding of L into U(L) {#fc14-c05-u011}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U011` · §17.3; p. 92 · after [`FC14-C05-U010`](#fc14-c05-u010)
:::

The canonical map i:L→U(L) is injective, so L may be identified with its image in U(L).

### Corollary: PBW basis {#fc14-c05-u012}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U012` · §17.3; p. 92 · after [`FC14-C05-U009`](#fc14-c05-u009), [`FC14-C05-U011`](#fc14-c05-u011)
:::

For any ordered basis (x_i) of L, 1 together with ordered monomials x_{i_1}⋯x_{i_m}, i_1≤⋯≤i_m, form a basis of U(L). Such a basis is called a PBW basis.

### Corollary: enveloping algebra over a subalgebra {#fc14-c05-u013}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U013` · §17.3; p. 92 · after [`FC14-C01-U004`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u004), [`FC14-C05-U012`](#fc14-c05-u012)
:::

If H≤L and an ordered basis of H is extended to one of L, the induced map U(H)→U(L) is injective and U(L) is a free U(H)-module on the ordered monomials in the complementary basis elements.

### Corollary: symmetric tensors as PBW filtration complements {#fc14-c05-u014}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U014` · §17.3; p. 92 · after [`FC14-C05-U004`](#fc14-c05-u004), [`FC14-C05-U010`](#fc14-c05-u010)
:::

In characteristic zero, the canonical map from homogeneous symmetric tensors S^mL⊂T^mL to U_m is an isomorphism onto a complement of U_{m−1}.

### Lemma: PBW proof: representation on S(L) {#fc14-c05-u016}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U016` · §17.4; p. 94 · after [`FC14-C01-U032`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u032), [`FC14-C05-U015`](#fc14-c05-u015)

### Lemma: PBW proof: highest homogeneous term of a relation {#fc14-c05-u017}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U017` · §17.4; p. 94 · after [`FC14-C05-U003`](#fc14-c05-u003), [`FC14-C05-U006`](#fc14-c05-u006), [`FC14-C05-U016`](#fc14-c05-u016)
:::

If t lies in the kernel J of T(L)→U(L), then its highest homogeneous component lies in the commutativity ideal I=Ker(T(L)→S(L)).

### Theorem: PBW kernel comparison {#fc14-c05-u018}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U018` · §17.4; pp. 94–95 · after [`FC14-C05-U017`](#fc14-c05-u017)

The preceding representation proves PBW by showing that a homogeneous t∈T^mL whose image lies in U_{m−1} must lie in I; equivalently S(L)≅gr U(L).

### Definition/construction: free Lie algebra {#fc14-c05-u019}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U019` · §17.5; p. 95 · after [`FC14-C05-U002`](#fc14-c05-u002), [`FC14-C05-U005`](#fc14-c05-u005)
:::

A Lie algebra L generated by X is free on X if every map X→M into a Lie algebra extends uniquely to a Lie homomorphism L→M. It exists as the Lie subalgebra generated by X inside the tensor algebra on the vector space with basis X.

### Construction: modules over a free Lie algebra {#fc14-c05-u020}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U020` · §17.5; p. 95 · after [`FC14-C01-U032`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u032), [`FC14-C05-U019`](#fc14-c05-u019)
:::

To make V an L-module when L is free on X, it suffices to assign arbitrary endomorphisms of V to the generators X; the assignment extends uniquely to a representation.

### Definition: Lie algebra by generators and relations {#fc14-c05-u021}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U021` · §17.5; p. 95 · after [`FC14-C01-U021`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u021), [`FC14-C05-U019`](#fc14-c05-u019)
:::

If L is free on X and R is the ideal generated by relations f_j, then L/R is called the Lie algebra with generators given by the images of X and relations f_j=0.

### Proposition: Chevalley–Serre relations satisfied by standard generators {#fc14-c05-u023}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U023` · §18.1; p. 96 · after [`FC14-C03-U036`](c03.html#fc14-c03-u036), [`FC14-C04-U004`](c04.html#fc14-c04-u004)
:::

For standard generators x_i,y_i,h_i associated with a base of Φ, L is generated by them and satisfies [h_i,h_j]=0; [x_i,y_j]=δ_{ij}h_i; [h_i,x_j]=c_{ji}x_j, [h_i,y_j]=−c_{ji}y_j; and the Serre nilpotence relations (ad x_i)^{1−c_{ji}}x_j=0 and (ad y_i)^{1−c_{ji}}y_j=0 for i≠j.

### Construction: Lie algebra L0 defined by relations S1–S3 {#fc14-c05-u024}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U024` · §18.2; pp. 96–97 · after [`FC14-C03-U036`](c03.html#fc14-c03-u036), [`FC14-C05-U019`](#fc14-c05-u019), [`FC14-C05-U023`](#fc14-c05-u023)
:::

For a root system with Cartan matrix (c_{ij}), start from the free Lie algebra on generators X_i,Y_i,H_i and quotient only by the relations [h_i,h_j]=0, [x_i,y_j]=δ_{ij}h_i, [h_i,x_j]=c_{ji}x_j, [h_i,y_j]=−c_{ji}y_j. The resulting algebra is denoted L_0.

### Proposition: the model representation factors through L0 {#fc14-c05-u026}

::: {.unit-meta}

### Theorem: triangular decomposition of L0 {#fc14-c05-u027}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U027` · §18.2; pp. 97–98 · after [`FC14-C05-U026`](#fc14-c05-u026)

In L_0 the h_i form a basis of an l-dimensional abelian subalgebra H, and L_0=Y⊕H⊕X, where X and Y are the subalgebras generated by the x_i and y_i. Nonzero H-weights are integral combinations of simple roots with coefficients all positive or all negative.

### Lemma: Serre generators are killed by opposite simple raising operators {#fc14-c05-u028}

### Definition/construction: locally nilpotent endomorphism and exponential {#fc14-c05-u029}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U029` · §18.3; p. 99 · after [`FC14-C01-U035`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u035)
:::

An endomorphism is locally nilpotent if every vector is killed by some power. Its exponential is defined by patching the finite exponential series on finite-dimensional invariant subspaces; this yields an automorphism.

### Theorem: Serre’s Theorem {#fc14-c05-u030}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U030` · §18.3; pp. 99–101 · after [`FC14-C03-U004`](c03.html#fc14-c03-u004), [`FC14-C04-U015`](c04.html#fc14-c04-u015), [`FC14-C05-U023`](#fc14-c05-u023), [`FC14-C05-U027`](#fc14-c05-u027), [`FC14-C05-U028`](#fc14-c05-u028), [`FC14-C05-U029`](#fc14-c05-u029)
:::

For a root system Φ with base Δ, the Lie algebra generated by x_i,y_i,h_i subject to S1–S3 and the Serre relations is finite-dimensional semisimple; H=span(h_i) is a Cartan subalgebra and its root system is Φ.

### Corollary: dimension from the root system in Serre’s construction {#fc14-c05-u031}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U031` · §18.3; p. 100 · after [`FC14-C05-U030`](#fc14-c05-u030)

The Lie algebra in Serre’s theorem has root decomposition H⊕⊕_{α∈Φ}L_α with dim H=rank Φ and dim L_α=1; hence dim L=rank Φ+|Φ|.

### Definition: reductive Lie algebra {#fc14-c05-u035}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U035` · §19.1; p. 102 · after [`FC14-C01-U022`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u022), [`FC14-C01-U042`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u042)
:::

A nonzero Lie algebra L is reductive if Rad L=Z(L). Abelian and semisimple algebras are the two extreme cases.

### Proposition: structure and irreducible-action criterion for reductive Lie algebras {#fc14-c05-u036}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U036` · §19.1; p. 102 · after [`FC14-C02-U003`](c02.html#fc14-c02-u003), [`FC14-C02-U041`](c02.html#fc14-c02-u041), [`FC14-C05-U035`](#fc14-c05-u035)
:::

If L is reductive, L=[L,L]⊕Z(L), with [L,L] semisimple or zero. If nonzero L⊆gl(V) acts irreducibly, then L is reductive with dim Z(L)≤1; if moreover L⊆sl(V), then L is semisimple.

### Proposition: classical Lie algebras are the simple types A–D {#fc14-c05-u037}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C05-U037` · §19.2; pp. 102–103 · after [`FC14-C01-U007`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u007), [`FC14-C01-U008`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u008), [`FC14-C01-U009`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u009), [`FC14-C01-U010`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u010), [`FC14-C05-U036`](#fc14-c05-u036)
:::

The classical algebras sl(l+1,F), o(2l+1,F), sp(2l,F), and o(2l,F) act irreducibly in their natural modules, are semisimple by the preceding criterion, and their diagonal Cartan subalgebras/root spaces identify them as the simple types A_l,B_l,C_l,D_l in the stated ranges.
