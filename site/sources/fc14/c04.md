### Proposition: simple Lie algebras have irreducible root systems {#fc14-c04-u001}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U001` · §14.1; pp. 73–74 · after [`FC14-C01-U025`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u025), [`FC14-C02-U064`](c02.html#fc14-c02-u064), [`FC14-C03-U030`](c03.html#fc14-c03-u030)
:::

If L is simple and H is maximal toral, the corresponding root system Φ is irreducible.

### Corollary: simple-ideal decomposition matches irreducible root components {#fc14-c04-u002}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U002` · §14.1; p. 74 · after [`FC14-C02-U022`](c02.html#fc14-c02-u022), [`FC14-C03-U041`](c03.html#fc14-c03-u041), [`FC14-C04-U001`](#fc14-c04-u001)
:::

If semisimple L=L_1⊕⋯⊕L_t and H is maximal toral, then H_i=H∩L_i is maximal toral in L_i; the root systems Φ_i of (L_i,H_i) are the irreducible components of Φ and Φ=Φ_1∪⋯∪Φ_t.

### Proposition: generation by simple root spaces {#fc14-c04-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U003` · §14.2; p. 74 · after [`FC14-C02-U061`](c02.html#fc14-c02-u061), [`FC14-C03-U014`](c03.html#fc14-c03-u014)
:::

For semisimple L with maximal toral H and base Δ of Φ, L is generated by L_α and L_{−α} for α∈Δ; equivalently by arbitrary nonzero root vectors x_α∈L_α and y_α∈L_{−α}.

### Definition: standard set of generators {#fc14-c04-u004}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U004` · §14.2; p. 74 · after [`FC14-C02-U060`](c02.html#fc14-c02-u060), [`FC14-C04-U003`](#fc14-c04-u003)
:::

After choosing nonzero x_α∈L_α and the unique y_α∈L_{−α} with [x_α,y_α]=h_α for α∈Δ, the sets {x_α,y_α} and {x_α,y_α,h_α} are called standard sets of generators.

### Theorem: Theorem 14.2 (isomorphism theorem) {#fc14-c04-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U005` · §14.2; pp. 74–76 · after [`FC14-C03-U007`](c03.html#fc14-c03-u007), [`FC14-C04-U003`](#fc14-c04-u003), [`FC14-C04-U004`](#fc14-c04-u004)
:::

Let simple L,L′ have maximal toral subalgebras H,H′ and isomorphic root systems Φ≅Φ′. The induced H≅H′ together with arbitrary chosen isomorphisms L_α→L_{α′} on simple-root spaces extends uniquely to a Lie-algebra isomorphism L→L′.

### Corollary: semisimple Lie algebra determined by its root system {#fc14-c04-u006}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U006` · §14.2; p. 76 · after [`FC14-C02-U022`](c02.html#fc14-c02-u022), [`FC14-C04-U002`](#fc14-c04-u002), [`FC14-C04-U005`](#fc14-c04-u005)
:::

The isomorphism theorem extends componentwise: a semisimple Lie algebra over F is determined up to isomorphism by its root system.

### Proposition: opposition involution {#fc14-c04-u007}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U007` · §14.3; p. 77 · after [`FC14-C04-U004`](#fc14-c04-u004), [`FC14-C04-U005`](#fc14-c04-u005)
:::

For semisimple L with fixed standard generators, there is an automorphism σ of order two satisfying σ(x_α)=−y_α, σ(y_α)=−x_α for simple α and σ(h)=−h for h∈H.

### Construction: inner automorphism realizing a root reflection {#fc14-c04-u008}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U008` · §14.3; p. 77 · after [`FC14-C01-U036`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u036), [`FC14-C02-U060`](c02.html#fc14-c02-u060), [`FC14-C03-U005`](c03.html#fc14-c03-u005)
:::

For α∈Φ, τ_α=exp(ad x_α) exp(ad(−y_α)) exp(ad x_α) is inner, restricts to the reflection σ_α on H, and sends each root space L_β onto L_{σ_αβ}. Hence Weyl-group elements are represented by inner automorphisms, though not canonically as a subgroup.

### Convention: field assumptions for Cartan-subalgebra theory {#fc14-c04-u009}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U009` · §15 opening; p. 78
:::

For §15 the field is algebraically closed; its characteristic is arbitrary except where explicitly restricted.

### Construction: generalized eigenspace decomposition for ad x {#fc14-c04-u010}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U010` · §15.1; p. 78 · after [`FC14-C02-U007`](c02.html#fc14-c02-u007)
:::

For x∈L, write L=⊕_{a∈F}L_a(ad x), where L_a(ad x) is the generalized a-eigenspace; write L=L_0(ad x)⊕L_*(ad x), with L_* the sum over a≠0. The same notation applies to ad x-stable subalgebras.

### Lemma: brackets of generalized ad-eigenspaces {#fc14-c04-u011}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U011` · §15.1; pp. 78–79 · after [`FC14-C01-U046`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u046), [`FC14-C04-U010`](#fc14-c04-u010)
:::

[L_a(ad x),L_b(ad x)]⊆L_{a+b}(ad x). Hence L_0(ad x) is a subalgebra; in characteristic zero, every element of L_a(ad x) for a≠0 is ad-nilpotent.

### Definition: Engel subalgebra {#fc14-c04-u012}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U012` · §15.2; p. 79 · after [`FC14-C04-U011`](#fc14-c04-u011)
:::

For x∈L, the subalgebra L_0(ad x) is called an Engel subalgebra.

### Lemma: minimal Engel-subalgebra containment {#fc14-c04-u013}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U013` · §15.2; p. 79 · after [`FC14-C04-U010`](#fc14-c04-u010), [`FC14-C04-U012`](#fc14-c04-u012)
:::

Let K≤L and choose z∈K with L_0(ad z) minimal among L_0(ad x), x∈K. If K⊆L_0(ad z), then L_0(ad z)⊆L_0(ad x) for every x∈K.

### Lemma: Engel-containing subalgebras are self-normalizing {#fc14-c04-u014}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U014` · §15.2; pp. 79–80 · after [`FC14-C01-U028`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u028), [`FC14-C04-U012`](#fc14-c04-u012)
:::

If a subalgebra K contains an Engel subalgebra of L, then N_L(K)=K. In particular every Engel subalgebra is self-normalizing.

### Definition: Cartan subalgebra (CSA) {#fc14-c04-u015}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U015` · §15.3; p. 80 · after [`FC14-C01-U043`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u043), [`FC14-C01-U028`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u028)
:::

A Cartan subalgebra of L is a nilpotent self-normalizing subalgebra.

### Theorem: Cartan subalgebras are precisely minimal Engel subalgebras {#fc14-c04-u016}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U016` · §15.3; p. 80 · after [`FC14-C01-U047`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u047), [`FC14-C01-U049`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u049), [`FC14-C04-U013`](#fc14-c04-u013), [`FC14-C04-U014`](#fc14-c04-u014), [`FC14-C04-U015`](#fc14-c04-u015)
:::

A subalgebra H is a Cartan subalgebra iff it is a minimal Engel subalgebra. In particular Cartan subalgebras exist under the standing field assumptions.

### Corollary: Cartan subalgebras in semisimple characteristic-zero Lie algebras {#fc14-c04-u017}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U017` · §15.3; pp. 80–81 · after [`FC14-C02-U052`](c02.html#fc14-c02-u052), [`FC14-C02-U058`](c02.html#fc14-c02-u058), [`FC14-C04-U016`](#fc14-c04-u016)
:::

For semisimple L in characteristic zero, Cartan subalgebras are exactly maximal toral subalgebras.

### Definition/result: regular semisimple element {#fc14-c04-u018}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U018` · §15.3; p. 81 · after [`FC14-C01-U029`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u029), [`FC14-C02-U052`](c02.html#fc14-c02-u052), [`FC14-C04-U017`](#fc14-c04-u017)
:::

Every maximal toral subalgebra H of a semisimple characteristic-zero L has the form C_L(s) for some semisimple s∈L; such an s is called regular semisimple.

### Lemma: epimorphic image of a Cartan subalgebra {#fc14-c04-u019}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U019` · §15.4; p. 81 · after [`FC14-C01-U030`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u030), [`FC14-C04-U016`](#fc14-c04-u016)
:::

If φ:L→L′ is surjective and H is a CSA of L, then φ(H) is a CSA of L′.

### Lemma: lifting a Cartan subalgebra through an epimorphism {#fc14-c04-u020}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U020` · §15.4; p. 81 · after [`FC14-C04-U019`](#fc14-c04-u019)
:::

If φ:L→L′ is surjective, H′ is a CSA of L′, K=φ^{-1}(H′), and H is a CSA of K, then H is a CSA of L.

### Definition: strongly ad-nilpotent element and the group E(L) {#fc14-c04-u022}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U022` · §16.1; p. 82 · after [`FC14-C01-U036`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u036), [`FC14-C04-U010`](#fc14-c04-u010), [`FC14-C04-U011`](#fc14-c04-u011)
:::

x∈L is strongly ad-nilpotent if x lies in L_a(ad y) for some y∈L and nonzero eigenvalue a. Let A(L) be this set and E(L) the subgroup of Int(L) generated by exp(ad x), x∈A(L). E(L) is normal in Aut(L).

### Construction: relative group E(L;K) {#fc14-c04-u023}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U023` · §16.1; p. 82 · after [`FC14-C04-U022`](#fc14-c04-u022)
:::

For K≤L, E(L;K) is generated by exp(ad_L x) for x strongly ad-nilpotent in K; restriction to K gives E(K). Strongly ad-nilpotent elements are functorial under epimorphisms.

### Lemma: lifting E-automorphisms through epimorphisms {#fc14-c04-u024}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U024` · §16.1; p. 82 · after [`FC14-C01-U030`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u030), [`FC14-C04-U022`](#fc14-c04-u022), [`FC14-C04-U023`](#fc14-c04-u023)
:::

For a surjection φ:L→L′, every σ′∈E(L′) lifts to some σ∈E(L) with φσ=σ′φ.

### Theorem: conjugacy of Cartan subalgebras in solvable Lie algebras {#fc14-c04-u025}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U025` · §16.2; pp. 82–83 · after [`FC14-C01-U039`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u039), [`FC14-C04-U016`](#fc14-c04-u016), [`FC14-C04-U024`](#fc14-c04-u024)
:::

If L is solvable, any two Cartan subalgebras of L are conjugate under E(L).

### Definition: Borel subalgebra {#fc14-c04-u026}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U026` · §16.3; p. 83 · after [`FC14-C01-U039`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u039)
:::

A Borel subalgebra of L is a maximal solvable subalgebra.

### Lemma: Borel subalgebras are self-normalizing {#fc14-c04-u027}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U027` · §16.3; p. 83 · after [`FC14-C01-U028`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u028), [`FC14-C04-U026`](#fc14-c04-u026)
:::

If B is a Borel subalgebra of L, then N_L(B)=B.

### Lemma: Borels and the semisimple quotient {#fc14-c04-u028}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U028` · §16.3; pp. 83–84 · after [`FC14-C01-U042`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u042), [`FC14-C04-U026`](#fc14-c04-u026)
:::

If Rad L≠L, Borel subalgebras of L correspond bijectively to Borel subalgebras of the semisimple quotient L/Rad L; every Borel contains Rad L.

### Construction: standard Borel and nilradical from a base {#fc14-c04-u029}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U029` · §16.3; p. 84 · after [`FC14-C01-U043`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u043), [`FC14-C02-U054`](c02.html#fc14-c02-u054), [`FC14-C03-U014`](c03.html#fc14-c03-u014), [`FC14-C04-U017`](#fc14-c04-u017)
:::

For semisimple L, CSA H, root system Φ, and base Δ, set B(Δ)=H⊕⊕_{α>0}L_α and N(Δ)=⊕_{α>0}L_α. N(Δ) is nilpotent, [B(Δ),B(Δ)]=N(Δ), and B(Δ) is solvable.

### Lemma: standard Borels and their conjugacy {#fc14-c04-u030}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U030` · §16.3; p. 84 · after [`FC14-C03-U026`](c03.html#fc14-c03-u026), [`FC14-C04-U008`](#fc14-c04-u008), [`FC14-C04-U029`](#fc14-c04-u029)
:::

B(Δ) is a Borel subalgebra, called standard relative to H. All standard Borel subalgebras relative to H are conjugate under E(L).

### Theorem: conjugacy of Borel subalgebras {#fc14-c04-u031}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U031` · §16.4; pp. 84–87 · after [`FC14-C04-U025`](#fc14-c04-u025), [`FC14-C04-U027`](#fc14-c04-u027), [`FC14-C04-U028`](#fc14-c04-u028), [`FC14-C04-U030`](#fc14-c04-u030)

All Borel subalgebras of an arbitrary Lie algebra L over the standing field are conjugate under E(L).

### Corollary: conjugacy of Cartan subalgebras {#fc14-c04-u032}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U032` · §16.4; pp. 84–85 · after [`FC14-C04-U025`](#fc14-c04-u025), [`FC14-C04-U031`](#fc14-c04-u031)

All Cartan subalgebras of an arbitrary Lie algebra L are conjugate under E(L).

### Definition: rank of a Lie algebra {#fc14-c04-u033}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U033` · §16.4; p. 86 · after [`FC14-C03-U009`](c03.html#fc14-c03-u009), [`FC14-C04-U032`](#fc14-c04-u032)
:::

The rank of an arbitrary Lie algebra L is the dimension of a Cartan subalgebra. This is well-defined by conjugacy; for semisimple L it equals the rank of its root system.

### Corollary: Borels containing a fixed Cartan subalgebra are standard {#fc14-c04-u034}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U034` · §16.4; pp. 86–87 · after [`FC14-C04-U030`](#fc14-c04-u030), [`FC14-C04-U031`](#fc14-c04-u031)
:::

For semisimple L with fixed CSA H, every Borel subalgebra containing H equals B(Δ) for some base Δ of the root system.

### Theorem/description: automorphism group of a semisimple Lie algebra {#fc14-c04-u035}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C04-U035` · §16.5; p. 87 · after [`FC14-C03-U053`](c03.html#fc14-c03-u053), [`FC14-C04-U022`](#fc14-c04-u022), [`FC14-C04-U031`](#fc14-c04-u031)
:::

For semisimple L, every automorphism is a product of an inner automorphism and a graph automorphism; diagonal automorphisms are inner. The source records Aut(L)=Int(L)⋊Γ(L) and E(L)=Int(L), with Γ(L) the Dynkin-diagram automorphism group.
