Chapter V. Existence Theorem

FC14 — Humphreys, Introduction to Lie Algebras and Representation Theory.

This chapter contains 9 definitions, 8 theorems, 4 propositions, 4 lemmas, 6 corollaries, 1 example, 7 constructions and 3 conventions.

Nobody has checked this chapter against Lean yet, so the statements below say nothing about whether they are formalized.

Convention: field and dimension conventions for enveloping algebras

FC14-C05-U001 · §17 opening; p. 89

In §17 the field F is arbitrary; Lie algebras may be infinite-dimensional where explicitly allowed.

Definition/construction: tensor algebra

FC14-C05-U002 · §17.1; pp. 89–90

For a vector space V, T(V)=⊕_{m≥0}T^mV with T^0V=F, T^1V=V and TmV=V{⊗m}; concatenation of tensors makes T(V) an associative graded F-algebra with 1, universal for linear maps V→A into associative unital F-algebras.

Definition/construction: symmetric algebra

FC14-C05-U003 · §17.1; pp. 89–90 · after FC14-C05-U002

S(V)=T(V)/I where I is generated by x⊗y−y⊗x. It is the universal commutative associative unital algebra receiving V, is graded, and for a basis of V identifies canonically with a polynomial algebra.

Definition/result: homogeneous symmetric tensors

FC14-C05-U004 · §17.1; p. 90 · after FC14-C05-U002, FC14-C05-U003

In characteristic zero, the symmetric group acts on T^mV by permuting tensor factors. Fixed tensors are homogeneous symmetric tensors of order m; averaging over permutations gives a subspace canonically isomorphic to S^mV and hence a graded vector-space copy of S(V) inside T(V).

Definition: universal enveloping algebra

FC14-C05-U005 · §17.2; pp. 90–91

A universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra L is an associative unital algebra U with a linear map i:L→U satisfying i([x,y])=i(x)i(y)−i(y)i(x), universal among such pairs. It is unique up to unique isomorphism.

Construction: U(L) as a quotient of T(L)

FC14-C05-U006 · §17.2; p. 91 · after FC14-C05-U002, FC14-C05-U005

Let J be the two-sided ideal of T(L) generated by x⊗y−y⊗x−[x,y]. Then U(L)=T(L)/J with the induced map i:L→U(L) satisfies the universal property.

Example: enveloping algebra of an abelian Lie algebra

FC14-C05-U007 · §17.2; p. 91 · after FC14-C01-U018, FC14-C05-U003, FC14-C05-U006

If L is abelian, U(L) is canonically the symmetric algebra S(L).

Construction: PBW filtration and associated graded algebra

FC14-C05-U008 · §17.3; p. 91 · after FC14-C05-U003, FC14-C05-U006

Filter T(L) by tensor degree T_m and U(L) by U_m=π(T_m). The quotients G^m=U_m/U_{m−1} form the associated graded algebra G; the canonical T(L)→G kills the commutativity ideal and induces a surjective graded algebra map w:S(L)→G.

Theorem: Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem

FC14-C05-U009 · §17.3; p. 92 · after FC14-C05-U008

The canonical graded algebra homomorphism w:S(L)→gr U(L) is an isomorphism.

Corollary: degree-m complements in the PBW filtration

FC14-C05-U010 · §17.3; p. 92 · after FC14-C05-U009

If W⊆T^mL maps isomorphically to S^mL, then its image in U_m is a vector-space complement of U_{m−1}.

Corollary: canonical embedding of L into U(L)

FC14-C05-U011 · §17.3; p. 92 · after FC14-C05-U010

The canonical map i:L→U(L) is injective, so L may be identified with its image in U(L).

Corollary: PBW basis

FC14-C05-U012 · §17.3; p. 92 · after FC14-C05-U009, FC14-C05-U011

For any ordered basis (x_i) of L, 1 together with ordered monomials x_{i_1}⋯x_{i_m}, i_1≤⋯≤i_m, form a basis of U(L). Such a basis is called a PBW basis.

Corollary: enveloping algebra over a subalgebra

FC14-C05-U013 · §17.3; p. 92 · after FC14-C01-U004, FC14-C05-U012

If H≤L and an ordered basis of H is extended to one of L, the induced map U(H)→U(L) is injective and U(L) is a free U(H)-module on the ordered monomials in the complementary basis elements.

Corollary: symmetric tensors as PBW filtration complements

FC14-C05-U014 · §17.3; p. 92 · after FC14-C05-U004, FC14-C05-U010

In characteristic zero, the canonical map from homogeneous symmetric tensors SmL⊂TmL to U_m is an isomorphism onto a complement of U_{m−1}.

Lemma: PBW proof: recursive action on the symmetric algebra

FC14-C05-U015 · §17.4; pp. 93–94 · after FC14-C05-U003

For an ordered basis of L, the source constructs compatible linear maps f_m:L⊗S_m→S satisfying the order, filtration, and commutator recursion needed to define the PBW representation.

Lemma: PBW proof: representation on S(L)

FC14-C05-U016 · §17.4; p. 94 · after FC14-C01-U032, FC14-C05-U015

The recursive maps assemble to a representation ρ:L→gl(S(L)) whose leading term is multiplication by the corresponding degree-one variable.

Lemma: PBW proof: highest homogeneous term of a relation

FC14-C05-U017 · §17.4; p. 94 · after FC14-C05-U003, FC14-C05-U006, FC14-C05-U016

If t lies in the kernel J of T(L)→U(L), then its highest homogeneous component lies in the commutativity ideal I=Ker(T(L)→S(L)).

Theorem: PBW kernel comparison

FC14-C05-U018 · §17.4; pp. 94–95 · after FC14-C05-U017

The preceding representation proves PBW by showing that a homogeneous t∈T^mL whose image lies in U_{m−1} must lie in I; equivalently S(L)≅gr U(L).

Definition/construction: free Lie algebra

FC14-C05-U019 · §17.5; p. 95 · after FC14-C05-U002, FC14-C05-U005

A Lie algebra L generated by X is free on X if every map X→M into a Lie algebra extends uniquely to a Lie homomorphism L→M. It exists as the Lie subalgebra generated by X inside the tensor algebra on the vector space with basis X.

Construction: modules over a free Lie algebra

FC14-C05-U020 · §17.5; p. 95 · after FC14-C01-U032, FC14-C05-U019

To make V an L-module when L is free on X, it suffices to assign arbitrary endomorphisms of V to the generators X; the assignment extends uniquely to a representation.

Definition: Lie algebra by generators and relations

FC14-C05-U021 · §17.5; p. 95 · after FC14-C01-U021, FC14-C05-U019

If L is free on X and R is the ideal generated by relations f_j, then L/R is called the Lie algebra with generators given by the images of X and relations f_j=0.

Convention: setting for Serre presentation

FC14-C05-U022 · §18 opening; pp. 95–96

In §18, L is semisimple over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero; infinite-dimensional intermediate Lie algebras are allowed.

Proposition: Chevalley–Serre relations satisfied by standard generators

FC14-C05-U023 · §18.1; p. 96 · after FC14-C03-U036, FC14-C04-U004

For standard generators x_i,y_i,h_i associated with a base of Φ, L is generated by them and satisfies [h_i,h_j]=0; [x_i,y_j]=δ_{ij}h_i; [h_i,x_j]=c_{ji}x_j, [h_i,y_j]=−c_{ji}y_j; and the Serre nilpotence relations (ad x_i)^{1−c_{ji}}x_j=0 and (ad y_i)^{1−c_{ji}}y_j=0 for i≠j.

Construction: Lie algebra L0 defined by relations S1–S3

FC14-C05-U024 · §18.2; pp. 96–97 · after FC14-C03-U036, FC14-C05-U019, FC14-C05-U023

For a root system with Cartan matrix (c_{ij}), start from the free Lie algebra on generators X_i,Y_i,H_i and quotient only by the relations [h_i,h_j]=0, [x_i,y_j]=δ_{ij}h_i, [h_i,x_j]=c_{ji}x_j, [h_i,y_j]=−c_{ji}y_j. The resulting algebra is denoted L_0.

Construction: model representation of L0 on a tensor algebra

FC14-C05-U025 · §18.2; pp. 96–97 · after FC14-C05-U002, FC14-C05-U024

On the tensor algebra generated by v_1,…,v_l, the source defines explicit endomorphisms for h_i,y_i,x_i and obtains a representation of the free Lie algebra.

Proposition: the model representation factors through L0

FC14-C05-U026 · §18.2; p. 97 · after FC14-C05-U024, FC14-C05-U025

The explicit representation annihilates the defining relations S1–S3, hence factors through L_0.

Theorem: triangular decomposition of L0

FC14-C05-U027 · §18.2; pp. 97–98 · after FC14-C05-U026

In L_0 the h_i form a basis of an l-dimensional abelian subalgebra H, and L_0=Y⊕H⊕X, where X and Y are the subalgebras generated by the x_i and y_i. Nonzero H-weights are integral combinations of simple roots with coefficients all positive or all negative.

Lemma: Serre generators are killed by opposite simple raising operators

FC14-C05-U028 · §18.3; p. 99 · after FC14-C05-U023, FC14-C05-U027

For y_{ij}=(ad y_i)^{1−c_{ji}}y_j in L_0, ad x_k(y_{ij})=0 for every k; the analogous statement holds after exchanging x and y.

Definition/construction: locally nilpotent endomorphism and exponential

FC14-C05-U029 · §18.3; p. 99 · after FC14-C01-U035

An endomorphism is locally nilpotent if every vector is killed by some power. Its exponential is defined by patching the finite exponential series on finite-dimensional invariant subspaces; this yields an automorphism.

Theorem: Serre’s Theorem

FC14-C05-U030 · §18.3; pp. 99–101 · after FC14-C03-U004, FC14-C04-U015, FC14-C05-U023, FC14-C05-U027, FC14-C05-U028, FC14-C05-U029

For a root system Φ with base Δ, the Lie algebra generated by x_i,y_i,h_i subject to S1–S3 and the Serre relations is finite-dimensional semisimple; H=span(h_i) is a Cartan subalgebra and its root system is Φ.

Corollary: dimension from the root system in Serre’s construction

FC14-C05-U031 · §18.3; p. 100 · after FC14-C05-U030

The Lie algebra in Serre’s theorem has root decomposition H⊕⊕_{α∈Φ}L_α with dim H=rank Φ and dim L_α=1; hence dim L=rank Φ+|Φ|.

Theorem: existence of semisimple Lie algebras with prescribed root system

FC14-C05-U032 · §18.4; p. 101 · after FC14-C03-U052, FC14-C05-U030

For every root system Φ there exists a semisimple Lie algebra having Φ as its root system.

Theorem: existence-and-uniqueness isomorphism theorem from Serre presentation

FC14-C05-U033 · §18.4; p. 101 · after FC14-C04-U005, FC14-C05-U030

If semisimple L,L′ have isomorphic root systems and chosen compatible Cartan subalgebras/bases and nonzero simple-root vectors, there is a unique Lie-algebra isomorphism extending the Cartan isomorphism and the chosen root-vector identifications.

Convention: classification setting for simple algebras

FC14-C05-U034 · §19 opening; p. 102 · after FC14-C03-U042, FC14-C05-U032, FC14-C05-U033

In §19, F is algebraically closed of characteristic zero. Up to isomorphism the simple Lie algebras correspond to A_l,B_l,C_l,D_l and the exceptional types E_6,E_7,E_8,F_4,G_2, with standard rank restrictions.

Definition: reductive Lie algebra

FC14-C05-U035 · §19.1; p. 102 · after FC14-C01-U022, FC14-C01-U042

A nonzero Lie algebra L is reductive if Rad L=Z(L). Abelian and semisimple algebras are the two extreme cases.

Proposition: structure and irreducible-action criterion for reductive Lie algebras

FC14-C05-U036 · §19.1; p. 102 · after FC14-C02-U003, FC14-C02-U041, FC14-C05-U035

If L is reductive, L=[L,L]⊕Z(L), with [L,L] semisimple or zero. If nonzero L⊆gl(V) acts irreducibly, then L is reductive with dim Z(L)≤1; if moreover L⊆sl(V), then L is semisimple.

Proposition: classical Lie algebras are the simple types A–D

FC14-C05-U037 · §19.2; pp. 102–103 · after FC14-C01-U007, FC14-C01-U008, FC14-C01-U009, FC14-C01-U010, FC14-C05-U036

The classical algebras sl(l+1,F), o(2l+1,F), sp(2l,F), and o(2l,F) act irreducibly in their natural modules, are semisimple by the preceding criterion, and their diagonal Cartan subalgebras/root spaces identify them as the simple types A_l,B_l,C_l,D_l in the stated ranges.

Construction: 14-dimensional matrix model of G2

FC14-C05-U038 · §19.3; pp. 103–104 · after FC14-C01-U009, FC14-C03-U051

Inside o(7,F), the source explicitly spans a 14-dimensional Lie algebra L by a two-dimensional diagonal subalgebra H and twelve displayed long/short root vectors, with bracket relations closing L.

Theorem/example: matrix model is the simple Lie algebra G2

FC14-C05-U039 · §19.3; p. 104 · after FC14-C05-U036, FC14-C05-U038

The constructed 14-dimensional L acts irreducibly on F^7, lies in sl(7,F), and is therefore semisimple; its rank-two root data force the irreducible root system G_2, so L is the simple algebra of type G_2.

Definition/construction: Cayley/octonion algebra model

FC14-C05-U040 · §19.3; pp. 104–105 · after FC14-C01-U013

The source defines an eight-dimensional nonassociative Cayley (octonion) algebra C as 2×2 block matrices built from scalars and F^3, with multiplication using dot and cross products; it records the identity and a seven-dimensional trace-zero commutator subspace C_0.

Construction: derivation algebra of the Cayley algebra

FC14-C05-U041 · §19.3; pp. 105–106 · after FC14-C01-U014, FC14-C05-U040

Der(C) acts faithfully on the seven-dimensional trace-zero subspace C_0 and trivially on scalars. The source constructs an embedded sl(3,F) of derivations and a two-dimensional diagonal subalgebra H that is self-centralizing.

Theorem/identification: G2 as the derivation algebra of the octonions

FC14-C05-U042 · §19.3; p. 106 · after FC14-C05-U039, FC14-C05-U041

The source identifies the derivation algebra Der(C), via its faithful action on C_0, with the previously constructed 14-dimensional simple Lie algebra of type G_2; the remaining explicit derivations are deferred to exercises.
