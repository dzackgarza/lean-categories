### Theorem: common eigenvector theorem for solvable linear Lie algebras {#fc14-c02-u002}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U002` · §4.1; pp. 15–16 · after [`FC14-C01-U041`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u041)
:::

If L⊆gl(V) is solvable, V finite-dimensional and nonzero, then V contains a common eigenvector for all elements of L.

### Corollary: Lie’s Theorem {#fc14-c02-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U003` · §4.1; p. 16 · after [`FC14-C01-U050`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u050), [`FC14-C02-U002`](#fc14-c02-u002)
:::

A solvable linear Lie algebra L⊆gl(V) stabilizes a complete flag; equivalently it is simultaneously upper triangularizable.

### Corollary: ideal flag in a solvable Lie algebra {#fc14-c02-u004}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U004` · §4.1; p. 16 · after [`FC14-C01-U041`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u041), [`FC14-C02-U003`](#fc14-c02-u003)
:::

Every solvable Lie algebra L has a chain of ideals 0=L_0⊂L_1⊂⋯⊂L_n=L with dim L_i=i.

### Corollary: nilpotence of the derived algebra of a solvable Lie algebra {#fc14-c02-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U005` · §4.1; pp. 16–17 · after [`FC14-C01-U023`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u023), [`FC14-C01-U047`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u047), [`FC14-C02-U004`](#fc14-c02-u004)
:::

If L is solvable and x∈[L,L], then ad_L x is nilpotent; in particular [L,L] is nilpotent.

### Definition: semisimple endomorphism {#fc14-c02-u006}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U006` · §4.2; p. 17
:::

For finite-dimensional V, x∈End(V) is semisimple if its minimal polynomial has distinct roots; over algebraically closed F this is equivalent to diagonalizability.

### Proposition: Jordan–Chevalley decomposition {#fc14-c02-u007}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U007` · §4.2; pp. 17–18 · after [`FC14-C02-U006`](#fc14-c02-u006)
:::

Every x∈End(V) has unique commuting semisimple and nilpotent parts x_s,x_n with x=x_s+x_n. They are polynomials in x without constant term, commute with every endomorphism commuting with x, and preserve the source/target condition x(B)⊆A between invariant subspaces.

### Definition: additive Jordan–Chevalley decomposition {#fc14-c02-u008}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U008` · §4.2; p. 17 · after [`FC14-C02-U007`](#fc14-c02-u007)
:::

The decomposition x=x_s+x_n of the proposition is the additive Jordan–Chevalley (Jordan) decomposition; x_s and x_n are the semisimple and nilpotent parts.

### Lemma: Jordan decomposition under the adjoint representation {#fc14-c02-u009}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U009` · §4.2; p. 18 · after [`FC14-C01-U016`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u016), [`FC14-C02-U007`](#fc14-c02-u007)
:::

If x=x_s+x_n in End(V), then ad x=ad x_s+ad x_n is the Jordan decomposition of ad x on End(V).

### Lemma: derivation algebras contain Jordan parts {#fc14-c02-u010}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U010` · §4.2; pp. 18–19 · after [`FC14-C01-U014`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u014), [`FC14-C02-U007`](#fc14-c02-u007)
:::

For a finite-dimensional F-algebra A, Der(A) contains the semisimple and nilpotent parts in End(A) of each derivation.

### Lemma: trace criterion for nilpotence {#fc14-c02-u011}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U011` · §4.3; pp. 19–20 · after [`FC14-C02-U007`](#fc14-c02-u007)
:::

Let A⊆B⊆gl(V) and M={x∈gl(V) | [x,B]⊆A}. If x∈M satisfies Tr(xy)=0 for every y∈M, then x is nilpotent.

### Formula: trace commutator identity {#fc14-c02-u012}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U012` · §4.3; p. 19 · after [`FC14-C01-U005`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u005)
:::

For endomorphisms x,y,z of a finite-dimensional vector space, Tr([x,y]z)=Tr(x[y,z]).

### Theorem: Cartan’s solvability criterion {#fc14-c02-u013}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U013` · §4.3; p. 20 · after [`FC14-C01-U023`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u023), [`FC14-C01-U047`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u047), [`FC14-C02-U011`](#fc14-c02-u011), [`FC14-C02-U012`](#fc14-c02-u012)
:::

If L⊆gl(V) and Tr(xy)=0 for all x∈[L,L], y∈L, then L is solvable.

### Corollary: Cartan criterion via adjoint traces {#fc14-c02-u014}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U014` · §4.3; p. 20 · after [`FC14-C01-U016`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u016), [`FC14-C02-U013`](#fc14-c02-u013)
:::

If Tr(ad x ad y)=0 for all x∈[L,L], y∈L, then L is solvable.

### Definition: Killing form {#fc14-c02-u015}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U015` · §5.1; p. 21 · after [`FC14-C01-U016`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u016), [`FC14-C02-U012`](#fc14-c02-u012)
:::

The Killing form of L is κ(x,y)=Tr(ad x ad y). It is symmetric bilinear and associative: κ([x,y],z)=κ(x,[y,z]).

### Lemma: Killing form on an ideal {#fc14-c02-u016}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U016` · §5.1; pp. 21–22 · after [`FC14-C01-U021`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u021), [`FC14-C02-U015`](#fc14-c02-u015)
:::

If I is an ideal of L, the Killing form of I equals the restriction of the Killing form of L to I×I.

### Definition: nondegenerate symmetric form and radical {#fc14-c02-u017}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U017` · §5.1; p. 22 · after [`FC14-C01-U021`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u021)
:::

A symmetric bilinear form B is nondegenerate when its radical {x | B(x,y)=0 for all y} is zero. For the Killing form the radical is an ideal.

::: {.unit-meta}

For char F≠2, the Killing form of sl(2,F) has the displayed nonsingular matrix in the standard basis, hence is nondegenerate.

L is semisimple iff it has no nonzero abelian ideals.

### Theorem: Cartan criterion for semisimplicity {#fc14-c02-u020}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U020` · §5.1; p. 22 · after [`FC14-C01-U042`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u042), [`FC14-C02-U013`](#fc14-c02-u013), [`FC14-C02-U015`](#fc14-c02-u015), [`FC14-C02-U017`](#fc14-c02-u017)
:::

A Lie algebra L is semisimple iff its Killing form is nondegenerate.

### Definition: direct sum of ideals {#fc14-c02-u021}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U021` · §5.2; p. 22 · after [`FC14-C01-U021`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u021)
:::

L is the direct sum of ideals I_1,…,I_t when L=I_1⊕⋯⊕I_t as vector spaces; then [I_i,I_j]=0 for i≠j and brackets are componentwise.

### Theorem: decomposition of a semisimple Lie algebra into simple ideals {#fc14-c02-u022}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U022` · §5.2; pp. 22–23 · after [`FC14-C01-U025`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u025), [`FC14-C02-U016`](#fc14-c02-u016), [`FC14-C02-U020`](#fc14-c02-u020), [`FC14-C02-U021`](#fc14-c02-u021)
:::

Every semisimple L decomposes as a direct sum of simple ideals L_1⊕⋯⊕L_t. These are exactly the simple ideals of L, and each Killing form is the restriction of that of L.

### Corollary: ideals and quotients of a semisimple Lie algebra {#fc14-c02-u023}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U023` · §5.2; p. 23 · after [`FC14-C01-U023`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u023), [`FC14-C01-U030`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u030), [`FC14-C02-U022`](#fc14-c02-u022)
:::

For semisimple L, L=[L,L]; every ideal and homomorphic image is semisimple, and every ideal is a sum of some simple summands.

### Theorem: all derivations of a semisimple Lie algebra are inner {#fc14-c02-u024}

If L is semisimple, Der(L)=ad(L).

### Definition: abstract Jordan decomposition in a semisimple Lie algebra {#fc14-c02-u025}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U025` · §5.4; p. 24 · after [`FC14-C02-U009`](#fc14-c02-u009), [`FC14-C02-U010`](#fc14-c02-u010), [`FC14-C02-U024`](#fc14-c02-u024)

For x∈L semisimple, unique s,n∈L satisfy x=s+n, [s,n]=0 and ad x=ad s+ad n is the ordinary Jordan decomposition. These are the ad-semisimple and ad-nilpotent parts of x.

### Definition: L-module {#fc14-c02-u027}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U027` · §6.1; p. 25 · after [`FC14-C01-U032`](c01.html#fc14-c01-u032)
:::

An L-module is a vector space V with a bilinear action satisfying [x,y]v=x(yv)−y(xv); equivalently it is a representation L→gl(V).

### Definition: homomorphism and isomorphism of L-modules {#fc14-c02-u028}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U028` · §6.1; p. 25 · after [`FC14-C02-U027`](#fc14-c02-u027)
:::

An L-module homomorphism is a linear map commuting with the L-action; an isomorphism is a vector-space isomorphism of this kind, and isomorphic modules afford equivalent representations.

### Definition: irreducible L-module {#fc14-c02-u029}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U029` · §6.1; p. 25 · after [`FC14-C02-U027`](#fc14-c02-u027)
:::

A nonzero L-module is irreducible if its only L-submodules are 0 and itself.

### Definition: completely reducible L-module {#fc14-c02-u030}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U030` · §6.1; pp. 25–26 · after [`FC14-C02-U029`](#fc14-c02-u029)
:::

V is completely reducible if it is a direct sum of irreducible L-submodules; equivalently every submodule has an L-stable complement.

### Construction: direct sum of L-modules {#fc14-c02-u031}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U031` · §6.1; p. 26 · after [`FC14-C02-U027`](#fc14-c02-u027)
:::

For L-modules W,W′, the vector-space direct sum becomes an L-module componentwise.

### Lemma: Schur’s Lemma {#fc14-c02-u032}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U032` · §6.1; p. 26 · after [`FC14-C02-U029`](#fc14-c02-u029)
:::

For an irreducible representation φ:L→gl(V), every endomorphism of V commuting with φ(L) is scalar.

### Construction: dual/contragredient module {#fc14-c02-u033}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U033` · §6.1; p. 26 · after [`FC14-C02-U027`](#fc14-c02-u027)
:::

For an L-module V, the dual V* is an L-module by (x·f)(v)=−f(x·v).

### Construction: tensor product of L-modules {#fc14-c02-u034}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U034` · §6.1; p. 26 · after [`FC14-C02-U027`](#fc14-c02-u027)
:::

For L-modules V,W, V⊗W is an L-module via x·(v⊗w)=xv⊗w+v⊗xw.

### Construction: Hom and End as L-modules {#fc14-c02-u035}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U035` · §6.1; pp. 26–27 · after [`FC14-C02-U033`](#fc14-c02-u033), [`FC14-C02-U034`](#fc14-c02-u034)
:::

Hom(V,W)≅V*⊗W carries the action (x·f)(v)=x f(v)−f(xv); in particular End(V) is an L-module.

### Definition/proposition: faithful representation and its trace form {#fc14-c02-u036}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U036` · §6.2; p. 27 · after [`FC14-C02-U013`](#fc14-c02-u013), [`FC14-C02-U020`](#fc14-c02-u020)
:::

A representation is faithful when it is injective. If L is semisimple and φ:L→gl(V) faithful, B(x,y)=Tr(φ(x)φ(y)) is a nondegenerate symmetric associative bilinear form on L.

### Definition: Casimir element {#fc14-c02-u037}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC14-C02-U037` · §6.2; pp. 27–28 · after [`FC14-C02-U032`](#fc14-c02-u032), [`FC14-C02-U036`](#fc14-c02-u036)
:::

For semisimple L, a representation φ, and dual bases x_i,y_i relative to a nondegenerate associative trace form on the faithful part, c_φ=Σφ(x_i)φ(y_i) commutes with φ(L). For faithful irreducible φ it is scalar with trace dim L and value dim L/dim V.

For the natural two-dimensional representation of sl(2,F), the Casimir element is xy+(1/2)h²+yx=(3/2)I.

For nonfaithful φ, decompose semisimple L into simple ideals; restrict to the sum complementary to Kerφ where φ is faithful and perform the Casimir construction there.

### Theorem: root-system axioms for the roots of a semisimple Lie algebra {#fc14-c02-u064}

::: {.unit-meta}

In the Euclidean space E, Φ spans E and excludes 0; the only scalar multiples of α∈Φ are ±α; reflection σ_α(β)=β−2(β,α)/(α,α) α preserves Φ; and 2(β,α)/(α,α)∈Z. Thus Φ is a root system.
