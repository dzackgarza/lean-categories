### Definition: reflection in a Euclidean space {#fc15-c01-u001}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C01-U001` · §1.1; p. 3
:::

For nonzero a in Euclidean V, the reflection s_a fixes H_a=a^⊥ pointwise and sends a to −a; explicitly s_a(λ)=λ−2(λ,a)/(a,a)a. It is orthogonal, has order two, and depends only on the line Ra.

### Definition: finite reflection group {#fc15-c01-u002}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C01-U002` · §1.1; p. 3 · after [`FC15-C01-U001`](#fc15-c01-u001)
:::

A finite reflection group is a finite subgroup of O(V) generated by reflections.

### Example: dihedral reflection group I_2(m) {#fc15-c01-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}

The symmetry group of a regular m-gon is the dihedral group of order 2m, generated by two adjacent reflections; their product is rotation through 2π/m.

### Example: symmetric group as type A_{n-1} {#fc15-c01-u004}

### Definition: essential reflection-group action {#fc15-c01-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C01-U005` · §1.1; p. 5 · after [`FC15-C01-U002`](#fc15-c01-u002)

A reflection group W acting on V is essential relative to V if its fixed-point space is zero. Any orthogonal action restricts to an essential action on the orthogonal complement of its fixed-point space.

### Proposition: orthogonal conjugacy of reflections {#fc15-c01-u008}

### Definition: root system for a finite reflection group {#fc15-c01-u009}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C01-U009` · §1.2; pp. 6–7 · after [`FC15-C01-U001`](#fc15-c01-u001)
:::

A root system Φ⊂V is a finite set of nonzero vectors satisfying Φ∩Rα={α,−α} and s_αΦ=Φ for all α∈Φ. Its associated reflection group is generated by the s_α. Any such group is finite.

### Remark/comparison: normalization of a root system {#fc15-c01-u010}

`FC15-C01-U010` · §1.2; p. 7 · after [`FC15-C01-U009`](#fc15-c01-u009)

Replacing every root line by its two unit vectors produces another root system with the same associated reflection group.

### Definition: total ordering and positive vectors {#fc15-c01-u011}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C01-U011` · §1.3; pp. 7–8
:::

A total ordering on V is compatible with addition and multiplication by positive scalars, with sign reversal for negative scalars. A vector is positive if it is greater than zero; lexicographic order from an ordered basis supplies such an ordering.

### Definition: positive and negative root systems {#fc15-c01-u012}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C01-U012` · §1.3; p. 8 · after [`FC15-C01-U009`](#fc15-c01-u009), [`FC15-C01-U011`](#fc15-c01-u011)
:::

A positive system Π consists of the roots positive for some total ordering; Φ is the disjoint union Π⊔(−Π), with −Π the corresponding negative system.

### Definition: simple system and simple roots {#fc15-c01-u013}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C01-U013` · §1.3; p. 8 · after [`FC15-C01-U009`](#fc15-c01-u009)

A simple system Δ⊂Φ is a basis of span_R Φ such that every root has coordinates in Δ all nonnegative or all nonpositive. Elements of Δ are simple roots.

### Definition: Coxeter group and Coxeter system {#fc15-c01-u034}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C01-U034` · §1.9; p. 18 · after [`FC15-C01-U033`](#fc15-c01-u033)

A Coxeter system (W,S) is a group with generators S and relations (ss′)^{m(s,s′)}=1, where m(s,s)=1 and m(s,s′) may be ∞, in which case the relation is omitted. W is then a Coxeter group.
