### Definition: Coxeter graph of a finite reflection group {#fc15-c02-u001}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C02-U001` · §2.1; p. 29 · after [`FC15-C01-U033`](c01.html#fc15-c01-u033)
:::

For simple roots Δ, the Coxeter graph has one vertex per simple reflection, an edge between distinct vertices when m(α,β)>2, labelled by m(α,β) when >3; omitted edges mean m=2 and label 3 is conventionally omitted. It determines W up to abstract isomorphism.

### Proposition: geometric uniqueness from the Coxeter graph {#fc15-c02-u002}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C02-U002` · §2.1; pp. 29–30 · after [`FC15-C01-U005`](c01.html#fc15-c01-u005), [`FC15-C01-U013`](c01.html#fc15-c01-u013), [`FC15-C02-U001`](#fc15-c02-u001)
:::

If two essential finite reflection groups have the same Coxeter graph, an isometry of their Euclidean spaces identifies compatible simple systems and conjugates the groups. In a common V they are O(V)-conjugate.

### Definition: irreducible Coxeter system {#fc15-c02-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C02-U003` · §2.2; p. 30 · after [`FC15-C02-U001`](#fc15-c02-u001)
:::

A Coxeter system is irreducible when its Coxeter graph is connected; the associated root system is also called irreducible.

### Proposition: decomposition by connected Coxeter-graph components {#fc15-c02-u004}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C02-U004` · §2.2; p. 30 · after [`FC15-C01-U035`](c01.html#fc15-c01-u035), [`FC15-C02-U003`](#fc15-c02-u003)
:::

If the Coxeter graph has connected components with vertex sets S_1,…,S_r, then W is the direct product W_{S_1}×⋯×W_{S_r}, and each (W_{S_i},S_i) is irreducible.

### Definition: abstract Coxeter graph {#fc15-c02-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C02-U005` · §2.3; p. 31 · after [`FC15-C01-U034`](c01.html#fc15-c01-u034)
:::

A Coxeter graph is a finite undirected graph whose edges are labelled by integers ≥3 or ∞, with label 3 omitted; set m(s,s)=1 and m(s,s′)=2 for nonadjacent distinct vertices.

### Construction: bilinear form associated to a Coxeter graph {#fc15-c02-u006}

::: {.unit-meta}

For vertex set S, associate the symmetric matrix/form with diagonal 1 and off-diagonal entry −cos(π/m(s,s′)), interpreting m=∞ as cos 0=1.

### Definition: positive definite, semidefinite, and positive type {#fc15-c02-u007}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C02-U007` · §2.3; p. 31 · after [`FC15-C02-U006`](#fc15-c02-u006)
:::

A symmetric matrix/form is positive definite when x^tAx>0 for x≠0, positive semidefinite when ≥0, and of positive type when positive semidefinite. A Coxeter graph inherits these adjectives through its associated form.

### Criterion: principal-minor criterion for positive type {#fc15-c02-u008}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C02-U008` · §2.3; p. 31 · after [`FC15-C02-U007`](#fc15-c02-u007)

A symmetric matrix is positive definite iff all leading principal minors are positive; the corresponding nonnegative condition characterizes positive semidefiniteness in the setup used by the source.

### Definition: indecomposable symmetric matrix {#fc15-c02-u012}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C02-U012` · §2.6; p. 35 · after [`FC15-C02-U006`](#fc15-c02-u006)

A real matrix is indecomposable if its index set admits no nontrivial partition I⊔J with all cross-block entries zero. A Coxeter matrix is indecomposable exactly when its graph is connected.

### Proposition: Perron–Frobenius/M-matrix lemma {#fc15-c02-u013}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C02-U013` · §2.6; pp. 35–36 · after [`FC15-C02-U007`](#fc15-c02-u007), [`FC15-C02-U012`](#fc15-c02-u012)
:::

Let A be symmetric, positive semidefinite, indecomposable, with nonpositive off-diagonal entries. Its nullspace has dimension at most one; its least eigenvalue is simple and has an eigenvector with all coordinates strictly positive.

### Definition: crystallographic subgroup {#fc15-c02-u016}

### Definition: crystallographic root system and Cartan integers {#fc15-c02-u018}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C02-U018` · §2.9; p. 39 · after [`FC15-C01-U009`](c01.html#fc15-c01-u009)
:::

A root system is crystallographic if 2(α,β)/(β,β)∈Z for all roots α,β. These integers are Cartan integers. The associated reflection group is its Weyl group.

### Theorem/recorded classification: Weyl groups are crystallographic reflection groups {#fc15-c02-u019}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C02-U019` · §2.9; p. 39 · after [`FC15-C02-U017`](#fc15-c02-u017), [`FC15-C02-U018`](#fc15-c02-u018)
:::

The Weyl groups arising from crystallographic root systems are exactly the finite reflection groups whose Coxeter labels lie in {2,3,4,6}. Distinct root systems B_n,C_n have the same Weyl group.

### Definition/result: long and short roots; Dynkin diagram {#fc15-c02-u020}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C02-U020` · §2.9; p. 39 · after [`FC15-C02-U003`](#fc15-c02-u003), [`FC15-C02-U018`](#fc15-c02-u018)
:::

An irreducible crystallographic root system has at most two root lengths, with squared-length ratio 2 or 3. Multiple Coxeter edges are oriented toward the short root, producing the Dynkin diagram; one-length systems are simply laced.

### Definition: weight and coweight lattices; index of connection {#fc15-c02-u023}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C02-U023` · §2.9; p. 40 · after [`FC15-C02-U021`](#fc15-c02-u021), [`FC15-C02-U022`](#fc15-c02-u022)
:::

The weight lattice is L^*(Φ)={λ | (λ,α∨)∈Z for all α}, and the coweight lattice analogously uses roots. The root lattice has finite index f in the weight lattice, equal to the determinant of the Cartan matrix; f is the index of connection.

### Construction: quaternionic realization of H4 roots {#fc15-c02-u032}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C02-U032` · §2.13; pp. 46–48 · after [`FC15-C01-U009`](c01.html#fc15-c01-u009), [`FC15-C02-U031`](#fc15-c02-u031)
:::

Identify R^4 with the real quaternions. Any finite even-order subgroup of unit quaternions is a root system because reflection in unit α is λ↦−αar λ α. The explicit 120-element quaternion subgroup built from 1,(1+i+j+k)/2 and golden-ratio coordinates gives the H_4 root system and a displayed simple system.

### Theorem/result: orders of H3 and H4 from the root construction {#fc15-c02-u034}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C02-U034` · §2.13; pp. 47–48 · after [`FC15-C01-U042`](c01.html#fc15-c01-u042), [`FC15-C02-U032`](#fc15-c02-u032), [`FC15-C02-U033`](#fc15-c02-u033)
