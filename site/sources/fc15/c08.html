Chapter 8. Complements

FC15 — Humphreys, Reflection Groups and Coxeter Groups.

This chapter contains 7 definitions, 16 theorems, 1 proposition, 2 corollaries, 2 examples, 7 constructions, 1 convention and 4 other statements.

Nobody has checked this chapter against Lean yet, so the statements below say nothing about whether they are formalized.

Convention: chapter-wide Coxeter-system setting

FC15-C08-U001 · Chapter 8 opening; p. 171 · after FC15-C05-U001

Unless otherwise stated, (W,S) denotes an arbitrary Coxeter system throughout this survey chapter.

Construction: Tits elementary simplifications for words

FC15-C08-U002 · §8.1; pp. 171–172 · after FC15-C05-U001, FC15-C05-U020

Let F be the free group on symbols in bijection with S and F^+ the positive word monoid mapping onto W. Elementary simplifications either apply one braid relation without changing word length or delete an adjacent equal pair; E(u) is the finite effectively computable set of words reachable from u by such simplifications.

Theorem: Tits solution of the word problem

FC15-C08-U003 · §8.1; p. 172 · after FC15-C08-U002

For positive words u,v, their images in W are equal iff E(u)∩E(v) is nonempty. In particular a word represents 1 iff its simplification set contains the empty word.

Theorem/recorded result: conjugacy problem for extra-large Coxeter groups

FC15-C08-U004 · §8.1; p. 172 · after FC15-C05-U001

The source records Appel–Schupp’s algorithm solving the conjugacy problem for extra-large Coxeter groups, where every finite m(s,s′) for distinct generators is greater than 4.

Definition: reflection subgroup

FC15-C08-U005 · §8.2; p. 172 · after FC15-C05-U017

Let T be the set of all reflections of W. A reflection subgroup is a subgroup W′≤W generated by a subset of T.

Construction: canonical generators of a reflection subgroup

FC15-C08-U006 · §8.2; pp. 172–173 · after FC15-C05-U005, FC15-C08-U005

For w∈W put N(w)={t∈T | ℓ(tw)<ℓ(w)}. If W′ is a reflection subgroup, define S′ as the reflections t′∈W′ for which N(t′)∩W′={t′}.

Theorem: reflection subgroups are Coxeter groups

FC15-C08-U007 · §8.2; pp. 172–173 · after FC15-C08-U006

If W′ is generated by reflections and S′ is the canonical set defined from N, then (W′,S′) is a Coxeter system.

Definition: the (−1)-condition and W-equivalence for parabolic subsets

FC15-C08-U008 · §8.3; p. 173 · after FC15-C05-U015, FC15-C05-U009, FC15-C01-U032

For I⊆S, say I satisfies the (−1)-condition if W_I is finite and its longest element w_I acts as −1 on the geometric subspace V_I. Let J be the family of such I; I,J∈J are W-equivalent when some w∈W carries their simple-root sets to one another.

Theorem: Richardson classification of involutions

FC15-C08-U009 · §8.3; pp. 173–174 · after FC15-C08-U008

Every involution of W is conjugate to w_I for some I satisfying the (−1)-condition; for I,J in that family, w_I and w_J are conjugate iff I and J are W-equivalent.

Theorem/recorded result: canonical orthogonal reflection decomposition of involutions

FC15-C08-U010 · §8.3; p. 174 · after FC15-C05-U017, FC15-C08-U009

The source records Deodhar’s result that every involution has a unique totally orthogonal set of roots whose commuting reflections multiply to that involution.

Definition: Coxeter element of an arbitrary Coxeter system

FC15-C08-U011 · §8.4; p. 174 · after FC15-C05-U001

For an enumeration S={s_1,…,s_n}, the product w=s_1⋯s_n is a Coxeter element.

Proposition/result: conjugacy of Coxeter elements for tree Coxeter graphs

FC15-C08-U012 · §8.4; p. 174 · after FC15-C02-U005, FC15-C08-U011

If the Coxeter graph is a tree, Coxeter elements obtained from different enumerations of S form a single conjugacy class; this can fail when the graph has circuits.

Construction/result: Howlett matrix for a Coxeter element

FC15-C08-U013 · §8.4; pp. 174–175 · after FC15-C05-U007, FC15-C08-U011

In the geometric representation, let A be the Gram matrix of B and write 2A=U+U^t with U upper triangular unipotent according to the chosen ordering. The Coxeter element has matrix −U{-1}Ut.

Theorem: spectral characterization of infinite and affine Coxeter groups

FC15-C08-U014 · §8.4; p. 175 · after FC15-C06-U008, FC15-C06-U009, FC15-C08-U013

W is infinite iff its Coxeter element has infinite order, iff the geometric-representation matrix has a real eigenvalue >1. W is of affine type iff 1 is an eigenvalue and all other eigenvalues have absolute value 1.

Definition/result: Möbius function and inversion on a locally finite poset

FC15-C08-U015 · §8.5; pp. 175–176

For a poset in which every lower interval is finite, the Möbius function μ(x,y) is uniquely characterized by Σ_{x≤z≤y}μ(x,z)=δ_{x,y}; it gives the usual Möbius inversion formula for abelian-group-valued functions.

Theorem: Möbius function of Bruhat order

FC15-C08-U016 · §8.5; p. 176 · after FC15-C05-U022, FC15-C08-U015

For every Coxeter group and x≤w in Bruhat order, μ(x,w)=(−1)^{ℓ(x)+ℓ(w)}.

Corollary: Eulerian Bruhat intervals and rank-two diamonds

FC15-C08-U017 · §8.5; p. 176 · after FC15-C07-U035, FC15-C08-U016

Every nontrivial Bruhat interval has equally many even- and odd-length elements. In particular, if ℓ(w)−ℓ(x)=2 there are exactly two z with x<z<w.

Theorem/recorded result: Möbius function on parabolic quotient representatives

FC15-C08-U018 · §8.5; p. 176 · after FC15-C05-U030, FC15-C08-U015, FC15-C08-U016

For minimal coset representatives W^I with induced Bruhat order, Deodhar’s formula gives μI(x,w)=(−1){ℓ(x)+ℓ(w)} when the full interval [x,w] lies in W^I, and μ^I(x,w)=0 otherwise.

Definition: Bruhat graph

FC15-C08-U019 · §8.6; p. 177 · after FC15-C05-U017, FC15-C05-U022

The Bruhat graph has vertex set W and a directed edge tw→w whenever t is a reflection and ℓ(tw)<ℓ(w), without requiring the length difference to be one.

Theorem: finiteness of fixed-length Bruhat interval types

FC15-C08-U020 · §8.6; p. 177 · after FC15-C08-U019

For finite Coxeter groups, only finitely many poset-isomorphism types of Bruhat intervals of any fixed length occur.

Theorem/recorded result: interval poset determines the induced Bruhat graph

FC15-C08-U021 · §8.6; p. 177 · after FC15-C08-U019

For a closed Bruhat interval Z, its poset isomorphism type determines the isomorphism type of the full subgraph of the Bruhat graph on Z.

Recorded conjecture: interval invariance of R- and Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials

FC15-C08-U022 · §8.6; p. 177 · after FC15-C07-U015, FC15-C07-U026, FC15-C08-U021

The source records Dyer’s conjecture, prompted by Kazhdan–Lusztig, that R_{x,w} and hence P_{x,w} depend only on the poset-isomorphism type of the Bruhat interval [x,w].

Theorem: lexicographic shellability of Bruhat intervals

FC15-C08-U023 · §8.7; pp. 177–178 · after FC15-C05-U029

Every closed interval [x,w] in the Bruhat order of a Coxeter group is lexicographically shellable.

Construction/result: canonical increasing chain label in a Bruhat interval

FC15-C08-U024 · §8.7; pp. 177–178 · after FC15-C05-U020, FC15-C08-U023

Fixing a reduced word for w labels each maximal chain in [x,w] by the successive omitted generator positions. There is a unique maximal chain with increasing label, and its label is lexicographically first among all maximal-chain labels.

Corollary: topological and Cohen–Macaulay consequences of shellability

FC15-C08-U025 · §8.7; p. 178 · after FC15-C08-U016, FC15-C08-U023

Shellability recovers the Bruhat Möbius formula; passing to the order complex gives the standard topological formulation, and the associated Stanley–Reisner ring is Cohen–Macaulay.

Definition: automorphism of the Bruhat poset

FC15-C08-U026 · §8.8; p. 178 · after FC15-C05-U022, FC15-C05-U029

A Bruhat-order automorphism is a bijection θ:W→W such that x≤w iff θ(x)≤θ(w). Such an automorphism fixes 1 and preserves length.

Construction: inversion and Coxeter-graph automorphisms of Bruhat order

FC15-C08-U027 · §8.8; p. 178 · after FC15-C05-U026, FC15-C08-U026

The inversion map w↦w^{-1} is a Bruhat-poset automorphism. Every automorphism of the labelled Coxeter graph induces a group automorphism preserving Bruhat order.

Example: Bruhat automorphisms in rank two

FC15-C08-U028 · §8.8; p. 178 · after FC15-C05-U023, FC15-C08-U026

For a dihedral Coxeter group, elements of each nonzero intermediate length may be interchanged independently; in the finite order-2m case the Bruhat automorphism group is (Z/2Z)^{m−1}.

Theorem: classification of Bruhat-order automorphisms in irreducible rank at least three

FC15-C08-U029 · §8.8; pp. 178–179 · after FC15-C06-U001, FC15-C08-U027

If |S|≥3 and (W,S) is irreducible, every automorphism of Bruhat order is induced by a Coxeter-graph automorphism or is inversion composed with one.

Construction/comparison: factorization input for affine Poincaré series

FC15-C08-U030 · §8.9; pp. 179–180 · after FC15-C03-U031, FC15-C03-U043, FC15-C04-U007, FC15-C05-U030

For a finite Weyl group W with degrees d_i and exponents m_i=d_i−1, identify the distinguished coset representatives of W inside its affine Weyl group W_a with the coroot lattice; Bott’s loop-space cell decomposition supplies the Poincaré series of this quotient.

Theorem: Bott formula for affine-Weyl Poincaré series

FC15-C08-U031 · §8.9; pp. 179–180 · after FC15-C05-U031, FC15-C08-U030

If W has exponents m_1,…,m_n, then the affine Weyl group satisfies W_a(t)=W(t)/∏_{i=1}n(1−t{m_i}).

Examples/comparison: irreducible representations of dihedral and symmetric groups

FC15-C08-U032 · §8.10; p. 180 · after FC15-C01-U003, FC15-C01-U004

Over C, irreducible dihedral representations have degree 1 or 2. Irreducible S_n-representations are parametrized by partitions of n, as are conjugacy classes; all irreducible S_n-representations can be realized over Q.

Theorem/recorded result: integrality of Weyl-group characters

FC15-C08-U033 · §8.10; pp. 180–181 · after FC15-C02-U019

The source records that all irreducible characters of finite Weyl groups are integer-valued, in contrast with general noncrystallographic finite reflection groups.

Theorem: rational realizability of irreducible Weyl-group representations

FC15-C08-U034 · §8.10; p. 181 · after FC15-C08-U033

Every irreducible representation of a finite Weyl group can be realized over Q.

Comparison: Springer and Kazhdan–Lusztig constructions of Weyl-group representations

FC15-C08-U035 · §8.10; p. 181 · after FC15-C07-U043, FC15-C08-U034

The source compares Springer’s rational cohomological construction with Kazhdan–Lusztig cell representations; for symmetric groups the cell construction yields a canonical Q-basis for each irreducible representation.

Definition: Schur multiplier

FC15-C08-U036 · §8.11; pp. 181–182

The Schur multiplier is taken as H²(G,C^) of factor-set classes under pointwise multiplication. The source also records the homological convention H_2(G,Z) and the natural identification H²(G,C*)≅Hom(H_2(G,Z),C).

Construction: Coxeter-graph invariants for the Schur multiplier

FC15-C08-U037 · §8.11; p. 182 · after FC15-C02-U005

Let n_1 be the number of Coxeter-graph vertices; n_2 the number of finite-labelled edges with label at least 3; n_3 the number of equivalence classes of nonadjacent vertex pairs under the odd-edge relation; and n_4 the number of components after deleting even- or ∞-labelled edges.

Theorem: Howlett formula for the Schur multiplier

FC15-C08-U038 · §8.11; p. 182 · after FC15-C08-U036, FC15-C08-U037

For any Coxeter group W, its Schur multiplier is an elementary abelian 2-group of rank n_2+n_3+n_4−n_1.

Comparison/result: crystallographic Coxeter groups as Kac–Moody Weyl groups

FC15-C08-U039 · §8.12; pp. 182–183 · after FC15-C06-U014

The source records that the Weyl groups attached to Kac–Moody Lie algebras may be arbitrary crystallographic Coxeter groups, extending the finite and affine Weyl-group settings.

Comparison/result: Schubert geometry and Bruhat/Kazhdan–Lusztig data

FC15-C08-U040 · §8.12; p. 183 · after FC15-C05-U022, FC15-C07-U026

Schubert varieties are closures of Bruhat cells indexed by the Weyl group; cell-closure inclusion is governed by Bruhat order. The source highlights the Kazhdan–Lusztig connection between Schubert-variety geometry and representation theory as the geometric thread linking the chapter’s constructions.
