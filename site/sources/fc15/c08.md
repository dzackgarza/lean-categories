### Construction: Tits elementary simplifications for words {#fc15-c08-u002}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C08-U002` · §8.1; pp. 171–172 · after [`FC15-C05-U001`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u001), [`FC15-C05-U020`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u020)
:::

Let F be the free group on symbols in bijection with S and F^+ the positive word monoid mapping onto W. Elementary simplifications either apply one braid relation without changing word length or delete an adjacent equal pair; E(u) is the finite effectively computable set of words reachable from u by such simplifications.

### Theorem: Tits solution of the word problem {#fc15-c08-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C08-U003` · §8.1; p. 172 · after [`FC15-C08-U002`](#fc15-c08-u002)
:::

For positive words u,v, their images in W are equal iff E(u)∩E(v) is nonempty. In particular a word represents 1 iff its simplification set contains the empty word.

### Definition: the (−1)-condition and W-equivalence for parabolic subsets {#fc15-c08-u008}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C08-U008` · §8.3; p. 173 · after [`FC15-C05-U015`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u015), [`FC15-C05-U009`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u009), [`FC15-C01-U032`](c01.html#fc15-c01-u032)
:::

For I⊆S, say I satisfies the (−1)-condition if W_I is finite and its longest element w_I acts as −1 on the geometric subspace V_I. Let J be the family of such I; I,J∈J are W-equivalent when some w∈W carries their simple-root sets to one another.

### Theorem: Richardson classification of involutions {#fc15-c08-u009}

### Construction/result: Howlett matrix for a Coxeter element {#fc15-c08-u013}

In the geometric representation, let A be the Gram matrix of B and write 2A=U+U^t with U upper triangular unipotent according to the chosen ordering. The Coxeter element has matrix −U^{-1}U^t.

### Theorem: spectral characterization of infinite and affine Coxeter groups {#fc15-c08-u014}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C08-U014` · §8.4; p. 175 · after [`FC15-C06-U008`](c06.html#fc15-c06-u008), [`FC15-C06-U009`](c06.html#fc15-c06-u009), [`FC15-C08-U013`](#fc15-c08-u013)
:::

W is infinite iff its Coxeter element has infinite order, iff the geometric-representation matrix has a real eigenvalue >1. W is of affine type iff 1 is an eigenvalue and all other eigenvalues have absolute value 1.

### Definition/result: Möbius function and inversion on a locally finite poset {#fc15-c08-u015}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C08-U015` · §8.5; pp. 175–176
:::

For a poset in which every lower interval is finite, the Möbius function μ(x,y) is uniquely characterized by Σ_{x≤z≤y}μ(x,z)=δ_{x,y}; it gives the usual Möbius inversion formula for abelian-group-valued functions.

### Theorem: Möbius function of Bruhat order {#fc15-c08-u016}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C08-U016` · §8.5; p. 176 · after [`FC15-C05-U022`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u022), [`FC15-C08-U015`](#fc15-c08-u015)
:::

For every Coxeter group and x≤w in Bruhat order, μ(x,w)=(−1)^{ℓ(x)+ℓ(w)}.

### Corollary: Eulerian Bruhat intervals and rank-two diamonds {#fc15-c08-u017}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C08-U017` · §8.5; p. 176 · after [`FC15-C07-U035`](c07.html#fc15-c07-u035), [`FC15-C08-U016`](#fc15-c08-u016)
:::

Every nontrivial Bruhat interval has equally many even- and odd-length elements. In particular, if ℓ(w)−ℓ(x)=2 there are exactly two z with x<z<w.

### Theorem/recorded result: Möbius function on parabolic quotient representatives {#fc15-c08-u018}

::: {.unit-meta}

### Definition: Bruhat graph {#fc15-c08-u019}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C08-U019` · §8.6; p. 177 · after [`FC15-C05-U017`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u017), [`FC15-C05-U022`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u022)
:::

The Bruhat graph has vertex set W and a directed edge tw→w whenever t is a reflection and ℓ(tw)<ℓ(w), without requiring the length difference to be one.

### Construction/result: canonical increasing chain label in a Bruhat interval {#fc15-c08-u024}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C08-U024` · §8.7; pp. 177–178 · after [`FC15-C05-U020`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u020), [`FC15-C08-U023`](#fc15-c08-u023)

Fixing a reduced word for w labels each maximal chain in [x,w] by the successive omitted generator positions. There is a unique maximal chain with increasing label, and its label is lexicographically first among all maximal-chain labels.

Shellability recovers the Bruhat Möbius formula; passing to the order complex gives the standard topological formulation, and the associated Stanley–Reisner ring is Cohen–Macaulay.

### Definition: automorphism of the Bruhat poset {#fc15-c08-u026}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C08-U026` · §8.8; p. 178 · after [`FC15-C05-U022`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u022), [`FC15-C05-U029`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u029)
:::

A Bruhat-order automorphism is a bijection θ:W→W such that x≤w iff θ(x)≤θ(w). Such an automorphism fixes 1 and preserves length.

### Construction: inversion and Coxeter-graph automorphisms of Bruhat order {#fc15-c08-u027}

:::

The inversion map w↦w^{-1} is a Bruhat-poset automorphism. Every automorphism of the labelled Coxeter graph induces a group automorphism preserving Bruhat order.

### Construction: Coxeter-graph invariants for the Schur multiplier {#fc15-c08-u037}
