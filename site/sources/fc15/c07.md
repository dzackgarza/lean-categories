### Construction: generic-algebra parameter module {#fc15-c07-u002}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U002` · §7.1; pp. 145–146 · after [`FC15-C07-U001`](#fc15-c07-u001)
:::

Let A be a commutative ring with 1. Choose parameters a_s,b_s∈A constant on W-conjugacy classes of simple reflections, and let E be the free A-module with basis {T_w | w∈W}.

### Theorem: existence and uniqueness of the generic multiplication {#fc15-c07-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U003` · §7.1; pp. 145–147 · after [`FC15-C05-U005`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u005), [`FC15-C07-U002`](#fc15-c07-u002)
:::

There is a unique associative A-algebra structure on E with T_1=1 and T_sT_w=T_{sw} when ℓ(sw)>ℓ(w), while T_sT_w=a_sT_w+b_sT_{sw} when ℓ(sw)<ℓ(w).

### Definition: generic algebra {#fc15-c07-u004}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U004` · §7.1; p. 146 · after [`FC15-C07-U003`](#fc15-c07-u003)
:::

The algebra supplied by the preceding theorem is the generic algebra E_A(a_s,b_s) attached to (W,S) and the chosen parameters.

### Example: group algebra as a generic algebra {#fc15-c07-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U005` · §7.1; p. 146 · after [`FC15-C07-U004`](#fc15-c07-u004)
:::

Taking a_s=0 and b_s=1 for every s identifies the generic algebra with the group algebra A[W].

### Result: equivalent quadratic and right-handed multiplication relations {#fc15-c07-u006}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U006` · §7.1; pp. 146–147 · after [`FC15-C05-U005`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u005), [`FC15-C07-U003`](#fc15-c07-u003)
:::

The defining rules are equivalent to the length-increasing rule together with T_s²=a_sT_s+b_sT_1; the corresponding right-handed rules follow as well. For a reduced expression w=s_1⋯s_r, T_w=T_{s_1}⋯T_{s_r}.

### Construction: left and right generic multiplication operators {#fc15-c07-u007}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U007` · §7.1; p. 147 · after [`FC15-C07-U002`](#fc15-c07-u002), [`FC15-C07-U006`](#fc15-c07-u006)
:::

Define A-linear endomorphisms λ_s and ρ_t of the free module E by the left- and right-handed versions of the generic multiplication rules on each basis vector T_w.

### Lemma: four-length configuration lemma {#fc15-c07-u008}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U008` · §7.2; p. 148 · after [`FC15-C05-U020`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u020), [`FC15-C07-U001`](#fc15-c07-u001)
:::

For w∈W and s,t∈S, if ℓ(swt)=ℓ(w) and ℓ(sw)=ℓ(wt), then sw=wt, equivalently swt=w.

### Proposition: commutation of left and right generic operators {#fc15-c07-u009}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U009` · §7.2; pp. 148–149 · after [`FC15-C07-U007`](#fc15-c07-u007), [`FC15-C07-U008`](#fc15-c07-u008)
:::

For all s,t∈S, the endomorphisms λ_s and ρ_t commute on E.

### Construction/result: endomorphism realization of the generic algebra {#fc15-c07-u010}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U010` · §7.3; p. 149 · after [`FC15-C07-U003`](#fc15-c07-u003), [`FC15-C07-U009`](#fc15-c07-u009)
:::

Let C⊂End_A(E) be generated by the λ_s. Evaluation φ:C→E at T_1 is an A-module isomorphism; for reduced w=s_1⋯s_r the operator λ_w=λ_{s_1}⋯λ_{s_r} is independent of the reduced expression. Transporting multiplication along φ realizes the generic algebra of §7.1.

### Definition: Hecke algebra of a Coxeter system {#fc15-c07-u011}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U011` · §7.4; p. 150 · after [`FC15-C07-U004`](#fc15-c07-u004)
:::

Over A=Z[q,q^{-1}], set a_s=q−1 and b_s=q for every s. The resulting generic algebra is the Hecke algebra H of W, with standard A-basis {T_w}.

### Proposition/result: invertibility of the standard Hecke basis {#fc15-c07-u012}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U012` · §7.4; pp. 150–151 · after [`FC15-C07-U006`](#fc15-c07-u006), [`FC15-C07-U011`](#fc15-c07-u011)
:::

In H, T_s^{-1}=q^{-1}T_s−(1−q^{-1})T_1. Hence every T_w is invertible, by multiplying inverses along any reduced expression.

### Lemma: Bruhat comparison under a left descent {#fc15-c07-u013}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U013` · §7.4; p. 151 · after [`FC15-C05-U022`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u022), [`FC15-C05-U026`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u026)
:::

If sw<w and x<w, then sx<x implies sx<sw, while sx>x implies sx<w and x<sw. In either case sx<w.

### Notation: sign and q-length notation {#fc15-c07-u014}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U014` · §7.4; p. 151 · after [`FC15-C05-U005`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u005), [`FC15-C07-U011`](#fc15-c07-u011)
:::

Write ε_w=(−1)^{ℓ(w)} and q_w=q^{ℓ(w)}; extend R_{x,w} by zero when x is not ≤w.

### Proposition: R-polynomial expansion of inverses {#fc15-c07-u015}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U015` · §7.4; pp. 151–152 · after [`FC15-C07-U012`](#fc15-c07-u012), [`FC15-C07-U013`](#fc15-c07-u013), [`FC15-C07-U014`](#fc15-c07-u014)
:::

For each w, the inverse of T_{w^{-1}} has a triangular expansion in {T_x | x≤w} with coefficients ε_xR_{x,w}(q) and the standard ε_w q_w normalization. The polynomial R_{x,w} lies in Z[q], has degree ℓ(w)−ℓ(x), satisfies R_{w,w}=1, and is nonzero exactly when x≤w.

### Construction/result: recursive computation of R-polynomials {#fc15-c07-u016}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U016` · §7.5; pp. 152–153 · after [`FC15-C07-U013`](#fc15-c07-u013), [`FC15-C07-U015`](#fc15-c07-u015)
:::

If sw<w, then R_{x,w}=R_{sx,sw} when sx<x, while R_{x,w}=(q−1)R_{x,sw}+qR_{sx,sw} when x<sx; symmetric right-handed recurrences also hold.

### Examples: R-polynomials in Bruhat codimension one and two {#fc15-c07-u017}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U017` · §7.5; p. 153 · after [`FC15-C07-U016`](#fc15-c07-u016)
:::

If ℓ(w)−ℓ(x)=1 then R_{x,w}=q−1; if the difference is 2 then R_{x,w}=(q−1)².

### Definition: distinguished subexpressions and their statistics {#fc15-c07-u018}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U018` · §7.5; pp. 153–154 · after [`FC15-C05-U025`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u025), [`FC15-C05-U026`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u026)
:::

For a fixed reduced word w=s_1⋯s_r, encode a subexpression by σ=(σ_0,…,σ_r), where σ_0=1 and σ_j is σ_{j−1} or σ_{j−1}s_j. Define the selected-step and descent statistics n(σ),m(σ); a subexpression is distinguished when σ_j≤σ_{j−1}s_j at every step. D(x) denotes the distinguished subexpressions ending at x.

### Theorem/recorded result: Deodhar formula for R-polynomials {#fc15-c07-u019}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U019` · §7.5; p. 154 · after [`FC15-C07-U016`](#fc15-c07-u016), [`FC15-C07-U018`](#fc15-c07-u018)
:::

The source records Deodhar’s closed formula expressing R_{x,w} as a finite sum over distinguished subexpressions in D(x), with each summand a monomial q^{m}(q−1)^{n} determined by the two statistics.

### Proposition: finite-longest-element symmetry of R-polynomials {#fc15-c07-u020}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U020` · §7.6; pp. 154–155 · after [`FC15-C01-U032`](c01.html#fc15-c01-u032), [`FC15-C07-U016`](#fc15-c07-u016)
:::

If W is finite with longest element w_0, then R_{x,w}=R_{w_0w,w_0x} for all x≤w.

### Definition: bar involution on the Hecke algebra {#fc15-c07-u021}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U021` · §7.7; pp. 155–156 · after [`FC15-C07-U012`](#fc15-c07-u012)
:::

Let the involution of A send q to q^{-1}. Extend semilinearly to H by ar T_w=(T_{w^{-1}})^{-1}.

### Proposition/result: the bar map is an algebra involution {#fc15-c07-u022}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U022` · §7.7; pp. 155–156 · after [`FC15-C07-U021`](#fc15-c07-u021)
:::

The semilinear bar map is multiplicative and has order two, hence is a ring automorphism of H compatible with q↦q^{-1}.

### Proposition: bar-symmetry and inversion identities for R-polynomials {#fc15-c07-u023}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U023` · §7.8; pp. 156–157 · after [`FC15-C07-U015`](#fc15-c07-u015), [`FC15-C07-U021`](#fc15-c07-u021), [`FC15-C07-U022`](#fc15-c07-u022)
:::

The R-polynomials satisfy the bar-reciprocity relation obtained by q↦q^{-1}; the bar of each T_w has a triangular R-polynomial expansion; and the resulting upper-unitriangular R-matrix has the explicit signed R-polynomial inverse, giving the Kronecker-delta convolution identity of Proposition 7.8(c).

### Theorem: Kazhdan–Lusztig basis theorem {#fc15-c07-u025}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U025` · §7.9; pp. 158–159 · after [`FC15-C05-U022`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u022), [`FC15-C07-U024`](#fc15-c07-u024)
:::

For every w∈W there is a unique bar-invariant C_w with triangular expansion in the T_x, x≤w, whose coefficient polynomials P_{x,w}(q) satisfy P_{w,w}=1 and deg P_{x,w}≤(ℓ(w)−ℓ(x)−1)/2 for x<w.

::: {.unit-meta}

:::

The polynomials P_{x,w} in the canonical-basis theorem are the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials; by convention P_{x,w}=0 when x is not ≤w.

### Definition: top-degree relation x≺w and coefficient μ(x,w) {#fc15-c07-u029}

### Proposition: inverse Kazhdan–Lusztig matrix for finite W {#fc15-c07-u034}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U034` · §7.13; pp. 164–165 · after [`FC15-C01-U032`](c01.html#fc15-c01-u032), [`FC15-C07-U020`](#fc15-c07-u020), [`FC15-C07-U023`](#fc15-c07-u023), [`FC15-C07-U026`](#fc15-c07-u026)

If W is finite with longest element w_0, the triangular matrix (P_{x,w}) has inverse given by the signed matrix whose entries use P_{w_0w,w_0x}; equivalently the convolution in Proposition 7.13 is the Kronecker delta.

### Corollary: Eulerian alternating sum for Bruhat intervals {#fc15-c07-u035}

For x≤w, the signed sum over the Bruhat interval [x,w] is the Kronecker delta. Thus every nontrivial closed Bruhat interval contains equally many even- and odd-length elements.

### Definition: left and right descent sets {#fc15-c07-u038}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U038` · §7.15; pp. 167–168 · after [`FC15-C05-U005`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u005), [`FC15-C05-U022`](c05.html#fc15-c05-u022)

For w∈W, L(w)={s∈S | sw<w} and R(w)={s∈S | ws<w}.

### Definition: left and right cell preorders and cells {#fc15-c07-u039}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U039` · §7.15; p. 168 · after [`FC15-C07-U029`](#fc15-c07-u029), [`FC15-C07-U038`](#fc15-c07-u038)
:::

Let x—w mean x≺w or w≺x. Define x≤_Lw by a chain of —-adjacent elements whose left descent sets fail the indicated inclusion; mutual reachability defines left cells. Replacing L by R gives the right preorder and right cells.

### Definition: two-sided cell preorder and two-sided cells {#fc15-c07-u040}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U040` · §7.15; p. 168 · after [`FC15-C07-U039`](#fc15-c07-u039)
:::

Define ≤_{LR} by chains whose steps are left- or right-preorder steps; mutual reachability gives two-sided cells. Every two-sided cell is a union of left cells and of right cells.

### Example: cells of a dihedral Coxeter group {#fc15-c07-u041}

::: {.unit-meta}

:::

For a dihedral group, the source determines the left/right/two-sided cells explicitly: there are three left cells in the infinite case and four in the finite case, and apart from 1 (and w_0 when finite) all elements form one two-sided cell.

### Proposition: left-cell preorder refines right descent sets {#fc15-c07-u042}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U042` · §7.15; pp. 168–169 · after [`FC15-C07-U037`](#fc15-c07-u037), [`FC15-C07-U038`](#fc15-c07-u038), [`FC15-C07-U039`](#fc15-c07-u039)
:::

If x≤_Ly then R(x)⊇R(y); consequently elements in the same left cell have equal right descent sets.

### Construction: left cell modules {#fc15-c07-u043}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U043` · §7.15; pp. 169–170 · after [`FC15-C07-U036`](#fc15-c07-u036), [`FC15-C07-U039`](#fc15-c07-u039), [`FC15-C07-U042`](#fc15-c07-u042)
:::

For a left cell Z, the span of C_w for w∈Z together with all C_x below Z in ≤_L is a left ideal; the span of the strictly lower terms is a left ideal, and the quotient M_Z is a free A-module with basis indexed by Z, affording an H-representation. Right-cell and two-sided analogues give right modules and bimodules.

### Theorem/recorded result: affine Weyl cells and unipotent classes {#fc15-c07-u044}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC15-C07-U044` · §7.15; p. 170 · after [`FC15-C07-U040`](#fc15-c07-u040)
:::

The source records Lusztig’s results that affine Weyl groups have only finitely many one-sided (hence two-sided) cells and that their two-sided cells correspond bijectively to unipotent classes in an associated simple algebraic group.
