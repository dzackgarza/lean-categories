Proposition: Proposition 9.1

FC04-C09-U001 · § Discrete Valuation Rings and Dedekind Domains; p. 93; L3382 · after FC04-C07-U015, FC04-C08-U005, FC04-C04-U012, FC04-C04-U028, FC04-C01-U043, FC04-C01-U046 · in Mathlib

In a Noetherian domain of dimension 1, every nonzero ideal has a unique expression as a product of primary ideals with pairwise distinct radicals.

Formalized. Mathlib/RingTheory/Lasker.lean::Submodule.isLasker; IsLasker.exists_isMinimalPrimaryDecomposition; IsMinimalPrimaryDecomposition.comap_localized₀_eq_iInf; Mathlib/RingTheory/KrullDimension/Basic.lean::Ideal.IsPrime.isMaximal_of_ne_bot; Mathlib/RingTheory/Finiteness/Ideal.lean::Ideal.exists_radical_pow_le_of_fg; Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Operations.lean::Ideal.prod_eq_iInf_of_pairwise_isCoprime — For a nonzero ideal in a one-dimensional Noetherian domain, a minimal primary decomposition has nonzero prime radicals, hence distinct maximal radicals. Suitable powers of these radicals lie in the components, making distinct components coprime, so their intersection is their product. All associated primes are isolated, so the second uniqueness theorem makes each primary component canonical. The product/existence assembly was compile-checked against P.

Comparison: Prime-power primary ideals and ideal factorization

FC04-C09-U002 · § Discrete Valuation Rings and Dedekind Domains; p. 93; L3388 · after FC04-C09-U001, FC04-C03-U009, FC04-C03-U027

If every primary ideal in a Noetherian one-dimensional domain is a prime power, then nonzero ideals factor uniquely into prime ideals; localization at a nonzero prime has every nonzero ideal a power of its maximal ideal.

Definition: Discrete valuation

FC04-C09-U003 · § Discrete valuation rings; p. 94; L3393–L3395 · after FC04-C01-U018

A discrete valuation on a field K is a surjection v:K*→Z satisfying v(xy)=v(x)+v(y) and v(x+y)≥min(v(x),v(y)).

Construction / comparison: Valuation ring attached to a discrete valuation

FC04-C09-U004 · § Discrete valuation rings; p. 94; L3396 · after FC04-C09-U003, FC04-C05-U029

The valuation ring of v is {0}∪{x∈K*:v(x)≥0}; the source also permits the convention v(0)=+∞.

Examples: p-adic and f-adic discrete valuations

FC04-C09-U005 · § Discrete valuation rings; p. 94; L3397–L3399 · after FC04-C09-U003, FC04-C09-U004, FC04-C03-U009

For K=Q, v_p has valuation ring Z_(p); for K=k(x) and irreducible f∈k[x], v_f has valuation ring k[x]_(f).

Definition: Discrete valuation ring

FC04-C09-U006 · § Discrete valuation rings; p. 94; L3400 · after FC04-C09-U003, FC04-C09-U004, FC04-C01-U013, FC04-C03-U004, FC04-C05-U030

An integral domain A is a discrete valuation ring when it is the valuation ring of a discrete valuation of its fraction field K; then A is local and m={x∈K:v(x)>0}.

Construction / comparison: Ideal structure and a value-one generator in a DVR

FC04-C09-U007 · § Discrete valuation rings; p. 94; L3401–L3403 · after FC04-C09-U006, FC04-C01-U015, FC04-C01-U016, FC04-C01-U041, FC04-C06-U014, FC04-C08-U005

Equal valuation gives associated elements and the same principal ideal. Every nonzero ideal is m_k={y∈A:v(y)≥k}; choosing x with v(x)=1 gives m=(x), m_k=(x^k), and A is a Noetherian local domain of dimension 1 with m its only nonzero prime.

Proposition: Proposition 9.2

FC04-C09-U008 · § Discrete valuation rings; p. 94; L3404 · after FC04-C09-U006, FC04-C06-U014, FC04-C08-U005, FC04-C08-U011, FC04-C08-U008, FC04-C07-U018, FC04-C05-U020

For a Noetherian local domain A of dimension 1 with maximal ideal m and residue field k, the following are equivalent: A is a DVR; A is integrally closed; m is principal; dim_k(m/m²)=1; every nonzero ideal is a power of m; there is x∈A such that every nonzero ideal is (x^k), k≥0.

Theorem: Theorem 9.3

FC04-C09-U009 · § Dedekind domains; p. 95; L3436 · after FC04-C09-U008, FC04-C06-U014, FC04-C08-U005, FC04-C05-U020, FC04-C05-U022, FC04-C04-U001, FC04-C03-U009, FC04-C04-U023

For a Noetherian domain A of dimension 1, the following are equivalent: A is integrally closed; every primary ideal is a prime power; for every nonzero prime p, A_p is a DVR.

Definition: Dedekind domain

FC04-C09-U010 · § Dedekind domains; p. 95; L3448 · after FC04-C09-U009

A ring satisfying the equivalent conditions of Theorem 9.3 is called a Dedekind domain.

Corollary: Corollary 9.4

FC04-C09-U011 · § Dedekind domains; p. 95; L3450 · after FC04-C09-U010, FC04-C09-U001, FC04-C09-U009

Every nonzero ideal in a Dedekind domain factors uniquely as a product of prime ideals.

Example: Principal ideal domains are Dedekind

FC04-C09-U012 · § Dedekind domains; p. 95; L3454 · after FC04-C09-U010, FC04-C09-U008, FC04-C09-U009, FC04-C03-U009

Every principal ideal domain is Dedekind: it is Noetherian of dimension 1 and its nonzero-prime localizations are DVRs.

Definition / construction: Ring of integers of a number field

FC04-C09-U013 · § Dedekind domains; p. 95; L3456 · after FC04-C05-U006

For a finite algebraic extension K/Q, its ring of integers A is the integral closure of Z in K; for K=Q(i), A=Z[i].

Theorem: Theorem 9.5

FC04-C09-U014 · § Dedekind domains; p. 95; L3458 · after FC04-C09-U013, FC04-C09-U010, FC04-C05-U012, FC04-C05-U015, FC04-C05-U016, FC04-C05-U028

The ring of integers of an algebraic number field is a Dedekind domain.

Named comparison: Primary decomposition as a generalization of ideal factorization

FC04-C09-U015 · § Dedekind domains; p. 95; L3462 · after FC04-C09-U011, FC04-C04-U027, FC04-C04-U028

The source compares 9.4 with Chapter 4 uniqueness: prime powers are replaced by primary ideals, and products by intersections.

Definition / notation: Fractional, integral, and principal ideals; (A:M)

FC04-C09-U016 · § Fractional ideals; pp. 96–98; L3466 · after FC04-C01-U013, FC04-C03-U004, FC04-C02-U007, FC04-C01-U016

For a domain A with fraction field K, an A-submodule M⊆K is fractional if xM⊆A for some 0≠x∈A. Ordinary ideals are called integral ideals; (u)=Au is principal; (A:M)={x∈K:xM⊆A}.

Comparison: Finite generation of fractional ideals

FC04-C09-U017 · § Fractional ideals; pp. 96–98; L3468 · after FC04-C09-U016, FC04-C02-U022, FC04-C06-U014

Every finitely generated A-submodule of K is fractional; conversely, if A is Noetherian, every fractional ideal is finitely generated and has the form x⁻¹a for an integral ideal a.

Definition: Invertible ideal

FC04-C09-U018 · § Fractional ideals; pp. 96–98; L3470 · after FC04-C09-U016, FC04-C09-U017

An A-submodule M⊆K is invertible if MN=A for some N⊆K. The inverse is unique and equals (A:M); every invertible ideal is finitely generated and hence fractional.

Construction / comparison: Group of invertible fractional ideals

FC04-C09-U019 · § Fractional ideals; pp. 96–98; L3472 · after FC04-C09-U018, FC04-C01-U015

Every nonzero principal fractional ideal (u) is invertible with inverse (u⁻¹); the invertible ideals form a multiplicative group with identity A=(1).

Proposition: Proposition 9.6 (invertibility is local)

FC04-C09-U020 · § Fractional ideals; pp. 96–98; L3476 · after FC04-C09-U016, FC04-C09-U018, FC04-C03-U009, FC04-C03-U032

For a fractional ideal M, invertibility is equivalent to finite generation plus invertibility of every M_p, and equivalently to finite generation plus invertibility of every M_m for maximal m.

Proposition: Proposition 9.7

FC04-C09-U021 · § Fractional ideals; pp. 96–98; L3488 · after FC04-C09-U006, FC04-C09-U018, FC04-C09-U008, FC04-C02-U031

A local domain A is a DVR if and only if every nonzero fractional ideal of A is invertible.

Theorem: Theorem 9.8

FC04-C09-U022 · § Fractional ideals; pp. 96–98; L3496 · after FC04-C09-U010, FC04-C09-U018, FC04-C09-U020, FC04-C09-U021, FC04-C09-U009

An integral domain A is Dedekind if and only if every nonzero fractional ideal of A is invertible.

Corollary: Corollary 9.9

FC04-C09-U023 · § Fractional ideals; pp. 96–98; L3502 · after FC04-C09-U022

In a Dedekind domain, the nonzero fractional ideals form a group under multiplication.

Definition / comparison: Group of ideals I

FC04-C09-U024 · § Fractional ideals; pp. 96–98; L3504 · after FC04-C09-U023, FC04-C09-U011

For a Dedekind domain, the group of nonzero fractional ideals is denoted I; by 9.4 it is the free abelian group on the nonzero prime ideals.

Definition / construction: Principal ideals, ideal class group, and unit exact sequence

FC04-C09-U025 · § Fractional ideals; p. 96–98; L3506–L3510 · after FC04-C09-U024, FC04-C09-U016, FC04-C01-U015

The map φ:K→I, u↦(u), has image P (principal fractional ideals) and kernel U=A. The ideal class group is H=I/P, giving 1→U→K*→I→H→1.

Named classical comparison: Class number criterion for rings of integers

FC04-C09-U026 · § Fractional ideals; p. 96–98; L3512–L3515 · after FC04-C09-U025, FC04-C09-U014

For the ring of integers A of a number field, H is finite; h=|H| is the class number, and h=1 ⇔ I=P ⇔ A is a PID ⇔ A is a UFD.

Named classical comparison: Structure of the unit group of a number field

FC04-C09-U027 · § Fractional ideals; p. 96–98; L3516–L3518 · after FC04-C09-U025, FC04-C09-U014

For a number field, the torsion units form the finite cyclic group W of roots of unity and U/W is free abelian of rank r₁+r₂−1, where r₁+2r₂=[K:Q].

Example: Units of Z[i]

FC04-C09-U028 · § Fractional ideals; pp. 96–98; L3520 · after FC04-C09-U013, FC04-C09-U027

For K=Q(i), r₁=0,r₂=1 and the units of Z[i] are exactly ±1,±i.

Example: Units of Z[√2]

FC04-C09-U029 · § Fractional ideals; pp. 96–98; L3522 · after FC04-C09-U013, FC04-C09-U027

For K=Q(√2), r₁=2,r₂=0, W={±1}, U/W is infinite cyclic, and the units are ±(1+√2)^n for n∈Z.
