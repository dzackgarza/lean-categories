Construction / comparison: Homogeneous finite-generation setup

FC04-C11-U001 · § Hilbert functions; p. 116–118; L4151–L4153 · after FC04-C10-U029, FC04-C10-U024, FC04-C10-U026, FC04-C06-U014, FC04-C02-U022 · not formalized

For a Noetherian graded ring A, A₀ is Noetherian and A is generated over A₀ by finitely many homogeneous x_i of positive degrees k_i. A finitely generated graded A-module M has homogeneous generators, and each homogeneous component Mₙ is finitely generated over A₀.

Not formalized. partial: GradedRing.GradeZero.isNoetherianRing; homogeneous graded APIs — P proves that degree zero of a Noetherian graded ring is Noetherian and has homogeneous infrastructure, but the source bundle also requires finite homogeneous generation of the whole Noetherian graded ring over degree zero and homogeneous finite generators/component finiteness for every finite graded module. The missing graded Hilbert-basis converse is exactly the C10-U029 gap.

Definition: Poincaré series

FC04-C11-U002 · § Hilbert functions; p. 116–118; L4155–L4159 · after FC04-C11-U001, FC04-C02-U040 · in lean-categories

For an additive integer-valued function λ on finitely generated A₀-modules, the Poincaré series of M is P(M,t)=∑_{n≥0} λ(Mₙ)tⁿ ∈ Zt.

Formalized. LeanCategories/Algebra/DimensionTheory.lean::LeanCategories.Algebra.PoincareSeries — The project definition sends an invariant λ of modules to the power series with coefficient λ(M n) in degree n, exactly the source formula. Additivity of λ is a surrounding hypothesis, not part of the constructor. The file compiles at current HEAD.

Searched: repo HEAD

Theorem: Theorem 11.1 (Hilbert–Serre)

FC04-C11-U003 · § Hilbert functions; pp. 116–118; L4161 · after FC04-C11-U002, FC04-C11-U001, FC04-C02-U042 · not formalized

If A is generated over A₀ by homogeneous x_i of degrees k_i and M is finitely generated graded, then P(M,t)=f(t)/∏_{i=1}s(1−t{k_i}) for some f(t)∈Z[t].

Not formalized. partial: Mathlib/RingTheory/Polynomial/HilbertPoly.lean rational-series coefficient backend — P/H can manipulate a series already known to have denominator (1-X)^d, but contain no theorem deriving the Hilbert–Serre rational form from a finitely generated graded module. Current FLT explicitly records this as still missing.

Definition / notation: The invariant d(M)

FC04-C11-U004 · § Hilbert functions; pp. 116–118; L4185 · after FC04-C11-U002, FC04-C11-U003 · not formalized

d(M) denotes the order of the pole of P(M,t) at t=1; d(A) is defined similarly in this graded setting.

Not formalized. partial: Polynomial.hilbertPoly; power-series algebra — No checked project/P/H/external declaration defines the order of the pole at t=1 of a module Poincaré series as the source invariant d(M).

Corollary: Corollary 11.2

FC04-C11-U005 · § Hilbert functions; pp. 116–118; L4187 · after FC04-C11-U003, FC04-C11-U004 · not formalized

If all algebra generators have degree k_i=1, then for all sufficiently large n, λ(Mₙ) is a rational-coefficient polynomial in n of degree d(M)−1.

Not formalized. partial: Polynomial.coeff_mul_invOneSubPow_eq_hilbertPoly_eval — P proves eventual polynomial coefficients for an already supplied rational function p/(1-X)^d, but without U003 there is no theorem producing that rational form from the graded module and no source-shaped degree d(M)-1 conclusion.

Remark / example: Integer-valued polynomials need not have integral coefficients

FC04-C11-U006 · § Hilbert functions; pp. 116–118; L4203 · after FC04-C11-U005 · not formalized

A polynomial taking integer values on all integers need not have integer coefficients; the source gives x(x+1)/2.

Not formalized. generic rational/polynomial arithmetic only — The elementary example x(x+1)/2 is readily expressible in Lean, but no checked project/P/H/current external declaration owns the source example as a reusable formalized unit.

Definition / notation: Hilbert function (polynomial)

FC04-C11-U007 · § Hilbert functions; pp. 116–118; L4205 · after FC04-C11-U005 · not formalized

The eventual polynomial supplied by 11.2 is called the Hilbert function (or Hilbert polynomial) of M with respect to λ.

Not formalized. partial: project LeanCategories.Algebra.HilbertFunction; P Polynomial.hilbertPoly — The project HilbertFunction is the raw coefficient function n ↦ λ(M_n), while the source row names the eventual polynomial supplied by Corollary 11.2. No declaration identifies those notions under the missing eventual-polynomial theorem.

Definition / convention: Non-zero-divisor on a module

FC04-C11-U008 · § Hilbert functions; pp. 116–118; L4207 · after FC04-C02-U001, FC04-C01-U012 · in Mathlib

For x∈A and an A-module M, the source says x is not a zero-divisor on M when xm=0 with m∈M implies m=0.

Formalized. Mathlib/RingTheory/Regular/IsSMulRegular.lean::IsSMulRegular; isSMulRegular_iff_right_eq_zero_of_smul — IsSMulRegular M x is exactly injectivity of scalar multiplication by x, equivalently x • m = 0 → m = 0.

Proposition: Proposition 11.3

FC04-C11-U009 · § Hilbert functions; pp. 116–118; L4215 · after FC04-C11-U004, FC04-C11-U008 · not formalized

If x∈A_k is not a zero-divisor on M, then d(M/xM)=d(M)−1.

Convention / comparison: Length as the additive function over an Artin base

FC04-C11-U010 · § Hilbert functions; pp. 116–118; L4217 · after FC04-C06-U015, FC04-C06-U026, FC04-C06-U028 · in Mathlib

When A₀ is Artin (in particular, a field), the source specializes λ to module length l, which is additive.

Formalized. Mathlib/RingTheory/Length.lean::Module.length; Module.length_ne_top; Module.length_eq_add_of_exact — Module length is finite under the Artinian/Noetherian hypotheses and is additive in short exact sequences, exactly the source specialization of λ to length over an Artin base.

Searched: P

Example: Poincaré series of a polynomial ring over an Artin ring

FC04-C11-U011 · § Hilbert functions; pp. 116–118; L4219 · after FC04-C11-U002, FC04-C11-U010, FC04-C10-U025 · not formalized

For A=A₀[x₁,…,x_s] with A₀ Artin and x_i independent of degree 1, Aₙ is free on the degree-n monomials and P(A,t)=(1−t)^{-s}.

Not formalized. partial: monomial bases; project PoincareSeries; power-series identities — P/H have the degree-monomial combinatorics and formal series algebra, but no checked whole-row theorem identifies the length Poincaré series of the standard polynomial ring with (1-t)^{-s}.

Proposition: Proposition 11.4

FC04-C11-U012 · § Hilbert functions; pp. 116–118; L4223 · after FC04-C06-U014, FC04-C01-U028, FC04-C04-U004, FC04-C02-U022, FC04-C10-U022, FC04-C10-U055, FC04-C08-U006, FC04-C06-U026, FC04-C11-U005 · not formalized

Let (A,m) be Noetherian local, q m-primary, M finitely generated, and (Mₙ) a stable q-filtration. Then each M/Mₙ has finite length; for large n this length is a polynomial g(n) of degree ≤s, where s is the least number of generators of q; and the degree and leading coefficient depend only on M and q, not the stable filtration.

Not formalized. partial: stable filtrations, module length, Polynomial.hilbertPoly backend — No project/P/H/current external theorem proves eventual polynomiality of length(M/M_n) for stable primary filtrations together with the generator bound and filtration-independence of degree/leading coefficient. Imperial FLT intentionally replaces this polynomial theorem by growth order.

Definition / notation: χq^M and the characteristic polynomial χq

FC04-C11-U013 · § Hilbert functions; p. 116–118; L4243–L4249 · after FC04-C11-U012 · not formalized

For the q-adic filtration, χ_q^M(n)=l(M/qⁿM) for all large n. For M=A write χ_q(n)=χ_q^A(n), called the characteristic polynomial of the m-primary ideal q.

Not formalized. partial: adic powers and module length — The source definition depends on the missing eventual polynomial in U012; no checked declaration packages χ_q^M as that eventual Hilbert–Samuel polynomial.

Corollary: Corollary 11.5

FC04-C11-U014 · § Hilbert functions; pp. 116–118; L4251 · after FC04-C11-U013, FC04-C11-U012 · not formalized

For all large n, l(A/qⁿ)=χ_q(n), a polynomial of degree at most the least number s of generators of q.

Not formalized. partial: U012 ingredients — P/H do not prove that length(A/q^n) eventually agrees with a polynomial of degree bounded by the least number of generators of q.

Proposition: Proposition 11.6

FC04-C11-U015 · § Hilbert functions; pp. 116–118; L4255 · after FC04-C11-U013, FC04-C07-U018 · not formalized

For a Noetherian local ring (A,m) and any m-primary q, deg χ_q = deg χ_m.

Not formalized. partial: primary ideals, cofinal adic filtrations — Cofinality and primary-ideal infrastructure exist, but the source conclusion compares degrees of Hilbert–Samuel characteristic polynomials, which are not formalized.

Definition / notation: The local invariant d(A)

FC04-C11-U016 · § Hilbert functions; pp. 116–118; L4269 · after FC04-C11-U015, FC04-C11-U004, FC04-C10-U053 · not formalized

For a Noetherian local ring A, d(A) is the common degree of the characteristic polynomials χ_q; equivalently d(A)=d(G_m(A)), the pole order at t=1 for the associated graded ring.

Not formalized. partial: associated graded/open PR infrastructure; Polynomial.hilbertPoly — Neither the common degree of the characteristic polynomials nor its equality with the associated-graded Poincaré pole order is formalized as the source invariant d(A).

Definition / notation: The invariant δ(A)
