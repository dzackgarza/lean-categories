### Definition / comparison: Topological abelian group and zero-neighborhood control {#fc04-c10-u001}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C10-U001` · § Topologies and completions; pp. 101–104; L3585 · [`mathlib`]{.route-mathlib}
:::

A topological abelian group has continuous addition and inversion. If {0} is closed then the group is Hausdorff; translations are homeomorphisms, so the topology is determined by the neighborhoods of 0.

::: {.unit-lean}
**In Lean.** [`Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/Group/Basic.lean::IsTopologicalAddGroup`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/Group/Basic.html); [`Homeomorph.addLeft`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/find/?pattern=Homeomorph.addLeft#doc); `map_add_left_nhds_zero`; [`Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/Group/Pointwise.lean::IsTopologicalAddGroup.t2Space_iff_zero_closed`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/Group/Pointwise.html#IsTopologicalAddGroup.t2Space_iff_zero_closed) — Addition/inversion continuity is the bundled topological-add-group structure. Translation is a homeomorphism, zero-neighborhoods determine all neighborhoods, and in a topological additive group closed `{0}` is equivalent to Hausdorffness.
:::

### Definition / construction: Cauchy sequences and completion of a topological abelian group {#fc04-c10-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C10-U003` · § Topologies and completions; p. 101–104; L3603–L3609 · after [`FC04-C10-U001`](#fc04-c10-u001) · [`mathlib`]{.route-mathlib}
:::

Assuming 0 has a countable fundamental system of neighborhoods, a sequence is Cauchy if its pairwise differences eventually lie in every zero-neighborhood. Equivalent Cauchy sequences differ by a sequence tending to 0; their classes form the completion Ĝ, with canonical homomorphism φ:G→Ĝ given by constant sequences.

::: {.unit-lean}
**In Lean.** [`Mathlib/Topology/UniformSpace/Completion.lean::UniformSpace.Completion`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/UniformSpace/Completion.html#UniformSpace.Completion); [`Completion.denseRange_coe`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/find/?pattern=Completion.denseRange_coe#doc); [`Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/GroupCompletion.lean::UniformSpace.Completion.toCompl`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/GroupCompletion.html#UniformSpace.Completion.toCompl); [`Mathlib/Topology/UniformSpace/Cauchy.lean::CauchySeq`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/UniformSpace/Cauchy.html) — Mathlib constructs the same Hausdorff completion by Cauchy filters modulo inseparability, without the source's countable-basis restriction. `CauchySeq` supplies the sequence formulation when desired, and `toCompl` is the canonical additive homomorphism.

### Construction / example: Topologies defined by descending subgroups {#fc04-c10-u006}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C10-U006` · § Topologies and completions; p. 101–104; L3621–L3627 · after [`FC04-C10-U001`](#fc04-c10-u001) · [`mathlib`]{.route-mathlib}

A descending sequence G=G₀⊇G₁⊇⋯ defines a topology by declaring U a zero-neighborhood iff U contains some Gₙ. Each Gₙ is open and closed. The standard example is the p-adic topology on Z with Gₙ=pⁿZ.
