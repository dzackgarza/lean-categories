Definition: Primary ideal

FC04-C04-U001 · Primary Decomposition; pp. 50–54; L1986–1992 · after FC04-C01-U006, FC04-C01-U012, FC04-C01-U014, FC04-C01-U007 · in Mathlib

A proper ideal 𝔮 such that xy∈𝔮 implies x∈𝔮 or y^n∈𝔮 for some n>0; equivalently A/𝔮≠0 and every zero-divisor of A/𝔮 is nilpotent.

Formalized. Ideal.IsPrimary; Ideal.isPrimary_iff; Submodule.isPrimary_iff_zero_divisor_quotient_imp_nilpotent_smul — The product criterion is the source definition; the quotient theorem gives the equivalent nonzero-quotient/nilpotent-zero-divisor characterization.

Comparison: Prime ideals and contractions of primary ideals

FC04-C04-U002 · Primary Decomposition; pp. 50–54; L1994 · after FC04-C01-U020, FC04-C01-U056, FC04-C04-U001 · in Mathlib

Every prime ideal is primary; the contraction of a primary ideal along a ring homomorphism is primary.

Formalized. Ideal.IsPrime.isPrimary; Ideal.IsPrimary.comap — Every prime is primary, and contraction of a primary ideal along a ring homomorphism is primary.

Proposition 4.1: Radical of a primary ideal is prime

FC04-C04-U003 · Primary Decomposition; pp. 50–54; L1996–1998 · after FC04-C01-U050, FC04-C01-U051, FC04-C04-U001 · in Mathlib

If 𝔮 is primary then r(𝔮) is the smallest prime ideal containing 𝔮.

Formalized. Ideal.isPrime_radical; Ideal.IsPrime.radical_le_iff — The radical of a primary ideal is prime and is the least prime containing it.

Definition: 𝔭-primary ideal

FC04-C04-U004 · Primary Decomposition; pp. 50–54; L2000 · after FC04-C04-U003 · in Mathlib

A primary ideal 𝔮 with r(𝔮)=𝔭 is called 𝔭-primary.

Formalized. Ideal.IsPrimary; Ideal.radical — “q is p-primary” is exactly q.IsPrimary ∧ q.radical = p.

Proposition 4.2: Maximal radical implies primary

FC04-C04-U008 · Primary Decomposition; pp. 50–54; L2008–2010 · after FC04-C01-U021, FC04-C01-U050, FC04-C04-U001 · in Mathlib

If r(𝔞) is maximal then 𝔞 is primary; hence powers of a maximal ideal are primary.

Formalized. Ideal.isPrimary_of_isMaximal_radical; Ideal.radical_pow — Maximal radical implies primary; positive powers of a maximal ideal have that maximal radical and are primary.

Lemma 4.3: Finite intersections of 𝔭-primary ideals

FC04-C04-U009 · Primary Decomposition; pp. 50–54; L2014 · after FC04-C01-U040, FC04-C04-U004 · in Mathlib

A finite intersection of 𝔭-primary ideals is 𝔭-primary.

Formalized. Ideal.isPrimary_finsetInf; Ideal.radical_finset_inf — A finite nonempty intersection of primary ideals with common radical is primary with the same radical.

Lemma 4.4: Colon of a primary ideal

FC04-C04-U010 · Primary Decomposition; pp. 50–54; L2016–2026 · after FC04-C01-U048, FC04-C04-U004 · not formalized

For 𝔮 𝔭-primary: x∈𝔮 gives (𝔮:x)=(1); x∉𝔮 gives (𝔮:x) 𝔭-primary; x∉𝔭 gives (𝔮:x)=𝔮.

Definition: Minimal primary decomposition

FC04-C04-U012 · Primary Decomposition; pp. 50–54; L2034 · after FC04-C04-U011 · in Mathlib

A primary decomposition with distinct radicals and no component containing the intersection of all the others; synonyms: irredundant, reduced, normal.

Formalized. Submodule.IsMinimalPrimaryDecomposition / alias Ideal.IsMinimalPrimaryDecomposition — Fields encode intersection equality, primary components, distinct radicals, and irredundancy.

Definition: Decomposable ideal

FC04-C04-U013 · Primary Decomposition; pp. 50–54; L2034 · after FC04-C04-U011, FC04-C04-U012 · in Mathlib

An ideal admitting a primary decomposition; every primary decomposition can be reduced to a minimal one.

Formalized. IsLasker; Submodule.exists_minimal_isPrimary_decomposition_of_isPrimary_decomposition — Decomposability is existence of a finite primary decomposition, and every such decomposition reduces to a minimal one.

Theorem 4.5 (1st uniqueness theorem): Associated primes are intrinsic

FC04-C04-U014 · Primary Decomposition; pp. 50–54; L2036–2038 · after FC04-C01-U048, FC04-C01-U050, FC04-C01-U047, FC04-C04-U010, FC04-C04-U012, FC04-C04-U013 · in Mathlib

For a minimal decomposition 𝔞=⋂𝔮_i with 𝔭_i=r(𝔮_i), the 𝔭_i are exactly the prime ideals occurring among r(𝔞:x); hence independent of the decomposition.

Formalized. Submodule.IsMinimalPrimaryDecomposition.image_radical_eq_associated_primes — Explicitly documented as Atiyah–Macdonald Theorem 4.5; component radicals equal the intrinsic associated primes/colon radicals.

Reusable example: A decomposition with embedded prime

FC04-C04-U015 · Primary Decomposition; pp. 50–54; L2044 · after FC04-C04-U008, FC04-C04-U014 · not formalized

Not formalized. partial: primary-decomposition/associated-prime APIs — No checked declaration proves (x²,xy)=(x)∩(x,y)² with the displayed associated primes and non-primaryity.

Proposition 4.6: Minimal associated primes are minimal over 𝔞

FC04-C04-U019 · Primary Decomposition; pp. 50–54; L2048–2050 · after FC04-C01-U047, FC04-C04-U013, FC04-C04-U017 · in Mathlib

Every prime 𝔭⊇𝔞 contains a minimal prime belonging to 𝔞; the minimal associated primes are exactly the minimal primes containing 𝔞.

Formalized. Ideal.exists_minimalPrimes_le; Ideal.IsMinimalPrimaryDecomposition.minimalPrimes_subset_image_radical; image_radical_eq_associated_primes — Every prime over the ideal contains a minimal associated prime; minimal associated primes are exactly minimal primes over the ideal.

Reusable comparison: Geometric meaning of isolated and embedded primes

FC04-C04-U020 · Primary Decomposition; pp. 50–54; L2052 · after FC04-C04-U017, FC04-C04-U018 · in Mathlib

For polynomial rings, minimal primes correspond to irreducible components, whereas embedded primes correspond to subvarieties inside those components.

Formalized. Ideal.minimalPrimes.equivIrreducibleComponents; PrimeSpectrum.zeroLocusEquivIrreducibleCloseds — Minimal primes correspond to irreducible components of V(I); the order-reversing prime/irreducible-closed correspondence places nonminimal associated primes inside components.

Not formalized. partial: Lasker uniqueness APIs — No checked declaration proves the two displayed decompositions of (x²,xy) with distinct embedded primary components.

Proposition 4.7: Zero-divisors from associated primes

FC04-C04-U022 · Primary Decomposition; pp. 50–54; L2056–2074 · after FC04-C01-U012, FC04-C01-U035, FC04-C01-U048, FC04-C04-U014, FC04-C04-U016 · not formalized

For decomposable 𝔞, ⋃𝔭_i={x:(𝔞:x)≠𝔞}; if 0 is decomposable, the zero-divisors are the union of primes belonging to 0 and nilpotents are the intersection of its minimal primes.

Not formalized. partial: Module.biUnion_associatedPrimes_eq_zero_divisors (Noetherian); minimal-prime/nilradical APIs — The closest zero-divisor union theorem assumes Noetherianity, while the source assumes only decomposability; the complete weaker-hypothesis bundle is absent.

Proposition 4.8: Localization of a primary ideal

FC04-C04-U023 · Primary Decomposition; pp. 50–54; L2078–2086 · after FC04-C03-U027, FC04-C04-U004 · not formalized

For 𝔮 𝔭-primary: if S meets 𝔭 then S⁻¹𝔮=S⁻¹A; if S∩𝔭=∅ then S⁻¹𝔮 is S⁻¹𝔭-primary and contracts back to 𝔮.

Not formalized. partial: IsLocalization.map_eq_top_of_not_subset; IsLocalization.under_map_of_isPrimary_disjoint — P/M prove discard and contraction-back clauses but not that the surviving extended ideal is primary with localized radical.

Proposition 4.9: Localization of a primary decomposition
