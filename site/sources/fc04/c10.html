Definition / comparison: Topological abelian group and zero-neighborhood control

FC04-C10-U001 · § Topologies and completions; pp. 101–104; L3585 · in Mathlib

A topological abelian group has continuous addition and inversion. If {0} is closed then the group is Hausdorff; translations are homeomorphisms, so the topology is determined by the neighborhoods of 0.

Formalized. Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/Group/Basic.lean::IsTopologicalAddGroup; Homeomorph.addLeft; map_add_left_nhds_zero; Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/Group/Pointwise.lean::IsTopologicalAddGroup.t2Space_iff_zero_closed — Addition/inversion continuity is the bundled topological-add-group structure. Translation is a homeomorphism, zero-neighborhoods determine all neighborhoods, and in a topological additive group closed {0} is equivalent to Hausdorffness.

Lemma: Lemma 10.1

FC04-C10-U002 · § Topologies and completions; pp. 101–104; L3587 · after FC04-C10-U001 · in Mathlib

If H is the intersection of all neighborhoods of 0 in a topological abelian group G, then H is a subgroup, H=closure({0}), G/H is Hausdorff, and G is Hausdorff iff H=0.

Formalized. Mathlib/Topology/Separation/Basic.lean::R0Space.closure_singleton; Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/FilterBasis.lean::AddGroupFilterBasis.t2Space_iff; Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/Group/Quotient.lean::QuotientAddGroup.instT3Space — In a topological additive group the neighborhood-kernel at zero is the closure of {0} and is an additive subgroup. Quotienting by that closed subgroup is Hausdorff; the filter-basis criterion identifies Hausdorffness with trivial intersection.

Definition / construction: Cauchy sequences and completion of a topological abelian group

FC04-C10-U003 · § Topologies and completions; p. 101–104; L3603–L3609 · after FC04-C10-U001 · in Mathlib

Assuming 0 has a countable fundamental system of neighborhoods, a sequence is Cauchy if its pairwise differences eventually lie in every zero-neighborhood. Equivalent Cauchy sequences differ by a sequence tending to 0; their classes form the completion Ĝ, with canonical homomorphism φ:G→Ĝ given by constant sequences.

Formalized. Mathlib/Topology/UniformSpace/Completion.lean::UniformSpace.Completion; Completion.denseRange_coe; Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/GroupCompletion.lean::UniformSpace.Completion.toCompl; Mathlib/Topology/UniformSpace/Cauchy.lean::CauchySeq — Mathlib constructs the same Hausdorff completion by Cauchy filters modulo inseparability, without the source’s countable-basis restriction. CauchySeq supplies the sequence formulation when desired, and toCompl is the canonical additive homomorphism.

Comparison: Kernel and injectivity of the completion map

FC04-C10-U004 · § Topologies and completions; p. 101–104; L3611–L3615 · after FC04-C10-U002, FC04-C10-U003 · in Mathlib

For the canonical map φ:G→Ĝ, ker φ is the intersection of all neighborhoods of 0; hence φ is injective exactly when G is Hausdorff.

Formalized. UniformSpace.Completion.isUniformInducing_coe; SeparationQuotient.mk_eq_mk; R0Space.closure_singleton; UniformSpace.Completion.coe_injective; IsTopologicalAddGroup.t2Space_iff_zero_closed — Equality in the separated completion is exactly source inseparability pulled back along the uniform inducing map. For a topological additive group the inseparability class of zero is closure {0}, i.e. the intersection of its neighborhoods; therefore the completion map is injective exactly in the Hausdorff case.

Construction / convention: Functoriality of completion

FC04-C10-U005 · § Topologies and completions; p. 101–104; L3617–L3619 · after FC04-C10-U003 · in Mathlib

A continuous homomorphism f:G→H sends Cauchy sequences to Cauchy sequences and induces a continuous homomorphism f̂:Ĝ→Ĥ; completion respects composition: (g∘f)^=ĝ∘f̂.

Formalized. Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/GroupCompletion.lean::AddMonoidHom.completion; AddMonoidHom.continuous_completion; Mathlib/Topology/UniformSpace/Completion.lean::UniformSpace.Completion.map_comp — A continuous additive homomorphism is uniformly continuous for the canonical group uniformities, extends to completions, and the underlying completion maps satisfy the source composition law.

Construction / example: Topologies defined by descending subgroups

FC04-C10-U006 · § Topologies and completions; p. 101–104; L3621–L3627 · after FC04-C10-U001 · in Mathlib

A descending sequence G=G₀⊇G₁⊇⋯ defines a topology by declaring U a zero-neighborhood iff U contains some Gₙ. Each Gₙ is open and closed. The standard example is the p-adic topology on Z with Gₙ=pⁿZ.

Formalized. Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/FilterBasis.lean::AddGroupFilterBasis; AddGroupFilterBasis.hasBasis; open-subgroup/closed-subgroup API; Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/Nonarchimedean/AdicTopology.lean::Ideal.hasBasis_nhds_zero_adic — A descending family of additive subgroups is a zero-neighborhood basis and generates the source topology; basis subgroups are open and hence closed. The principal-ideal adic basis specializes this to the standard p-adic topology on ℤ.

Construction / comparison: Completion as coherent residue classes

FC04-C10-U007 · § Topologies and completions; p. 101–104; L3629–L3641 · after FC04-C10-U003, FC04-C10-U006 · not formalized

For a subgroup topology, a Cauchy sequence determines compatible classes ξₙ∈G/Gₙ, and every coherent family arises this way. Thus Ĝ can equivalently be defined as the group of coherent sequences of residue classes.

Not formalized. partial: UniformSpace.Completion; quotient groups; categorical limits — P/H have both abstract completion and quotient/inverse-limit infrastructure, but no checked bridge identifies completion for an arbitrary descending subgroup topology with coherent residue classes G/G_n, including surjectivity from every coherent family. Definition / construction: Inverse system and inverse limit

FC04-C10-U008 · § Topologies and completions; p. 101–104; L3643–L3653 · after FC04-C10-U007 · not formalized

A sequence of groups Aₙ with transition maps θₙ₊₁:Aₙ₊₁→Aₙ is an inverse system. Its inverse limit lim←Aₙ is the group of coherent sequences (aₙ) with θₙ₊₁(aₙ₊₁)=aₙ. In particular Ĝ≅lim←G/Gₙ.

Not formalized. partial: Mathlib/Order/DirectedInverseSystem.lean::InverseSystem; CategoryTheory.Limits.limit; U007 infrastructure — Inverse systems and categorical inverse limits are formalized, but the row also asserts the specific canonical isomorphism Ĝ ≃ lim G/G_n; that missing bridge prevents strict whole-bundle credit.

Comparison / convention: Topology versus a chosen inverse system

FC04-C10-U009 · § Topologies and completions; pp. 101–104; L3655 · after FC04-C10-U008, FC04-C10-U006 · not formalized

Different descending subgroup sequences can define the same topology and hence the same completion; the topological formulation packages this choice-independence, whereas a single inverse-system presentation depends on the chosen subgroups.

Not formalized. partial: topological completion; inverse-system APIs — P/H have topology and limit interfaces separately, but no checked theorem proves that cofinal/different descending subgroup presentations defining the same topology yield the source completion comparison.

Definition: Surjective inverse system

FC04-C10-U010 · § Topologies and completions; pp. 101–104; L3657 · after FC04-C10-U008 · in Mathlib

An inverse system is called surjective when every transition homomorphism θₙ₊₁ is surjective.

Formalized. Mathlib/Order/DirectedInverseSystem.lean::InverseSystem; Function.Surjective — Mathlib’s transition-law structure together with pointwise Function.Surjective is exactly the source notion that every transition homomorphism is surjective.

Definition / construction: Exact sequence of inverse systems

FC04-C10-U011 · § Topologies and completions; p. 101–104; L3657–L3669 · after FC04-C10-U008, FC04-C02-U036 · in Mathlib

Compatible short exact sequences 0→Aₙ→Bₙ→Cₙ→0 arranged in commuting transition diagrams form an exact sequence of inverse systems and induce maps between their inverse limits.

Formalized. Mathlib/Algebra/Homology/ShortComplex/FunctorEquivalence.lean::ShortComplex.functorEquivalence; functor-category abelian/exactness API; CategoryTheory.Limits.lim.map — A compatible family of short exact sequences is equivalently a functor into short complexes, with exactness checked pointwise. A natural transformation induces the source maps on inverse limits through lim.map.

Proposition: Proposition 10.2

FC04-C10-U012 · § Topologies and completions; pp. 101–104; L3671 · after FC04-C10-U010, FC04-C10-U011 · in another Lean project

For an exact sequence 0→{Aₙ}→{Bₙ}→{Cₙ}→0 of inverse systems, 0→lim←Aₙ→lim←Bₙ→lim←Cₙ is always exact. If {Aₙ} is surjective, the map lim←Bₙ→lim←Cₙ is also surjective, giving a short exact sequence.
