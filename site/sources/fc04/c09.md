### Proposition: Proposition 9.1 {#fc04-c09-u001}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C09-U001` · § Discrete Valuation Rings and Dedekind Domains; p. 93; L3382 · after [`FC04-C07-U015`](c07.html#fc04-c07-u015), [`FC04-C08-U005`](c08.html#fc04-c08-u005), [`FC04-C04-U012`](c04.html#fc04-c04-u012), [`FC04-C04-U028`](c04.html#fc04-c04-u028), [`FC04-C01-U043`](c01.html#fc04-c01-u043), [`FC04-C01-U046`](c01.html#fc04-c01-u046) · [in Mathlib]{.route-mathlib}
:::

In a Noetherian domain of dimension 1, every nonzero ideal has a unique expression as a product of primary ideals with pairwise distinct radicals.

::: {.unit-lean}
**Formalized.** [`Mathlib/RingTheory/Lasker.lean::Submodule.isLasker`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Lasker.html#Submodule.isLasker); [`IsLasker.exists_isMinimalPrimaryDecomposition`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/find/?pattern=IsLasker.exists_isMinimalPrimaryDecomposition#doc); `IsMinimalPrimaryDecomposition.comap_localized₀_eq_iInf`; [`Mathlib/RingTheory/KrullDimension/Basic.lean::Ideal.IsPrime.isMaximal_of_ne_bot`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/KrullDimension/Basic.html#Ideal.IsPrime.isMaximal_of_ne_bot); [`Mathlib/RingTheory/Finiteness/Ideal.lean::Ideal.exists_radical_pow_le_of_fg`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Finiteness/Ideal.html#Ideal.exists_radical_pow_le_of_fg); [`Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Operations.lean::Ideal.prod_eq_iInf_of_pairwise_isCoprime`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Operations.html#Ideal.prod_eq_iInf_of_pairwise_isCoprime) — For a nonzero ideal in a one-dimensional Noetherian domain, a minimal primary decomposition has nonzero prime radicals, hence distinct maximal radicals. Suitable powers of these radicals lie in the components, making distinct components coprime, so their intersection is their product. All associated primes are isolated, so the second uniqueness theorem makes each primary component canonical. The product/existence assembly was compile-checked against P.
:::

### Comparison: Prime-power primary ideals and ideal factorization {#fc04-c09-u002}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C09-U002` · § Discrete Valuation Rings and Dedekind Domains; p. 93; L3388 · after [`FC04-C09-U001`](#fc04-c09-u001), [`FC04-C03-U009`](c03.html#fc04-c03-u009), [`FC04-C03-U027`](c03.html#fc04-c03-u027)
:::

If every primary ideal in a Noetherian one-dimensional domain is a prime power, then nonzero ideals factor uniquely into prime ideals; localization at a nonzero prime has every nonzero ideal a power of its maximal ideal.

### Definition: Discrete valuation {#fc04-c09-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C09-U003` · § Discrete valuation rings; p. 94; L3393–L3395 · after [`FC04-C01-U018`](c01.html#fc04-c01-u018)
:::

A discrete valuation on a field K is a surjection v:K*→Z satisfying v(xy)=v(x)+v(y) and v(x+y)≥min(v(x),v(y)).

### Construction / comparison: Valuation ring attached to a discrete valuation {#fc04-c09-u004}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C09-U004` · § Discrete valuation rings; p. 94; L3396 · after [`FC04-C09-U003`](#fc04-c09-u003), [`FC04-C05-U029`](c05.html#fc04-c05-u029)
:::

The valuation ring of v is {0}∪{x∈K*:v(x)≥0}; the source also permits the convention v(0)=+∞.

### Examples: p-adic and f-adic discrete valuations {#fc04-c09-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C09-U005` · § Discrete valuation rings; p. 94; L3397–L3399 · after [`FC04-C09-U003`](#fc04-c09-u003), [`FC04-C09-U004`](#fc04-c09-u004), [`FC04-C03-U009`](c03.html#fc04-c03-u009)
:::

For K=Q, v_p has valuation ring Z_(p); for K=k(x) and irreducible f∈k[x], v_f has valuation ring k[x]_(f).

### Definition: Discrete valuation ring {#fc04-c09-u006}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C09-U006` · § Discrete valuation rings; p. 94; L3400 · after [`FC04-C09-U003`](#fc04-c09-u003), [`FC04-C09-U004`](#fc04-c09-u004), [`FC04-C01-U013`](c01.html#fc04-c01-u013), [`FC04-C03-U004`](c03.html#fc04-c03-u004), [`FC04-C05-U030`](c05.html#fc04-c05-u030)
:::

An integral domain A is a discrete valuation ring when it is the valuation ring of a discrete valuation of its fraction field K; then A is local and m={x∈K:v(x)>0}.

### Construction / comparison: Ideal structure and a value-one generator in a DVR {#fc04-c09-u007}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C09-U007` · § Discrete valuation rings; p. 94; L3401–L3403 · after [`FC04-C09-U006`](#fc04-c09-u006), [`FC04-C01-U015`](c01.html#fc04-c01-u015), [`FC04-C01-U016`](c01.html#fc04-c01-u016), [`FC04-C01-U041`](c01.html#fc04-c01-u041), [`FC04-C06-U014`](c06.html#fc04-c06-u014), [`FC04-C08-U005`](c08.html#fc04-c08-u005)
:::

Equal valuation gives associated elements and the same principal ideal. Every nonzero ideal is m_k={y∈A:v(y)≥k}; choosing x with v(x)=1 gives m=(x), m_k=(x^k), and A is a Noetherian local domain of dimension 1 with m its only nonzero prime.

### Proposition: Proposition 9.2 {#fc04-c09-u008}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C09-U008` · § Discrete valuation rings; p. 94; L3404 · after [`FC04-C09-U006`](#fc04-c09-u006), [`FC04-C06-U014`](c06.html#fc04-c06-u014), [`FC04-C08-U005`](c08.html#fc04-c08-u005), [`FC04-C08-U011`](c08.html#fc04-c08-u011), [`FC04-C08-U008`](c08.html#fc04-c08-u008), [`FC04-C07-U018`](c07.html#fc04-c07-u018), [`FC04-C05-U020`](c05.html#fc04-c05-u020)
:::

For a Noetherian local domain A of dimension 1 with maximal ideal m and residue field k, the following are equivalent: A is a DVR; A is integrally closed; m is principal; dim_k(m/m²)=1; every nonzero ideal is a power of m; there is x∈A such that every nonzero ideal is (x^k), k≥0.

### Theorem: Theorem 9.3 {#fc04-c09-u009}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C09-U009` · § Dedekind domains; p. 95; L3436 · after [`FC04-C09-U008`](#fc04-c09-u008), [`FC04-C06-U014`](c06.html#fc04-c06-u014), [`FC04-C08-U005`](c08.html#fc04-c08-u005), [`FC04-C05-U020`](c05.html#fc04-c05-u020), [`FC04-C05-U022`](c05.html#fc04-c05-u022), [`FC04-C04-U001`](c04.html#fc04-c04-u001), [`FC04-C03-U009`](c03.html#fc04-c03-u009), [`FC04-C04-U023`](c04.html#fc04-c04-u023)

### Definition / notation: Fractional, integral, and principal ideals; (A:M) {#fc04-c09-u016}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C09-U016` · § Fractional ideals; pp. 96–98; L3466 · after [`FC04-C01-U013`](c01.html#fc04-c01-u013), [`FC04-C03-U004`](c03.html#fc04-c03-u004), [`FC04-C02-U007`](c02.html#fc04-c02-u007), [`FC04-C01-U016`](c01.html#fc04-c01-u016)
:::

For a domain A with fraction field K, an A-submodule M⊆K is fractional if xM⊆A for some 0≠x∈A. Ordinary ideals are called integral ideals; (u)=Au is principal; (A:M)={x∈K:xM⊆A}.

### Comparison: Finite generation of fractional ideals {#fc04-c09-u017}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C09-U017` · § Fractional ideals; pp. 96–98; L3468 · after [`FC04-C09-U016`](#fc04-c09-u016), [`FC04-C02-U022`](c02.html#fc04-c02-u022), [`FC04-C06-U014`](c06.html#fc04-c06-u014)
:::

Every finitely generated A-submodule of K is fractional; conversely, if A is Noetherian, every fractional ideal is finitely generated and has the form x⁻¹a for an integral ideal a.

### Definition: Invertible ideal {#fc04-c09-u018}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C09-U018` · § Fractional ideals; pp. 96–98; L3470 · after [`FC04-C09-U016`](#fc04-c09-u016), [`FC04-C09-U017`](#fc04-c09-u017)
:::

An A-submodule M⊆K is invertible if MN=A for some N⊆K. The inverse is unique and equals (A:M); every invertible ideal is finitely generated and hence fractional.

### Construction / comparison: Group of invertible fractional ideals {#fc04-c09-u019}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C09-U019` · § Fractional ideals; pp. 96–98; L3472 · after [`FC04-C09-U018`](#fc04-c09-u018), [`FC04-C01-U015`](c01.html#fc04-c01-u015)
:::

Every nonzero principal fractional ideal (u) is invertible with inverse (u⁻¹); the invertible ideals form a multiplicative group with identity A=(1).

### Proposition: Proposition 9.6 (invertibility is local) {#fc04-c09-u020}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C09-U020` · § Fractional ideals; pp. 96–98; L3476 · after [`FC04-C09-U016`](#fc04-c09-u016), [`FC04-C09-U018`](#fc04-c09-u018), [`FC04-C03-U009`](c03.html#fc04-c03-u009), [`FC04-C03-U032`](c03.html#fc04-c03-u032)

For a fractional ideal M, invertibility is equivalent to finite generation plus invertibility of every M_p, and equivalently to finite generation plus invertibility of every M_m for maximal m.

### Definition / construction: Principal ideals, ideal class group, and unit exact sequence {#fc04-c09-u025}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C09-U025` · § Fractional ideals; p. 96–98; L3506–L3510 · after [`FC04-C09-U024`](#fc04-c09-u024), [`FC04-C09-U016`](#fc04-c09-u016), [`FC04-C01-U015`](c01.html#fc04-c01-u015)

### Named classical comparison: Class number criterion for rings of integers {#fc04-c09-u026}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC04-C09-U026` · § Fractional ideals; p. 96–98; L3512–L3515 · after [`FC04-C09-U025`](#fc04-c09-u025), [`FC04-C09-U014`](#fc04-c09-u014)

For the ring of integers A of a number field, H is finite; h=|H| is the class number, and h=1 ⇔ I=P ⇔ A is a PID ⇔ A is a UFD.

### Named classical comparison: Structure of the unit group of a number field {#fc04-c09-u027}

::: {.unit-meta}

For a number field, the torsion units form the finite cyclic group W of roots of unity and U/W is free abelian of rank r₁+r₂−1, where r₁+2r₂=[K:Q].
