### Definition/result: weights and multiplicities of a rational representation {#fc16-c11-u001}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C11-U001` · §31.1; pp. 188–189 · after [`FC16-C06-U026`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u026), [`FC16-C09-U006`](c09.html#fc16-c09-u006), [`FC16-C02-U013`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u013)
:::

For a rational G-module V, a weight is a character λ∈X(T) with V_λ={v | t·v=λ(t)v}; dim V_λ is its multiplicity. W permutes weights and preserves multiplicities.

### Definition/result: abstract weight lattice and dominant weights {#fc16-c11-u002}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C11-U002` · §31.1; p. 189 · after [`FC16-C10-U003`](c10.html#fc16-c10-u003), [`FC16-C10-U002`](c10.html#fc16-c10-u002), [`FC16-C10-U004`](c10.html#fc16-c10-u004)
:::

Every representation weight is an abstract weight: its Cartan integers against all roots are integral. Abstract weights form a lattice Λ containing the root lattice Λ_r with finite index; a base gives fundamental dominant weights and the dominant cone.

### Definition: fundamental group, simply connected type and adjoint type {#fc16-c11-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C11-U003` · §31.1; p. 189 · after [`FC16-C11-U002`](#fc16-c11-u002), [`FC16-C06-U012`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u012), [`FC16-C09-U031`](c09.html#fc16-c09-u031)
:::

For semisimple G, π(G)=Λ/X(T). G is simply connected (universal type) when X(T)=Λ, and of adjoint type when X(T)=Λ_r.

### Definition: maximal vector {#fc16-c11-u004}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C11-U004` · §31.2; p. 189 · after [`FC16-C07-U014`](c07.html#fc16-c07-u014), [`FC16-C09-U033`](c09.html#fc16-c09-u033), [`FC16-C11-U001`](#fc16-c11-u001)
:::

A nonzero v in a rational G-module is maximal relative to B when Kv is B-stable, equivalently v is a weight vector fixed by all positive root groups.

### Proposition: module generated by a maximal vector {#fc16-c11-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C11-U005` · §31.2; pp. 189–190 · after [`FC16-C11-U004`](#fc16-c11-u004), [`FC16-C10-U003`](c10.html#fc16-c10-u003), [`FC16-C10-U021`](c10.html#fc16-c10-u021)
:::

If v is maximal of weight λ and V′=⟨Gv⟩, then every weight is λ minus a nonnegative integral combination of positive roots, λ has multiplicity one, and V′ has a unique maximal proper submodule.

### Corollary: highest weight of a maximal-generated module is dominant {#fc16-c11-u006}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C11-U006` · §31.2; p. 190 · after [`FC16-C11-U005`](#fc16-c11-u005), [`FC16-C11-U002`](#fc16-c11-u002)
:::

The maximal weight λ occurring in the preceding proposition is dominant.

A nonzero rational G-module is irreducible when its only G-stable subspaces are 0 and itself.

### Theorem/recorded result: geometric reductivity of reductive groups {#fc16-c11-u008}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C11-U008` · §31.3; p. 190 · after [`FC16-C07-U030`](c07.html#fc16-c07-u030), [`FC16-C05-U020`](c05.html#fc16-c05-u020)

### Theorem: highest-weight classification of irreducible modules {#fc16-c11-u009}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C11-U009` · §31.3; pp. 190–191 · after [`FC16-C11-U005`](#fc16-c11-u005), [`FC16-C11-U006`](#fc16-c11-u006), [`FC16-C11-U007`](#fc16-c11-u007), [`FC16-C11-U001`](#fc16-c11-u001)
:::

Every irreducible rational G-module has a unique B-stable line generated by a maximal vector of dominant highest weight λ with multiplicity one; all other weights are lower by nonnegative positive-root combinations and W preserves multiplicities. Two irreducibles are isomorphic iff their highest weights agree.

### Construction/result: addition of highest weights under tensor products {#fc16-c11-u010}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C11-U010` · §31.4; p. 191 · after [`FC16-C11-U004`](#fc16-c11-u004), [`FC16-C03-U023`](c03.html#fc16-c03-u023)
:::

If V,V′ contain maximal vectors of weights λ,μ, then their tensor product contains a maximal vector of weight λ+μ; hence existence for λ and μ implies existence for λ+μ.

### Construction: maximal-parabolic representations realizing multiples of fundamental weights {#fc16-c11-u011}

::: {.unit-meta}

### Construction: canonical highest-weight matrix coefficient {#fc16-c11-u012}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C11-U012` · §31.4; p. 192 · after [`FC16-C11-U011`](#fc16-c11-u011), [`FC16-C10-U021`](c10.html#fc16-c10-u021), [`FC16-C11-U001`](#fc16-c11-u001)

Given highest vector v_λ and dual functional r selecting it, c_λ(g)=r(gv_λ) is a regular matrix coefficient satisfying c_λ(xgy)=λ(x) c_λ(g) λ(y) for x in the opposite Borel and y in B, with the corresponding conventions.

### Theorem: existence of irreducible modules of every dominant character weight {#fc16-c11-u013}

::: {.unit-meta}

### Construction/result: function-space realization of highest-weight modules {#fc16-c11-u014}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C11-U014` · §31.4; pp. 192–193 · after [`FC16-C11-U013`](#fc16-c11-u013), [`FC16-C11-U012`](#fc16-c11-u012), [`FC16-C02-U027`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u027), [`FC16-C05-U019`](c05.html#fc16-c05-u019)
:::

For dominant λ, the semi-invariant function space F_λ⊆K[G] defined by left opposite-Borel covariance is right-translation stable; its canonical maximal vector c_λ generates the irreducible module V(λ), and in characteristic zero F_λ itself is irreducible by complete reducibility.

### Definition/result: minimal dominant highest weights {#fc16-c11-u015}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C11-U015` · §31.5; p. 193 · after [`FC16-C11-U009`](#fc16-c11-u009), [`FC16-C11-U002`](#fc16-c11-u002)
:::

A dominant λ is minimal when no smaller dominant weight exists. Then V(λ) has exactly the W-orbit of λ as its weights, each with multiplicity one, so dim V(λ)=|W·λ|; the source records the fundamental-weight examples by type.

### Theorem/result: highest weight of the contragredient {#fc16-c11-u016}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C11-U016` · §31.6; pp. 193–194 · after [`FC16-C11-U009`](#fc16-c11-u009), [`FC16-C03-U023`](c03.html#fc16-c03-u023), [`FC16-C09-U032`](c09.html#fc16-c09-u032)
:::

If V(λ) is irreducible and w_0 sends B to the opposite Borel, then V(λ)^* is irreducible of highest weight −w_0(λ). Thus V(λ) is self-dual exactly when −w_0(λ)=λ.

### Result: invariant bilinear form on a self-dual irreducible {#fc16-c11-u017}

::: {.unit-meta}
