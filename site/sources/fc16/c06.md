### Definition: semisimple and nilpotent endomorphisms {#fc16-c06-u001}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U001` · §15.1; p. 95 · after [`FC16-C01-U001`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u001)
:::

An endomorphism is semisimple when its minimal polynomial has distinct roots, equivalently it is diagonalizable over K; nilpotent means some power is zero.

### Lemma: additive Jordan–Chevalley decomposition {#fc16-c06-u002}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U002` · §15.1; pp. 95–96 · after [`FC16-C06-U001`](#fc16-c06-u001)
:::

Every endomorphism x decomposes uniquely as x=x_s+x_n with commuting semisimple and nilpotent parts, both polynomial expressions in x without constant term.

### Definition: unipotent endomorphism {#fc16-c06-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U003` · §15.1; p. 96 · after [`FC16-C06-U001`](#fc16-c06-u001)
:::

An invertible endomorphism is unipotent when it is 1 plus a nilpotent endomorphism, equivalently its only eigenvalue is 1.

### Lemma: multiplicative Jordan decomposition in GL(V) {#fc16-c06-u004}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U004` · §15.1; p. 96 · after [`FC16-C06-U002`](#fc16-c06-u002), [`FC16-C06-U003`](#fc16-c06-u003)
:::

Every x∈GL(V) decomposes uniquely as x=x_sx_u=x_ux_s with semisimple x_s and unipotent x_u; the factors preserve every x-stable subspace and commute functorially with commuting operators.

### Lemma: one-parameter subgroup generated by a unipotent element in characteristic zero {#fc16-c06-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U005` · §15.1; pp. 96–97 · after [`FC16-C06-U003`](#fc16-c06-u003), [`FC16-C02-U003`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u003)
:::

In characteristic zero, a nontrivial unipotent x determines exp(a log x):G_a→GL(V), an isomorphism onto the smallest closed subgroup containing x.

### Proposition: Jordan decomposition of regular representations of GLn and gln {#fc16-c06-u006}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U006` · §15.2; pp. 97–98 · after [`FC16-C06-U004`](#fc16-c06-u004), [`FC16-C06-U002`](#fc16-c06-u002), [`FC16-C02-U027`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u027), [`FC16-C03-U003`](c03.html#fc16-c03-u003)
:::

For GL_n and gl_n, the operators induced on coordinate functions by an element have semisimple/unipotent or semisimple/nilpotent parts induced by its matrix Jordan decomposition.

### Result: semisimple and unipotent loci under representations {#fc16-c06-u007}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U007` · §15.2–15.3; pp. 97–99 · after [`FC16-C06-U006`](#fc16-c06-u006), [`FC16-C02-U030`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u030)
:::

The polynomial nature of Jordan parts in faithful representations permits the intrinsic definitions of semisimple/unipotent group elements and semisimple/nilpotent Lie elements.

### Theorem: Jordan decomposition in algebraic groups and Lie algebras {#fc16-c06-u008}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U008` · §15.3; p. 99 · after [`FC16-C06-U006`](#fc16-c06-u006), [`FC16-C02-U030`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u030), [`FC16-C02-U011`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u011)
:::

Every x∈G has unique commuting semisimple and unipotent parts in G; every x∈g has unique commuting semisimple and nilpotent parts in g. Homomorphisms and differentials preserve these decompositions.

### Definition: simultaneously diagonalizable and trigonalizable sets {#fc16-c06-u009}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U009` · §15.4; p. 99 · after [`FC16-C02-U005`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u005)
:::

A subset of matrices is diagonalizable/trigonalizable when a single conjugation carries it into the diagonal/upper-triangular matrices.

### Proposition: simultaneous triangularization of commuting matrices {#fc16-c06-u010}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U010` · §15.4; pp. 99–100 · after [`FC16-C06-U009`](#fc16-c06-u009), [`FC16-C06-U001`](#fc16-c06-u001)
:::

Every commuting set of matrices is simultaneously triangularizable; any semisimple subfamily can be simultaneously diagonalized.

### Theorem: structure of commutative algebraic groups by Jordan parts {#fc16-c06-u011}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U011` · §15.5; p. 100 · after [`FC16-C06-U008`](#fc16-c06-u008), [`FC16-C06-U010`](#fc16-c06-u010)
:::

For a commutative algebraic group G, the semisimple and unipotent subsets G_s,G_u are closed subgroups, connected when G is; multiplication gives G_s×G_u≅G and Lie algebras split compatibly.

### Definition: character group {#fc16-c06-u012}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U012` · §16.1; pp. 101–102 · after [`FC16-C04-U005`](c04.html#fc16-c04-u005)
:::

X(G)=Hom_alg.grp(G,G_m) is an abelian group under multiplication.

### Definition: diagonalizable group (d-group) {#fc16-c06-u013}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U013` · §16.1; pp. 101–103 · after [`FC16-C06-U012`](#fc16-c06-u012), [`FC16-C02-U013`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u013)
:::

A d-group is an algebraic group all of whose rational representations decompose into character weight spaces; equivalently it is isomorphic to a closed subgroup of a diagonal group.

### Lemma: linear independence of distinct characters {#fc16-c06-u014}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U014` · §16.1; p. 102 · after [`FC16-C06-U012`](#fc16-c06-u012)
:::

Distinct homomorphisms from an abstract group to K^* are linearly independent as K-valued functions.

### Proposition: closed subgroups and character kernels of d-groups {#fc16-c06-u015}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U015` · §16.2; p. 103 · after [`FC16-C06-U013`](#fc16-c06-u013), [`FC16-C06-U012`](#fc16-c06-u012)
:::

Closed subgroups of d-groups are d-groups and intersections of character kernels; conversely every d-group is diagonalizable.

### Definition: torus {#fc16-c06-u016}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U016` · §16.2; p. 103 · after [`FC16-C06-U013`](#fc16-c06-u013), [`FC16-C02-U003`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u003)
:::

A torus is a connected diagonalizable algebraic group, equivalently a group isomorphic to G_m^r.

### Lemma: finite generation of character groups of d-groups {#fc16-c06-u017}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U017` · §16.2; pp. 103–104 · after [`FC16-C06-U013`](#fc16-c06-u013), [`FC16-C06-U012`](#fc16-c06-u012)
:::

For a d-group G, X(G) is a finitely generated abelian group.

### Lemma: character group of a connected group is torsion-free {#fc16-c06-u018}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U018` · §16.2; p. 104 · after [`FC16-C06-U012`](#fc16-c06-u012), [`FC16-C02-U006`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u006)

If G is connected then X(G) has no nonzero torsion.

### Lemma: independent characters prescribe torus coordinates {#fc16-c06-u019}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U019` · §16.2; p. 104 · after [`FC16-C06-U016`](#fc16-c06-u016), [`FC16-C06-U014`](#fc16-c06-u014)
:::

Linearly independent characters χ_1,…,χ_r of a torus jointly surject T→G_m^r, so arbitrary nonzero coordinate values can be prescribed.

### Theorem: structure of a d-group {#fc16-c06-u020}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U020` · §16.2; pp. 104–105 · after [`FC16-C06-U015`](#fc16-c06-u015), [`FC16-C06-U017`](#fc16-c06-u017), [`FC16-C06-U018`](#fc16-c06-u018)
:::

A d-group is a direct product G°×H, where G° is a torus and H is finite of order prime to char K; in particular connected d-groups are tori.

### Definition: one-parameter multiplicative subgroups and character–cocharacter pairing {#fc16-c06-u021}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U021` · §16.2; p. 105 · after [`FC16-C06-U016`](#fc16-c06-u016), [`FC16-C06-U012`](#fc16-c06-u012)
:::

Y(D)=Hom(G_m,D) is the cocharacter group; composition with χ∈X(D) gives the integer pairing ⟨χ,λ⟩, perfect for a torus.

### Construction: coordinate algebra of a diagonalizable group from its character group {#fc16-c06-u022}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U022` · §16.2; pp. 104–105 · after [`FC16-C06-U013`](#fc16-c06-u013), [`FC16-C06-U017`](#fc16-c06-u017), [`FC16-C01-U030`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u030)
:::

The coordinate algebra of a d-group is described by the group algebra K[X(G)], relating diagonalizable groups contravariantly to finitely generated abelian groups with allowed torsion.

### Result: finite-order elements are dense in a d-group {#fc16-c06-u023}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U023` · §16.3; p. 105 · after [`FC16-C06-U020`](#fc16-c06-u020)

The elements of finite order prime to the characteristic form a dense subset of a diagonalizable group.

### Proposition: rigidity for families of homomorphisms from dense torsion {#fc16-c06-u024}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U024` · §16.3; pp. 105–106 · after [`FC16-C06-U023`](#fc16-c06-u023), [`FC16-C01-U025`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u025)
:::

Under the stated hypotheses on a connected parameter variety V, a morphic family of homomorphisms from a group with dense torsion into a group with finitely many m-torsion points is constant in the parameter.

### Corollary: normalizer identity component equals centralizer for a d-group {#fc16-c06-u025}

### Definition: weights of a d-group action {#fc16-c06-u026}

### Definition: roots relative to a diagonalizable subgroup {#fc16-c06-u027}

::: {.unit-meta}

### Proposition: generic element of a torus has the same centralizer {#fc16-c06-u028}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C06-U028` · §16.4; pp. 107–108 · after [`FC16-C06-U027`](#fc16-c06-u027), [`FC16-C06-U019`](#fc16-c06-u019), [`FC16-C03-U022`](c03.html#fc16-c03-u022)
