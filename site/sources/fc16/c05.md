### Definition: algebraic Lie subalgebra {#fc16-c05-u002}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C05-U002` · §13.1; p. 87 · after [`FC16-C03-U005`](c03.html#fc16-c03-u005), [`FC16-C05-U001`](#fc16-c05-u001)
:::

A Lie subalgebra h⊆g is called algebraic (relative to G) when h=Lie(H) for a closed connected subgroup H≤G.

### Theorem: lattice correspondence between connected subgroups and Lie subalgebras {#fc16-c05-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C05-U003` · §13.1; p. 87 · after [`FC16-C04-U014`](c04.html#fc16-c04-u014), [`FC16-C05-U002`](#fc16-c05-u002)
:::

For connected G in characteristic zero, H↦Lie(H) is injective and inclusion-preserving on closed connected subgroups.

### Result: Lie algebras of vector and subspace stabilizers {#fc16-c05-u004}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C05-U004` · §13.2; p. 88 · after [`FC16-C04-U014`](c04.html#fc16-c04-u014), [`FC16-C02-U022`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u022), [`FC16-C03-U004`](c03.html#fc16-c03-u004)
:::

For W⊆V and v∈V, the Lie algebras of their GL(V)-stabilizers are exactly the linear stabilizer algebra g_W and annihilator algebra g_v.

### Theorem: group and Lie algebra have the same invariant subspaces and vectors {#fc16-c05-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C05-U005` · §13.2; p. 88 · after [`FC16-C05-U004`](#fc16-c05-u004), [`FC16-C04-U014`](c04.html#fc16-c04-u014)
:::

If H≤GL(V) is closed, Lie(H_W)=h∩g_W and Lie(H_v)=h∩g_v; hence a rational H-module and its Lie algebra stabilize the same subspaces and fix the same vectors.

### Corollary: automorphisms and derivations coincide infinitesimally {#fc16-c05-u006}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C05-U006` · §13.2; p. 88 · after [`FC16-C05-U005`](#fc16-c05-u005), [`FC16-C03-U024`](c03.html#fc16-c03-u024)
:::

For a finite-dimensional K-algebra A in characteristic zero, Lie(Aut A)=Der A.

### Theorem: normal connected subgroups correspond to ideals {#fc16-c05-u007}

::: {.unit-meta}

For connected G and closed connected H, H is normal in G iff h is an ideal of g.

### Theorem: centralizers and centers in characteristic zero {#fc16-c05-u009}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C05-U009` · §13.4; p. 89 · after [`FC16-C04-U014`](c04.html#fc16-c04-u014), [`FC16-C03-U022`](c03.html#fc16-c03-u022), [`FC16-C03-U017`](c03.html#fc16-c03-u017)

For connected G, Lie(C_G(x))=g^{Ad x}; Ker Ad=Z(G) and Lie(Z(G))=Ker ad=z(g).

### Corollary/result: commutativity detected by the Lie algebra {#fc16-c05-u010}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C05-U010` · §13.5; p. 89 · after [`FC16-C05-U009`](#fc16-c05-u009), [`FC16-C05-U003`](#fc16-c05-u003)
:::

A connected algebraic group in characteristic zero is commutative iff its Lie algebra is commutative.

### Definition: semisimple Lie algebra and semisimple algebraic group {#fc16-c05-u011}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C05-U011` · §13.5; p. 89 · after [`FC16-C05-U010`](#fc16-c05-u010), [`FC16-C05-U007`](#fc16-c05-u007)
:::

A positive-dimensional Lie algebra is semisimple when it has no nonzero commutative ideal; a connected positive-dimensional algebraic group is semisimple when it has no nontrivial closed connected commutative normal subgroup.

### Theorem: semisimplicity of group and Lie algebra {#fc16-c05-u012}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C05-U012` · §13.5; pp. 89–90 · after [`FC16-C05-U011`](#fc16-c05-u011), [`FC16-C05-U007`](#fc16-c05-u007), [`FC16-C05-U009`](#fc16-c05-u009)
:::

For connected G in characteristic zero, G is semisimple iff g is semisimple.

### Theorem: adjoint image and derivations of a semisimple group {#fc16-c05-u013}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C05-U013` · §14.1; p. 90 · after [`FC16-C05-U012`](#fc16-c05-u012), [`FC16-C05-U009`](#fc16-c05-u009), [`FC16-C05-U006`](#fc16-c05-u006)
:::

If G is semisimple, Ad(G)=(Aut g)° and ad(g)=Der g; in particular Z(G) is finite.

### Theorem: finite generation of invariant polynomial rings under complete reducibility {#fc16-c05-u021}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C05-U021` · §14.3; pp. 92–93 · after [`FC16-C01-U002`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u002), [`FC16-C05-U019`](#fc16-c05-u019), [`FC16-C05-U020`](#fc16-c05-u020)
:::

If closed G≤GL(n) acts naturally on R=K[T_1,…,T_n] and all rational G-modules are completely reducible, then R^G is finitely generated.
