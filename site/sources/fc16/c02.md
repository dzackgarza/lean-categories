### Definition: affine algebraic group {#fc16-c02-u001}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U001` · §7.1; p. 51 · after [`FC16-C01-U051`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u051), [`FC16-C01-U045`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u045)
:::

An algebraic group is an affine variety G whose multiplication G×G→G and inversion G→G are morphisms.

### Definition/comparison: abelian variety {#fc16-c02-u002}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U002` · §7.1; p. 51 · after [`FC16-C02-U001`](#fc16-c02-u001), [`FC16-C01-U090`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u090)
:::

A complete algebraic group is called an abelian variety; the text thereafter reserves “algebraic group” for affine algebraic groups.

### Examples: additive and multiplicative groups {#fc16-c02-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U003` · §7.1; pp. 51–52 · after [`FC16-C02-U001`](#fc16-c02-u001), [`FC16-C01-U020`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u020)
:::

G_a is A¹ under addition and G_m=A¹−{0} under multiplication, both affine algebraic groups.

### Example/construction: general linear group {#fc16-c02-u004}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U004` · §7.2; p. 52 · after [`FC16-C02-U001`](#fc16-c02-u001), [`FC16-C01-U047`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u047)
:::

GL(n,K) is the principal open det≠0 in matrix affine space, with regular multiplication and inversion.

### Examples: classical matrix algebraic groups {#fc16-c02-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U005` · §7.2; pp. 52–53 · after [`FC16-C02-U004`](#fc16-c02-u004)
:::

SL(n), diagonal, triangular, unipotent triangular, symplectic and orthogonal groups are realized as closed algebraic subgroups of GL(n).

### Definition: identity component {#fc16-c02-u006}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U006` · §7.3; p. 53 · after [`FC16-C01-U027`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u027), [`FC16-C02-U001`](#fc16-c02-u001)
:::

G° is the irreducible component of the identity.

### Proposition: structure of the identity component {#fc16-c02-u007}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U007` · §7.3; pp. 53–54 · after [`FC16-C02-U006`](#fc16-c02-u006)
:::

G° is a normal subgroup of finite index; its cosets are exactly the connected/irreducible components, and every closed finite-index subgroup contains G°.

### Lemma: product of dense open subsets {#fc16-c02-u008}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U008` · §7.4; p. 54 · after [`FC16-C01-U025`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u025), [`FC16-C02-U001`](#fc16-c02-u001)
:::

If U,V are dense open subsets of an algebraic group G, then G=UV.

### Proposition: closure of a subgroup {#fc16-c02-u009}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U009` · §7.4; p. 54 · after [`FC16-C01-U072`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u072), [`FC16-C02-U001`](#fc16-c02-u001)
:::

The closure of an abstract subgroup of an algebraic group is a subgroup; a constructible subgroup is closed.

### Corollary: product of normalized closed subgroups {#fc16-c02-u010}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U010` · §7.4; p. 54 · after [`FC16-C02-U009`](#fc16-c02-u009), [`FC16-C01-U072`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u072)
:::

If closed B normalizes closed A, then AB is a closed subgroup.

### Definition: morphism of algebraic groups {#fc16-c02-u011}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U011` · §7.4; p. 54 · after [`FC16-C02-U001`](#fc16-c02-u001), [`FC16-C01-U045`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u045)
:::

An algebraic-group morphism is simultaneously a group homomorphism and a morphism of varieties.

### Proposition: kernel, image, components and dimension of a homomorphism {#fc16-c02-u012}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U012` · §7.4; pp. 54–55 · after [`FC16-C02-U011`](#fc16-c02-u011), [`FC16-C01-U066`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u066), [`FC16-C02-U009`](#fc16-c02-u009)
:::

For φ:G→G′, Kerφ and Imφ are closed, φ(G°)=φ(G)°, and dim G=dim Kerφ+dim Imφ.

### Definition: rational representation {#fc16-c02-u013}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U013` · §7.5; p. 55 · after [`FC16-C02-U011`](#fc16-c02-u011), [`FC16-C02-U004`](#fc16-c02-u004)
:::

A morphism of algebraic groups G→GL(V) is a rational representation.

### Definition: group closure generated by a subset {#fc16-c02-u014}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U014` · §7.5; p. 55 · after [`FC16-C02-U009`](#fc16-c02-u009)
:::

For M⊆G, the group closure is the smallest closed subgroup containing M.

### Proposition: generation by irreducible images {#fc16-c02-u015}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U015` · §7.5; pp. 55–56 · after [`FC16-C02-U014`](#fc16-c02-u014), [`FC16-C01-U026`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u026), [`FC16-C01-U072`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u072)
:::

Images of irreducible varieties through e generate a connected closed subgroup, and finitely many signed products already equal that subgroup.

### Corollary: connected subgroups generate connected group {#fc16-c02-u016}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U016` · §7.5; p. 56 · after [`FC16-C02-U015`](#fc16-c02-u015)
:::

A group abstractly generated by closed connected subgroups is connected.

### Construction/result: Hopf-algebra structure on K[G] {#fc16-c02-u017}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U017` · §7.6; pp. 56–57 · after [`FC16-C01-U030`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u030), [`FC16-C02-U001`](#fc16-c02-u001), [`FC16-C01-U029`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u029)
:::

Multiplication, inversion and identity induce comultiplication K[G]→K[G]⊗K[G], antipode and counit satisfying the Hopf identities.

### Definition: group action, orbit, stabilizer and fixed points {#fc16-c02-u018}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U018` · §8.1; p. 58 · after [`FC16-C02-U001`](#fc16-c02-u001)
:::

An action satisfies associativity and identity; G·x is the orbit, G_x the stabilizer, X^G the fixed set, and G/G_x maps bijectively to the orbit.

### Examples: translation and conjugation actions {#fc16-c02-u019}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U019` · §8.1; pp. 58–59 · after [`FC16-C02-U018`](#fc16-c02-u018)
:::

Left/right translation and conjugation are canonical group actions; centralizers and normalizers arise as stabilizers/fixed-point constructions.

### Definition: morphic algebraic-group action {#fc16-c02-u020}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U020` · §8.2; p. 59 · after [`FC16-C02-U018`](#fc16-c02-u018), [`FC16-C01-U045`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u045)
:::

An algebraic group acts morphically on X when the action map G×X→X is a morphism.

### Definition: transporter {#fc16-c02-u021}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U021` · §8.2; p. 59 · after [`FC16-C02-U020`](#fc16-c02-u020)
:::

For Y,Z⊆X, Trans_G(Y,Z)={g∈G | gY⊆Z}; stabilizers, centralizers and normalizers are special cases.

### Proposition: closedness of transporters and fixed loci {#fc16-c02-u022}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U022` · §8.2; p. 59 · after [`FC16-C02-U021`](#fc16-c02-u021), [`FC16-C01-U051`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u051)
:::

Transporters into closed sets are closed; stabilizers and centralizers are closed subgroups, and fixed-point sets are closed.

### Corollary: normalizers and centralizers are closed {#fc16-c02-u023}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U023` · §8.2; pp. 59–60 · after [`FC16-C02-U022`](#fc16-c02-u022)
:::

Normalizers and centralizers of closed subgroups, and centralizers of elements, are closed algebraic subgroups.

### Definition: equivariant morphism {#fc16-c02-u024}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U024` · §8.2; p. 60 · after [`FC16-C02-U020`](#fc16-c02-u020), [`FC16-C01-U045`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u045)
:::

A morphism φ:X→Y between G-varieties is G-equivariant when φ(gx)=gφ(x).

### Proposition: geometry of orbits {#fc16-c02-u025}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U025` · §8.3; pp. 60–61 · after [`FC16-C02-U020`](#fc16-c02-u020), [`FC16-C01-U080`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u080), [`FC16-C01-U071`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u071)
:::

Every orbit of an algebraic-group action is smooth and locally closed; its boundary is a union of strictly lower-dimensional orbits, so minimal-dimensional orbits are closed.

### Definition: semidirect product {#fc16-c02-u026}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U026` · §8.4; p. 61 · after [`FC16-C02-U018`](#fc16-c02-u018), [`FC16-C02-U001`](#fc16-c02-u001)
:::

If G acts by automorphisms on N, N⋊G has multiplication (n,g)(n′,g′)=(n(g·n′),gg′); N is normal and every element factors uniquely.

### Definition: translation of functions {#fc16-c02-u027}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U027` · §8.5; pp. 61–62 · after [`FC16-C02-U020`](#fc16-c02-u020), [`FC16-C01-U030`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u030)
:::

An action on X induces linear K-algebra automorphisms τ_g of K[X]; for G acting on itself these are left and right translations λ_g,ρ_g.

### Lemma: subgroup ideal criterion under right translation {#fc16-c02-u028}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U028` · §8.5; p. 62 · after [`FC16-C02-U027`](#fc16-c02-u027), [`FC16-C01-U030`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u030)
:::

For closed H⊆G with vanishing ideal I, H={x∈G | ρ_x(I)⊆I}.

### Proposition: finite-dimensional translation-stable enlargement {#fc16-c02-u029}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U029` · §8.6; pp. 62–63 · after [`FC16-C02-U027`](#fc16-c02-u027), [`FC16-C02-U017`](#fc16-c02-u017)
:::

Every finite-dimensional subspace of K[X] is contained in a finite-dimensional subspace stable under all translations; stability is characterized by the comorphism landing in K[G]⊗F.

### Theorem: linearization of affine algebraic groups {#fc16-c02-u030}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C02-U030` · §8.6; pp. 63–64 · after [`FC16-C02-U029`](#fc16-c02-u029), [`FC16-C02-U028`](#fc16-c02-u028), [`FC16-C02-U013`](#fc16-c02-u013)
:::

Every affine algebraic group is isomorphic to a closed subgroup of some GL(n,K).
