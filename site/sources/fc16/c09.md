### Definition: regular and singular tori {#fc16-c09-u001}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U001` · §24 opening; p. 147 · after [`FC16-C08-U032`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u032), [`FC16-C06-U016`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u016)
:::

For a torus S, let 𝔅^S be the Borel subgroups containing S. S is regular when 𝔅^S is finite and singular otherwise.

### Definition: Weyl group relative to a torus {#fc16-c09-u002}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U002` · §24.1; p. 147 · after [`FC16-C06-U025`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u025), [`FC16-C02-U023`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u023), [`FC16-C08-U006`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u006)
:::

W(G,S)=N_G(S)/C_G(S), finite by torus rigidity. For maximal tori these groups are all isomorphic and are called the Weyl group of G.

### Lemma: centralizer of a maximal torus lies in every containing Borel {#fc16-c09-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U003` · §24.1; p. 147 · after [`FC16-C08-U021`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u021), [`FC16-C08-U024`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u024)
:::

If T is maximal and C=C_G(T), then C is contained in every Borel subgroup containing T.

### Proposition: simple transitivity of Weyl group on Borel subgroups through T {#fc16-c09-u004}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U004` · §24.1; pp. 147–148 · after [`FC16-C09-U002`](#fc16-c09-u002), [`FC16-C09-U003`](#fc16-c09-u003), [`FC16-C08-U027`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u027)
:::

For maximal T, W(G,T) acts simply transitively on 𝔅^T; hence |𝔅^T|=|W| and T is regular.

### Proposition: Weyl groups and Borel sets under epimorphisms {#fc16-c09-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U005` · §24.1; p. 148 · after [`FC16-C08-U011`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u011), [`FC16-C09-U004`](#fc16-c09-u004)
:::

An epimorphism G→G′ with T↦T′ induces surjections 𝔅^T→𝔅^{T′} and W(G,T)→W(G′,T′), bijective if the kernel lies in every Borel subgroup.

### Construction: Weyl group action on characters, cocharacters and roots {#fc16-c09-u006}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U006` · §24.1; pp. 148–149 · after [`FC16-C09-U002`](#fc16-c09-u002), [`FC16-C06-U021`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u021), [`FC16-C06-U027`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u027)
:::

N_G(T) acts on X(T),Y(T), descends to W, preserves the pairing, and permutes roots/root spaces by w·α(t)=α(n^{-1}tn), Ad n(g_α)=g_{wα}.

### Proposition: regular torus criterion by solvability of its centralizer {#fc16-c09-u007}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U007` · §24.2; p. 149 · after [`FC16-C09-U001`](#fc16-c09-u001), [`FC16-C08-U020`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u020), [`FC16-C08-U024`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u024)
:::

A torus S is regular iff C_G(S) is solvable. Then C_G(S) lies in every Borel containing S, and for every maximal T⊇S one has 𝔅^S=𝔅^T.

### Definition/construction: intersection subgroup I(T) and exterior roots {#fc16-c09-u008}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U008` · §24.3; pp. 149–150 · after [`FC16-C09-U003`](#fc16-c09-u003), [`FC16-C06-U026`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u026), [`FC16-C08-U032`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u032)
:::

For maximal T, I(T) is the identity component of the intersection of all Borel subgroups containing T. Splitting g=Lie(I(T))⊕⊕_{α∈Ψ}g_α defines the finite set Ψ of root weights outside Lie(I(T)).

### Definition: root-kernel torus {#fc16-c09-u009}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U009` · §24.3; p. 150 · after [`FC16-C09-U008`](#fc16-c09-u008), [`FC16-C06-U012`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u012)
:::

For α∈Ψ, T_α=(Ker α)° is a codimension-one subtorus of T.

### Proposition: singular subtori detected by roots {#fc16-c09-u010}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U010` · §24.3; p. 150 · after [`FC16-C09-U007`](#fc16-c09-u007), [`FC16-C09-U009`](#fc16-c09-u009), [`FC16-C07-U021`](c07.html#fc16-c07-u021)
:::

For S⊆T, S is singular iff S is contained in T_α for some α∈Ψ; in particular the maximal singular subtori are the T_α.

### Corollary: rank-one semisimple quotient of a root centralizer {#fc16-c09-u011}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U011` · §24.3; p. 150 · after [`FC16-C09-U010`](#fc16-c09-u010), [`FC16-C07-U030`](c07.html#fc16-c07-u030), [`FC16-C08-U007`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u007)
:::

For α∈Ψ and Z_α=C_G(T_α), the quotient Z_α/R(Z_α) is semisimple of rank one.

### Definition: regular cocharacter {#fc16-c09-u012}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U012` · §24.4; pp. 150–151 · after [`FC16-C06-U021`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u021), [`FC16-C09-U001`](#fc16-c09-u001)
:::

A one-parameter subgroup λ∈Y(T) is regular when λ(G_m) is a regular torus; Y(T)_reg denotes the regular cocharacters.

### Proposition: root pairing criterion for regular cocharacters {#fc16-c09-u013}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U013` · §24.4; p. 151 · after [`FC16-C09-U012`](#fc16-c09-u012), [`FC16-C09-U010`](#fc16-c09-u010), [`FC16-C06-U021`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u021)
:::

λ∈Y(T) is regular iff ⟨α,λ⟩≠0 for every α∈Ψ.

### Construction/result: extension of a cocharacter orbit in projective space {#fc16-c09-u014}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U014` · §25.1; pp. 151–153 · after [`FC16-C06-U026`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u026), [`FC16-C01-U092`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u092), [`FC16-C06-U021`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u021)
:::

For a torus representation with weights χ_i and λ∈Y(T), the orbit map t↦λ(t)[v] extends to P¹; the 0 and ∞ limits are obtained by retaining the minimum and maximum λ-weight components and are λ(G_m)-fixed.

### Result: cocharacters with separated weights have the same projective fixed points as T {#fc16-c09-u015}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U015` · §25.1; p. 153 · after [`FC16-C09-U014`](#fc16-c09-u014), [`FC16-C06-U019`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u019)
:::

Choosing λ so that distinct T-weights have distinct pairings makes the fixed points of λ(G_m) in P(V) coincide with the T-fixed points.

### Lemma: hyperplane section of a projective variety {#fc16-c09-u016}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U016` · §25.2; p. 153 · after [`FC16-C01-U092`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u092), [`FC16-C01-U061`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u061)
:::

If irreducible projective X⊄P(W) with W codimension one and dim X>0, then X∩P(W) is nonempty and every irreducible component has codimension one.

### Theorem: fixed points of a torus on an invariant projective variety {#fc16-c09-u017}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U017` · §25.2; pp. 153–154 · after [`FC16-C09-U015`](#fc16-c09-u015), [`FC16-C09-U016`](#fc16-c09-u016), [`FC16-C09-U014`](#fc16-c09-u014)
:::

For torus T acting on irreducible closed X⊆P(V): if dim X≥1 the source obtains at least two T-fixed points; if dim X≥2 it obtains at least three.

### Corollary: torus fixed points on G/P {#fc16-c09-u018}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U018` · §25.2; p. 154 · after [`FC16-C09-U017`](#fc16-c09-u017), [`FC16-C08-U008`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u008), [`FC16-C08-U032`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u032)
:::

For proper parabolic P and torus T, T fixes at least two points of G/P, and at least three when dim G/P>1; a maximal torus therefore lies in at least two/three corresponding Borel subgroups.

### Corollary: lower bounds for the Weyl group from dim G/B {#fc16-c09-u019}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U019` · §25.2; p. 154 · after [`FC16-C09-U018`](#fc16-c09-u018), [`FC16-C09-U004`](#fc16-c09-u004)
:::

By simple transitivity, dim G/B=1 forces |W|=2, while higher-dimensional cases give the corresponding ≥3 lower bound recorded in the source.

### Corollary: Borel subgroups through a maximal torus generate G {#fc16-c09-u020}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U020` · §25.2; p. 154 · after [`FC16-C09-U018`](#fc16-c09-u018), [`FC16-C08-U009`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u009), [`FC16-C08-U030`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u030)
:::

For maximal T, G is generated by all Borel subgroups containing T.

### Definition: semisimple and reductive rank {#fc16-c09-u021}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U021` · §25.3; p. 154 · after [`FC16-C08-U007`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u007), [`FC16-C07-U029`](c07.html#fc16-c07-u029)
:::

rank_s G=rank(G/R(G)) and rank_red G=rank(G/R_u(G)).

### Theorem: equivalent descriptions of semisimple rank one {#fc16-c09-u022}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U022` · §25.3; pp. 154–155 · after [`FC16-C09-U021`](#fc16-c09-u021), [`FC16-C09-U019`](#fc16-c09-u019), [`FC16-C01-U094`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u094), [`FC16-C08-U032`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u032)
:::

For connected G and maximal T, rank_sG=1 iff |W|=2 iff |𝔅^T|=2 iff dim G/B=1 iff G/B≅P¹ iff there is an epimorphism G→PGL_2 with kernel identity component R(G).

### Corollary: structure of reductive groups of semisimple rank one {#fc16-c09-u023}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U023` · §25.3; pp. 155–156 · after [`FC16-C09-U022`](#fc16-c09-u022), [`FC16-C07-U031`](c07.html#fc16-c07-u031), [`FC16-C07-U004`](c07.html#fc16-c07-u004)
:::

If G is reductive of semisimple rank one, (G,G) is 3-dimensional semisimple, G=(G,G)Z(G) with finite intersection, C_G(T)=T, and any epimorphism to PGL_2 has kernel Z(G).

### Construction: rank-one Weyl reflection attached to a singular torus {#fc16-c09-u024}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U024` · §25.4; p. 156 · after [`FC16-C09-U011`](#fc16-c09-u011), [`FC16-C09-U022`](#fc16-c09-u022), [`FC16-C09-U002`](#fc16-c09-u002)
:::

For α∈Ψ, Z_α=C_G(T_α) has Weyl group of order two; its nontrivial element s_α embeds canonically in W(G,T) and exchanges its two Borel subgroups through T.

### Construction: Borel subgroup B(λ) from a regular cocharacter {#fc16-c09-u025}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U025` · §25.4; pp. 156–157 · after [`FC16-C09-U014`](#fc16-c09-u014), [`FC16-C09-U012`](#fc16-c09-u012), [`FC16-C08-U032`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u032), [`FC16-C09-U006`](#fc16-c09-u006)
:::

A regular λ∈Y(T) selects a canonical T-fixed point on G/B by the ∞-limit construction, hence a Borel subgroup B(λ) containing T; the assignment is W-equivariant and onto 𝔅^T.

### Definition: Weyl chamber {#fc16-c09-u026}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U026` · §25.4; p. 157 · after [`FC16-C09-U025`](#fc16-c09-u025), [`FC16-C09-U004`](#fc16-c09-u004)
:::

For B∈𝔅^T, C(B)={λ∈Y(T)_reg | B(λ)=B}; these nonempty sets partition the regular cocharacters and are permuted simply transitively by W.

### Proposition: root sign detects the Borel in a rank-one centralizer {#fc16-c09-u027}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U027` · §25.4; p. 157 · after [`FC16-C09-U024`](#fc16-c09-u024), [`FC16-C09-U025`](#fc16-c09-u025), [`FC16-C09-U026`](#fc16-c09-u026)

For α∈Ψ, the two Borels B_α,B′_α of Z_α through T can be labeled so that ⟨α,λ⟩>0 iff B(λ)∩Z_α=B_α.

### Lemma: regular cocharacter separates nonproportional roots {#fc16-c09-u028}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U028` · §26.1; p. 158 · after [`FC16-C09-U013`](#fc16-c09-u013), [`FC16-C06-U021`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u021)

For nonproportional α,β∈Ψ there is λ∈Y(T)_reg with ⟨α,λ⟩>0 and ⟨β,λ⟩<0.

### Theorem: unipotent radical from intersection of Borels through T {#fc16-c09-u029}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U029` · §26.1; pp. 158–159 · after [`FC16-C09-U008`](#fc16-c09-u008), [`FC16-C09-U028`](#fc16-c09-u028), [`FC16-C09-U020`](#fc16-c09-u020), [`FC16-C07-U029`](c07.html#fc16-c07-u029)
:::

If I(T) is the identity component of the intersection of B∈𝔅^T, then R_u(G) equals the unipotent part I(T)_u.

### Corollary: torus centralizers in reductive groups {#fc16-c09-u030}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U030` · §26.2; pp. 159–160 · after [`FC16-C09-U029`](#fc16-c09-u029), [`FC16-C07-U030`](c07.html#fc16-c07-u030), [`FC16-C09-U007`](#fc16-c09-u007)
:::

If G is reductive and S⊆T is a torus, C_G(S) is reductive. If S is regular then C_G(S)=T; hence Cartan subgroups are maximal tori and Z(G)⊆T.

### Corollary: root-space, root-centralizer, center and root-lattice structure of a reductive group {#fc16-c09-u031}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U031` · §26.2; p. 160 · after [`FC16-C09-U030`](#fc16-c09-u030), [`FC16-C09-U008`](#fc16-c09-u008), [`FC16-C09-U010`](#fc16-c09-u010), [`FC16-C09-U023`](#fc16-c09-u023), [`FC16-C09-U020`](#fc16-c09-u020), [`FC16-C09-U021`](#fc16-c09-u021)
:::

For reductive G: Ψ=Φ(G,T) and Φ=−Φ; g=t⊕⊕_{α∈Φ}g_α with dim g_α=1; the codimension-one singular tori are T_α; each Z_α=C_G(T_α) is reductive of semisimple rank one with Lie algebra t⊕g_α⊕g_{−α}, and the Z_α generate G; Z(G)=∩_{α∈Φ}Ker α; and the root lattice in X(T) has rank rank_sG.

### Corollary/definition: opposite Borel subgroup {#fc16-c09-u032}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U032` · §26.2; p. 160 · after [`FC16-C09-U025`](#fc16-c09-u025), [`FC16-C09-U031`](#fc16-c09-u031), [`FC16-C09-U027`](#fc16-c09-u027)
:::

For reductive G, every B∈𝔅^T has a unique opposite B^- with B∩B^-=T; writing B=TU and B^-=TU^- gives g=u^-⊕t⊕u.

### Theorem/definition: root subgroups {#fc16-c09-u033}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U033` · §26.3; pp. 160–162 · after [`FC16-C09-U031`](#fc16-c09-u031), [`FC16-C07-U042`](c07.html#fc16-c07-u042), [`FC16-C09-U006`](#fc16-c09-u006)
:::

For each α∈Φ there is a unique connected T-stable one-dimensional unipotent subgroup U_α with Lie algebra g_α; W-conjugation sends U_α to U_{wα}, and there is an admissible isomorphism x_α:G_a→U_α satisfying t x_α(c)t^{-1}=x_α(α(t)c). Together T and all U_α generate G.

### Definition: admissible root-group parametrization {#fc16-c09-u034}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C09-U034` · §26.3; p. 162 · after [`FC16-C09-U033`](#fc16-c09-u033)
:::

An isomorphism x_α:G_a→U_α satisfying the T-equivariance formula is called admissible; it is unique up to nonzero scalar.
