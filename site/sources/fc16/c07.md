### Definition: commutator subgroup (A,B) {#fc16-c07-u001}

::: {.unit-meta}

For subgroups A,B, (A,B) is the subgroup generated by commutators (x,y)=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}.

### Lemma: finite central quotient gives finite derived group {#fc16-c07-u002}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C07-U002` · §17.1; pp. 109–110 · after [`FC16-C07-U001`](#fc16-c07-u001)

If [G:Z(G)] is finite then (G,G) is finite.

### Lemma: finite commutator set for normal subgroups gives finite commutator subgroup {#fc16-c07-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C07-U003` · §17.1; p. 110 · after [`FC16-C07-U001`](#fc16-c07-u001)
:::

If A,B are normal and the set of pairwise commutators {(a,b)} is finite, then (A,B) is finite.

### Proposition: closedness and connectedness of algebraic commutator groups {#fc16-c07-u004}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C07-U004` · §17.2; p. 110 · after [`FC16-C02-U015`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u015), [`FC16-C07-U001`](#fc16-c07-u001), [`FC16-C07-U003`](#fc16-c07-u003)
:::

For closed A,B≤G, if A is connected then (A,B) is closed and connected; if A,B are normal then (A,B) is closed and normal. In particular (G,G) is closed.

### Definition: derived series and solvable group {#fc16-c07-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C07-U005` · §17.3; pp. 110–111 · after [`FC16-C07-U004`](#fc16-c07-u004)
:::

The derived series is G^{(0)}=G, G^{(i+1)}=(G^{(i)},G^{(i)}); G is solvable if this reaches e, with least index its derived length.

### Lemma: closure properties of solvable groups {#fc16-c07-u006}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C07-U006` · §17.3; p. 111 · after [`FC16-C07-U005`](#fc16-c07-u005)
:::

Subgroups and homomorphic images of solvable groups are solvable; solvable extensions are solvable; products of normal solvable subgroups are solvable.

### Example/result: upper triangular groups are solvable {#fc16-c07-u007}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C07-U007` · §17.3; p. 111 · after [`FC16-C02-U005`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u005), [`FC16-C07-U005`](#fc16-c07-u005)
:::

The upper triangular matrix group is solvable, with its derived behavior controlled by powers of the strictly upper-triangular ideal.

### Definition: descending central series and nilpotent group {#fc16-c07-u008}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C07-U008` · §17.4; pp. 111–112 · after [`FC16-C07-U001`](#fc16-c07-u001), [`FC16-C07-U004`](#fc16-c07-u004)
:::

γ^0G=G and γ^{i+1}G=(G,γ^iG); G is nilpotent when some γ^nG=e.

### Lemma: elementary properties of nilpotent groups {#fc16-c07-u009}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C07-U009` · §17.4; p. 112 · after [`FC16-C07-U008`](#fc16-c07-u008)
:::

Subgroups and quotients of nilpotent groups are nilpotent; if G/Z(G) is nilpotent then G is; nontrivial nilpotent groups have nontrivial center, and proper subgroups lie properly in their normalizers.

### Proposition: center and normalizers in connected nilpotent algebraic groups {#fc16-c07-u010}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C07-U010` · §17.4; p. 112 · after [`FC16-C07-U009`](#fc16-c07-u009), [`FC16-C02-U006`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u006)
:::

A connected positive-dimensional nilpotent algebraic group has positive-dimensional center; proper closed H has dim H<dim N_G(H), so codimension-one closed subgroups are normal.

### Definition: unipotent algebraic subgroup {#fc16-c07-u011}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C07-U011` · §17.5; p. 112 · after [`FC16-C06-U008`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u008)
:::

A subgroup is unipotent when every element is unipotent in the Jordan-decomposition sense.

### Theorem: common fixed vector for a unipotent group {#fc16-c07-u012}

::: {.unit-meta}

:::

A unipotent subgroup of GL(V), V≠0 finite-dimensional, has a nonzero vector fixed by every element.

### Corollary: unipotent groups are triangularizable and nilpotent {#fc16-c07-u013}

Every unipotent subgroup of GL_n is conjugate into U(n,K), hence is nilpotent.

### Theorem: Lie–Kolchin theorem {#fc16-c07-u014}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C07-U014` · §17.6; pp. 113–114 · after [`FC16-C07-U005`](#fc16-c07-u005), [`FC16-C07-U004`](#fc16-c07-u004), [`FC16-C04-U007`](c04.html#fc16-c04-u007)
:::

A connected solvable subgroup of GL(V), V≠0, has a common eigenvector; equivalently it is conjugate into an upper triangular group.

### Corollary/result: connected solvable groups stabilize a full flag {#fc16-c07-u015}

### Proposition: global and infinitesimal centralizers for semisimple normalizers {#fc16-c07-u016}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C07-U016` · §18.1; p. 116 · after [`FC16-C06-U008`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u008), [`FC16-C03-U022`](c03.html#fc16-c03-u022), [`FC16-C06-U027`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u027)

### Proposition: nilpotent connected solvable groups {#fc16-c07-u025}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C07-U025` · §19.2; p. 122 · after [`FC16-C07-U008`](#fc16-c07-u008), [`FC16-C06-U011`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u011), [`FC16-C07-U024`](#fc16-c07-u024)
:::

A connected solvable G is nilpotent iff its semisimple elements form a subgroup; then that subgroup is closed and G decomposes as its semisimple and unipotent factors.

### Theorem: structure of connected solvable algebraic groups {#fc16-c07-u026}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C07-U026` · §19.3; pp. 123–124 · after [`FC16-C07-U014`](#fc16-c07-u014), [`FC16-C07-U004`](#fc16-c07-u004), [`FC16-C06-U020`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u020), [`FC16-C07-U025`](#fc16-c07-u025)
:::

For connected solvable G, G_u is closed connected normal and contains (G,G), with a normal codimension-one filtration; maximal tori are conjugate under G_u and multiplication T⋉G_u→G is an isomorphism of varieties/groups in the stated form.

### Corollary: semisimple and unipotent elements lie in maximal subgroups {#fc16-c07-u027}

:::

Every semisimple element of a connected solvable group lies in a maximal torus; every unipotent element lies in a maximal connected unipotent subgroup.

### Proposition: semisimple subgroups of a connected solvable group {#fc16-c07-u028}

:::

A subgroup consisting entirely of semisimple elements lies in a torus, and its centralizer and normalizer in G coincide and are connected.
