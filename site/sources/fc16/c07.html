Chapter VII. Solvable Groups

FC16 — Humphreys, Linear Algebraic Groups.

This chapter contains 9 definitions, 5 theorems, 11 propositions, 7 lemmas, 7 corollaries, 1 example, 1 construction and 1 other statements.

Nobody has checked this chapter against Lean yet, so the statements below say nothing about whether they are formalized.

Definition: commutator subgroup (A,B)

FC16-C07-U001 · §17.1; p. 109 · after FC16-C02-U001

For subgroups A,B, (A,B) is the subgroup generated by commutators (x,y)=xyx{-1}y{-1}.

Lemma: finite central quotient gives finite derived group

FC16-C07-U002 · §17.1; pp. 109–110 · after FC16-C07-U001

If [G:Z(G)] is finite then (G,G) is finite.

Lemma: finite commutator set for normal subgroups gives finite commutator subgroup

FC16-C07-U003 · §17.1; p. 110 · after FC16-C07-U001

If A,B are normal and the set of pairwise commutators {(a,b)} is finite, then (A,B) is finite.

Proposition: closedness and connectedness of algebraic commutator groups

FC16-C07-U004 · §17.2; p. 110 · after FC16-C02-U015, FC16-C07-U001, FC16-C07-U003

For closed A,B≤G, if A is connected then (A,B) is closed and connected; if A,B are normal then (A,B) is closed and normal. In particular (G,G) is closed.

Definition: derived series and solvable group

FC16-C07-U005 · §17.3; pp. 110–111 · after FC16-C07-U004

The derived series is G^{(0)}=G, G{(i+1)}=(G{(i)},G^{(i)}); G is solvable if this reaches e, with least index its derived length.

Lemma: closure properties of solvable groups

FC16-C07-U006 · §17.3; p. 111 · after FC16-C07-U005

Subgroups and homomorphic images of solvable groups are solvable; solvable extensions are solvable; products of normal solvable subgroups are solvable.

Example/result: upper triangular groups are solvable

FC16-C07-U007 · §17.3; p. 111 · after FC16-C02-U005, FC16-C07-U005

The upper triangular matrix group is solvable, with its derived behavior controlled by powers of the strictly upper-triangular ideal.

Definition: descending central series and nilpotent group

FC16-C07-U008 · §17.4; pp. 111–112 · after FC16-C07-U001, FC16-C07-U004

γ^0G=G and γ{i+1}G=(G,γiG); G is nilpotent when some γ^nG=e.

Lemma: elementary properties of nilpotent groups

FC16-C07-U009 · §17.4; p. 112 · after FC16-C07-U008

Subgroups and quotients of nilpotent groups are nilpotent; if G/Z(G) is nilpotent then G is; nontrivial nilpotent groups have nontrivial center, and proper subgroups lie properly in their normalizers.

Proposition: center and normalizers in connected nilpotent algebraic groups

FC16-C07-U010 · §17.4; p. 112 · after FC16-C07-U009, FC16-C02-U006

A connected positive-dimensional nilpotent algebraic group has positive-dimensional center; proper closed H has dim H<dim N_G(H), so codimension-one closed subgroups are normal.

Definition: unipotent algebraic subgroup

FC16-C07-U011 · §17.5; p. 112 · after FC16-C06-U008

A subgroup is unipotent when every element is unipotent in the Jordan-decomposition sense.

Theorem: common fixed vector for a unipotent group

FC16-C07-U012 · §17.5; pp. 112–113 · after FC16-C07-U011, FC16-C02-U030

A unipotent subgroup of GL(V), V≠0 finite-dimensional, has a nonzero vector fixed by every element.

Corollary: unipotent groups are triangularizable and nilpotent

FC16-C07-U013 · §17.5; p. 113 · after FC16-C07-U012, FC16-C07-U007, FC16-C07-U008

Every unipotent subgroup of GL_n is conjugate into U(n,K), hence is nilpotent.

Theorem: Lie–Kolchin theorem

FC16-C07-U014 · §17.6; pp. 113–114 · after FC16-C07-U005, FC16-C07-U004, FC16-C04-U007

A connected solvable subgroup of GL(V), V≠0, has a common eigenvector; equivalently it is conjugate into an upper triangular group.

Corollary/result: connected solvable groups stabilize a full flag

FC16-C07-U015 · §17.6; p. 114 · after FC16-C07-U014, FC16-C01-U038

Iterating Lie–Kolchin yields a full G-stable flag in every finite-dimensional rational module of a connected solvable group.

Proposition: global and infinitesimal centralizers for semisimple normalizers

FC16-C07-U016 · §18.1; p. 116 · after FC16-C06-U008, FC16-C03-U022, FC16-C06-U027

If closed H≤GL_n is normalized by a semisimple s, then Lie(C_H(s)) is the 1-eigenspace of Ad s on h, and the complementary eigenspaces give the tangent space of the commutator image at e.

Corollary: semisimple conjugacy orbit as homogeneous space

FC16-C07-U017 · §18.1; p. 117 · after FC16-C07-U016, FC16-C04-U009

For semisimple s normalizing H, the conjugacy class map H→Cl_H(s) identifies with H/C_H(s).

Corollary: transverse centralizer–commutator differential decomposition

FC16-C07-U018 · §18.1; p. 117 · after FC16-C07-U016, FC16-C01-U085

For the centralizer C and commutator image M′ near s, the product C×M′→H has an isomorphism on tangent spaces at the identity pair.

Proposition: semisimple conjugacy classes are closed

FC16-C07-U019 · §18.2; pp. 117–118 · after FC16-C07-U017, FC16-C02-U025

If H≤GL_n is normalized by a semisimple element s, then the H-conjugacy class of s is closed.

Theorem: semisimple action on a connected unipotent group

FC16-C07-U020 · §18.3; pp. 118–119 · after FC16-C07-U011, FC16-C07-U016, FC16-C07-U019

If connected unipotent U is normalized by semisimple s, the centralizer C_U(s) and commutator image M give a bijective product decomposition C×M→U; C and M are connected/closed and the commutator map restricts bijectively on M.

Proposition: centralizers of d-group actions infinitesimally

FC16-C07-U021 · §18.4; pp. 119–120 · after FC16-C06-U028, FC16-C04-U014

If closed H≤G is normalized by a d-group D, then Lie(C_H(D))=h^D.

Corollary: equivariant epimorphisms on d-group fixed identity components

FC16-C07-U022 · §18.4; p. 120 · after FC16-C07-U021, FC16-C02-U012

A D-equivariant epimorphism H→H′ maps (H^D)° onto (H′^D)°.

Proposition: connected fixed subgroup in connected unipotent groups

FC16-C07-U023 · §18.4; p. 120 · after FC16-C07-U020, FC16-C07-U021

If connected unipotent U is normalized by a d-group D, then C_U(D) is connected.

Construction/result: semisimple quotient exact sequence for a connected solvable group

FC16-C07-U024 · §19.1; p. 122 · after FC16-C06-U008, FC16-C07-U014

For connected solvable G, Jordan decomposition yields the exact structural relation between its semisimple part, unipotent part, and a torus quotient used throughout §19.

Proposition: nilpotent connected solvable groups

FC16-C07-U025 · §19.2; p. 122 · after FC16-C07-U008, FC16-C06-U011, FC16-C07-U024

A connected solvable G is nilpotent iff its semisimple elements form a subgroup; then that subgroup is closed and G decomposes as its semisimple and unipotent factors.

Theorem: structure of connected solvable algebraic groups

FC16-C07-U026 · §19.3; pp. 123–124 · after FC16-C07-U014, FC16-C07-U004, FC16-C06-U020, FC16-C07-U025

For connected solvable G, G_u is closed connected normal and contains (G,G), with a normal codimension-one filtration; maximal tori are conjugate under G_u and multiplication T⋉G_u→G is an isomorphism of varieties/groups in the stated form.

Corollary: semisimple and unipotent elements lie in maximal subgroups

FC16-C07-U027 · §19.3; p. 124 · after FC16-C07-U026

Every semisimple element of a connected solvable group lies in a maximal torus; every unipotent element lies in a maximal connected unipotent subgroup.

Proposition: semisimple subgroups of a connected solvable group

FC16-C07-U028 · §19.4; p. 124 · after FC16-C07-U026, FC16-C06-U025

A subgroup consisting entirely of semisimple elements lies in a torus, and its centralizer and normalizer in G coincide and are connected.

Definition: radical and unipotent radical

FC16-C07-U029 · §19.5; p. 125 · after FC16-C07-U006, FC16-C07-U011

R(G) is the largest connected normal solvable subgroup; R_u(G) is the largest connected normal unipotent subgroup.

Definition: semisimple and reductive groups via radicals

FC16-C07-U030 · §19.5; p. 125 · after FC16-C07-U029, FC16-C05-U011

A connected nontrivial G is semisimple when R(G)=e and reductive when R_u(G)=e.

Lemma: radical of a reductive group

FC16-C07-U031 · §19.5; p. 125 · after FC16-C07-U030, FC16-C06-U025, FC16-C07-U004

For reductive G, R(G)=Z(G)° is a torus and has finite intersection with (G,G).

Result: connected one-dimensional groups are commutative

FC16-C07-U032 · §20.1; pp. 126–127 · after FC16-C07-U010, FC16-C07-U027

Every connected one-dimensional algebraic group is commutative.

Definition: vector group

FC16-C07-U033 · §20.2; p. 127 · after FC16-C02-U003

A vector group is an algebraic group isomorphic to G_a^n.

Definition: e-group

FC16-C07-U034 · §20.2; p. 127 · after FC16-C07-U011, FC16-C07-U033

In positive characteristic, an e-group is a commutative unipotent algebraic group of exponent p.

Proposition: embedding e-groups into vector groups

FC16-C07-U035 · §20.2; pp. 127–128 · after FC16-C07-U034, FC16-C02-U030

Every e-group embeds as a closed subgroup of a vector group of dimension one larger.

Definition: p-polynomial

FC16-C07-U036 · §20.3; p. 129 · after FC16-C01-U001

In characteristic p, a p-polynomial is a K-linear combination of p^r-th powers of coordinate variables.

Lemma: additive polynomials are p-polynomials

FC16-C07-U037 · §20.3; p. 129 · after FC16-C07-U036, FC16-C07-U033

Every polynomial map K^n→K additive under vector addition is a p-polynomial.

Lemma: squarefree subgroup zero sets force p-polynomials

FC16-C07-U038 · §20.3; p. 129 · after FC16-C07-U037

If a squarefree polynomial has zero set an additive subgroup of K^n, then it is a p-polynomial.

Proposition: codimension-one vector subgroups are p-polynomial kernels

FC16-C07-U039 · §20.3; pp. 129–130 · after FC16-C07-U038, FC16-C07-U033

Every closed codimension-one subgroup of a vector group is the zero set/kernel of a p-polynomial.

Proposition: coordinate reduction of a p-polynomial homomorphism

FC16-C07-U040 · §20.4; p. 130 · after FC16-C07-U039

For n>1, a p-polynomial homomorphism K^n→G_a can be put by a vector-group automorphism into coordinates whose kernel contains the first coordinate line.

Corollary: normal form for connected vector subgroups

FC16-C07-U041 · §20.4; pp. 130–131 · after FC16-C07-U040

A closed connected subgroup of a vector group can be carried by an automorphism to a coordinate vector subgroup.

Theorem: classification of connected one-dimensional algebraic groups

FC16-C07-U042 · §20.5; p. 131 · after FC16-C07-U032, FC16-C07-U026, FC16-C07-U041, FC16-C06-U020

Over an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic, every connected one-dimensional algebraic group is isomorphic to G_a or G_m.
