Chapter I. Algebraic Geometry

FC16 — Humphreys, Linear Algebraic Groups.

This chapter contains 36 definitions, 21 theorems, 17 propositions, 4 lemmas, 5 corollaries, 1 example, 5 constructions, 1 convention and 5 other statements.

Nobody has checked this chapter against Lean yet, so the statements below say nothing about whether they are formalized.

Convention: base field and rings

FC16-C01-U001 · Chapter I opening; §0; p. 1

K is algebraically closed unless a smaller field is explicitly introduced; rings in §0 are commutative with identity.

Definition/result: noetherian rings and Hilbert basis theorem

FC16-C01-U002 · §0.1; p. 1 · after FC16-C01-U001

A ring is noetherian iff its ideals satisfy the standard finite-generation/ACC/maximal-element conditions; quotients are noetherian, and R noetherian implies R[T] noetherian.

Recorded result: polynomial rings over a field are UFDs

FC16-C01-U003 · §0.2; p. 1 · after FC16-C01-U001

For a field K, K[T_1,…,T_n] is a unique factorization domain.

Theorem/recorded result: weak Nullstellensatz

FC16-C01-U004 · §0.3; p. 1 · after FC16-C01-U001

If a finitely generated K-algebra K[x_1,…,x_n] is a field, then every x_i is algebraic over K.

Definition/result: algebraic independence and transcendence degree

FC16-C01-U005 · §0.4; pp. 1–2 · after FC16-C01-U001

A transcendence basis is a maximal algebraically independent subset; its cardinality is tr.deg. For finitely generated field extensions a transcendence basis may be chosen among the generators and the residual extension is finite algebraic.

Theorem/recorded result: Lüroth theorem

FC16-C01-U006 · §0.4; p. 2 · after FC16-C01-U005

Every proper intermediate field K⊊L⊆K(T) is itself a simple purely transcendental extension of K.

Definition/result: norm of a finite field extension

FC16-C01-U007 · §0.5; p. 2 · after FC16-C01-U001

For finite E/F, N_{E/F}(a) is the determinant of multiplication by a on the F-vector space E; it is multiplicative and agrees with the stated power/minimal-polynomial formula.

Definition/result: integral elements, integral closure, integrally closed domains

FC16-C01-U008 · §0.6; p. 2 · after FC16-C01-U001

x is integral over S iff it satisfies a monic polynomial, equivalently S[x] is finite as S-module; integral closure and integrally closed domains are defined accordingly, and polynomial rings over integrally closed domains remain integrally closed.

Theorem/recorded result: Noether normalization

FC16-C01-U009 · §0.7; p. 2 · after FC16-C01-U005, FC16-C01-U008

A finitely generated integral K-domain R of transcendence degree d is integral over a polynomial subalgebra K[y_1,…,y_d] on algebraically independent elements of R.

Theorem/recorded result: going up

FC16-C01-U010 · §0.8; p. 2 · after FC16-C01-U008

For an integral extension R/S, prime and maximal ideals of S lift to prime and maximal ideals of R.

Theorem/recorded result: going down

FC16-C01-U011 · §0.8; p. 2 · after FC16-C01-U008

If R/S is integral and S integrally closed, a chain P_1⊂P_2 under a prime Q_2 admits Q_1⊂Q_2 lying over P_1.

Theorem/recorded result: extension theorem for maps from integral extensions

FC16-C01-U012 · §0.8; pp. 2–3 · after FC16-C01-U008

A K-valued homomorphism on S extends across an integral extension R/S; the source also records the criterion for prescribing the value of one integral element.

Lemma/recorded result: finite prime avoidance

FC16-C01-U013 · §0.9; p. 3 · after FC16-C01-U001

If an ideal lies in a finite union of prime ideals, it lies in one of them.

Construction/result: generalized rings of quotients and local rings

FC16-C01-U014 · §0.10; p. 3 · after FC16-C01-U008, FC16-C01-U002

For a multiplicative set S, S^{-1}R is formed from fraction classes; primes correspond to primes of R disjoint from S. Localization R_P is local, preserves noetherianity and integral closedness in the stated cases, and gives the standard residue/tangent-quotient identifications.

Lemma/recorded result: Nakayama lemma

FC16-C01-U015 · §0.11; p. 3 · after FC16-C01-U001

If V is finitely generated and V=MV for a maximal ideal M, some x∉M annihilates V; over a local ring this forces V=0.

Definition/result: M-adic topology, Krull dimension, regular local rings

FC16-C01-U016 · §0.12; p. 3 · after FC16-C01-U002, FC16-C01-U008

The powers of the maximal ideal define the separated M-adic topology; Krull dimension is maximal prime-chain length; a local ring is regular when this equals the minimal number of maximal-ideal generators. Regular local rings are integrally closed domains.

Recorded result: minimal primes and nilpotent nilradical

FC16-C01-U017 · §0.13; p. 3 · after FC16-C01-U002

For I in a noetherian ring, the intersection of primes minimal over I maps to the nilradical of R/I, which is nilpotent.

Definition/results: separable and separably generated field extensions

FC16-C01-U018 · §0.14; pp. 3–4 · after FC16-C01-U001, FC16-C01-U005

The source defines separability in arbitrary characteristic via p-power linear independence and separably generated extensions; for finitely generated extensions the two notions agree, perfect bases give separability, and separability is transitive in towers.

Definition/result: field derivations and separability criterion

FC16-C01-U019 · §0.15; p. 4 · after FC16-C01-U005, FC16-C01-U018

An F-derivation E→L is additive, Leibniz, and vanishes on F. For separably generated E/F, Der_F(E,L) has dimension tr.deg_F E; separability is characterized by extension of derivations, and derivations vanish on pth powers in characteristic p.

Definition: affine space and affine variety

FC16-C01-U020 · §1.1; pp. 4–5 · after FC16-C01-U001

An=Kn; an affine algebraic set/variety is a common zero locus of finitely many polynomials, with V(I) and I(X) giving the ideal–zero-set correspondence.

Definition/result: radical ideals and vanishing ideals

FC16-C01-U021 · §1.1; p. 5 · after FC16-C01-U020, FC16-C01-U004

The radical √I consists of f with some power in I; I(V(I))=√I over K.

Theorem: Hilbert Nullstellensatz

FC16-C01-U022 · §1.1; p. 5 · after FC16-C01-U004, FC16-C01-U021

For every ideal I⊆K[T_1,…,T_n], I(V(I))=√I.

Definition/result: Zariski topology on affine space

FC16-C01-U023 · §1.2; pp. 6–7 · after FC16-C01-U020, FC16-C01-U021

Closed sets are algebraic zero loci; V converts sums to intersections and products/intersections to unions, giving the Zariski topology. Principal open sets form a useful basis.

Definition/result: noetherian topological spaces

FC16-C01-U024 · §1.2; pp. 6–7 · after FC16-C01-U002, FC16-C01-U023

A space is noetherian when closed subsets satisfy DCC; affine varieties are noetherian because their coordinate rings are.

Definition: irreducible space

FC16-C01-U025 · §1.3; p. 7 · after FC16-C01-U023

A nonempty space is irreducible when it is not a union of two proper closed subsets; equivalently any two nonempty opens meet, or every nonempty open is dense.

Proposition: closure and images preserve irreducibility

FC16-C01-U026 · §1.3; p. 7 · after FC16-C01-U025

A subspace is irreducible iff its closure is, and the continuous image of an irreducible space is irreducible.

Proposition/definition: finite irreducible decomposition in a noetherian space

FC16-C01-U027 · §1.3; pp. 7–8 · after FC16-C01-U024, FC16-C01-U025

Every nonempty noetherian space is a finite union of maximal irreducible closed subsets, uniquely determined up to order; these are its irreducible components.

Proposition: irreducible affine sets and prime ideals

FC16-C01-U028 · §1.3; p. 8 · after FC16-C01-U022, FC16-C01-U025

A closed affine set X is irreducible iff I(X) is prime.

Construction/result: product of affine varieties

FC16-C01-U029 · §1.4; pp. 8–9 · after FC16-C01-U020, FC16-C01-U022

For X⊆Am,Y⊆An, X×Y is affine with the Zariski product construction; K[X×Y] identifies with K[X]⊗_K K[Y].

Definition: affine algebra and polynomial functions

FC16-C01-U030 · §1.5; pp. 9–10 · after FC16-C01-U020, FC16-C01-U022

K[X]=K[T]/I(X) is the affine coordinate algebra; its elements are polynomial functions on X.

Definition/result: morphisms and comorphisms of affine varieties

FC16-C01-U031 · §1.5; pp. 9–11 · after FC16-C01-U030

A map X→Y is regular when its coordinates are polynomial functions. It induces a contravariant K-algebra map K[Y]→K[X], and every such K-algebra map arises uniquely this way.

Comparison: affine varieties and affine K-algebras

FC16-C01-U032 · §1.5; pp. 10–11 · after FC16-C01-U029, FC16-C01-U031

The source identifies affine varieties contravariantly with reduced finitely generated K-algebras and notes that affine products correspond to tensor-product coproducts.

Definition: projective space and homogeneous coordinates

FC16-C01-U033 · §1.6; p. 11 · after FC16-C01-U001

P^n is the set of one-dimensional subspaces of K^{n+1}, with homogeneous coordinates modulo nonzero scalar; P(V) denotes the projectivization of a vector space V.

Definition: projective algebraic sets and varieties

FC16-C01-U034 · §1.6; pp. 11–13 · after FC16-C01-U033, FC16-C01-U020

Projective closed sets are common zero loci of homogeneous polynomials; standard affine charts U_i≅A^n define their local affine structure.

Proposition: projective zero sets and homogeneous radical ideals

FC16-C01-U035 · §1.6; p. 12 · after FC16-C01-U022, FC16-C01-U034

Projective closed subsets correspond inclusion-reversingly to homogeneous radical ideals other than the irrelevant ideal.

Proposition/construction: Segre embedding and product of projective varieties

FC16-C01-U036 · §1.7; p. 13 · after FC16-C01-U034

The Segre map Pm×Pn→P^{mn+m+n} is a bijection onto a closed subset and takes products of projective varieties to projective varieties.

Construction: Grassmann varieties via Plücker coordinates

FC16-C01-U037 · §1.8; pp. 14–15 · after FC16-C01-U034

The d-dimensional subspaces of V embed in P(∧^dV) by the Plücker map; its image is closed, giving the Grassmann variety G_d(V).

Definition/construction: flags and flag variety

FC16-C01-U038 · §1.8; pp. 15–16 · after FC16-C01-U037, FC16-C01-U036

A flag is a strictly increasing chain of subspaces, full when successive dimensions increase by one. Full flags form a closed subvariety of a product of Grassmann varieties.

Definition/result: local ring of a point

FC16-C01-U039 · §2.1; pp. 16–17 · after FC16-C01-U014, FC16-C01-U030

For irreducible X and x∈X, O_x consists of rational functions defined at x; it is the localization K[X]_{I(x)}, with maximal ideal of functions vanishing at x.

Proposition: global affine functions as intersection of local rings

FC16-C01-U040 · §2.1; p. 17 · after FC16-C01-U039, FC16-C01-U022

For irreducible affine X, K[X]=∩_{x∈X}O_x inside K(X).

Construction: regular-function sheaf on affine varieties

FC16-C01-U041 · §2.1; p. 17 · after FC16-C01-U039

Regular functions on opens are locally quotients of polynomial functions with nonvanishing denominators; these assignments satisfy gluing and restriction and define the structure sheaf.

Definition: prevariety and affine open covering

FC16-C01-U042 · §2.2; pp. 17–18 · after FC16-C01-U041, FC16-C01-U024

A prevariety is a noetherian topological space with a sheaf of K-valued functions locally isomorphic to affine varieties; affine open subsets form a basis.

Definition: subprevariety and quasiprojective variety

FC16-C01-U043 · §2.2; p. 18 · after FC16-C01-U042, FC16-C01-U034

Locally closed subsets inherit prevariety structures; open subsets of projective varieties are quasiprojective.

Definition: function field of an irreducible prevariety

FC16-C01-U044 · §2.2; p. 18 · after FC16-C01-U042, FC16-C01-U030

The affine charts of an irreducible prevariety have canonically identified fraction fields; this common field is K(X).

Definition/result: morphisms of prevarieties

FC16-C01-U045 · §2.3; pp. 18–20 · after FC16-C01-U042, FC16-C01-U031

A morphism is continuous and pulls regular functions back to regular functions; it can be checked on affine covers and agrees with the affine comorphism notion.

Proposition: local criterion for a map to be a morphism

FC16-C01-U046 · §2.3; p. 19 · after FC16-C01-U045

Given compatible affine covers on source and target, a map is a morphism when pullbacks of regular functions are regular on the chosen source opens.

Construction: rational functions as maps on domains of definition

FC16-C01-U047 · §2.3; pp. 19–20 · after FC16-C01-U044, FC16-C01-U045

A rational function is regular on an open domain of definition and thereby gives a morphism to A^1; zero and nonzero loci are defined on that domain.

Definition: birational morphism and birational equivalence

FC16-C01-U048 · §2.3; p. 20 · after FC16-C01-U044, FC16-C01-U045

For irreducible prevarieties, a morphism is birational when the induced map on function fields is an isomorphism; varieties with isomorphic function fields are birationally equivalent.

Proposition/construction: product of affine prevarieties

FC16-C01-U049 · §2.4; pp. 20–21 · after FC16-C01-U029, FC16-C01-U042

For affine X,Y the cartesian product with tensor-product coordinate algebra has the expected regular functions and projections.

Theorem: existence of products of prevarieties

FC16-C01-U050 · §2.4; pp. 21–22 · after FC16-C01-U049, FC16-C01-U045

Products exist in the category of prevarieties by gluing products of affine charts and satisfy the categorical universal property.

Definition: variety via the diagonal/Hausdorff axiom

FC16-C01-U051 · §2.5; pp. 22–23 · after FC16-C01-U042, FC16-C01-U050

A prevariety is a variety when its diagonal is closed in X×X; equivalently morphisms into it have closed equality loci.

Lemma: affine-neighborhood criterion for a prevariety to be a variety

FC16-C01-U052 · §2.5; p. 23 · after FC16-C01-U051

If every pair of points of a prevariety lies in a common affine open subset, then it satisfies the diagonal axiom and is a variety.

Proposition: closed graphs and uniqueness on dense subsets

FC16-C01-U053 · §2.5; p. 23 · after FC16-C01-U051, FC16-C01-U045

For a morphism X→Y with Y a variety the graph is closed; two morphisms to a variety agreeing on a dense subset agree everywhere.

Definition/result: dimension of an irreducible variety

FC16-C01-U054 · §3.1; p. 24 · after FC16-C01-U005, FC16-C01-U044, FC16-C01-U027

For irreducible X, dim X is tr.deg_K K(X); arbitrary varieties have dimension equal to the maximum over irreducible components.

Proposition: dimension of a product

FC16-C01-U055 · §3.1; pp. 24–25 · after FC16-C01-U054, FC16-C01-U050

For irreducible X,Y, dim(X×Y)=dim X+dim Y.

Definition: codimension

FC16-C01-U056 · §3.2; p. 25 · after FC16-C01-U054
