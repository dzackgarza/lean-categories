### Theorem: Chevalley stabilizer theorem {#fc16-c04-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C04-U003` · §11.2; p. 80 · after [`FC16-C02-U030`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u030), [`FC16-C02-U028`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u028), [`FC16-C04-U002`](#fc16-c04-u002)

For closed H≤G there is a rational G-module V and a line L⊂V such that H is exactly the stabilizer of L and h is exactly its infinitesimal stabilizer.

### Construction: passage from line stabilizers to projective orbits {#fc16-c04-u004}

### Definition: character group {#fc16-c04-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C04-U005` · §11.4; p. 81 · after [`FC16-C02-U011`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u011), [`FC16-C02-U003`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u003)
:::

A character is a morphism of algebraic groups χ:G→G_m; characters form the abelian group X(G) under multiplication.

### Definition: semi-invariant and weight space {#fc16-c04-u006}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C04-U006` · §11.4; p. 81 · after [`FC16-C04-U005`](#fc16-c04-u005), [`FC16-C02-U013`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u013)
:::

For a rational G-module V and χ∈X(G), V_χ={v | g·v=χ(g)v}; nonzero elements are semi-invariants of weight χ.

### Lemma: independence of semi-invariant weight spaces {#fc16-c04-u007}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C04-U007` · §11.4; pp. 81–82 · after [`FC16-C04-U006`](#fc16-c04-u006)
:::

Distinct character weight spaces are linearly independent; only finitely many are nonzero in finite dimension.

### Theorem: closed normal subgroups as kernels of rational representations {#fc16-c04-u008}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C04-U008` · §11.5; p. 82 · after [`FC16-C04-U003`](#fc16-c04-u003), [`FC16-C04-U007`](#fc16-c04-u007), [`FC16-C02-U013`](c02.html#fc16-c02-u013)
:::

Every closed normal N◁G is the kernel of a rational representation ψ:G→GL(W), and n=Ker dψ.

### Construction: homogeneous space G/H {#fc16-c04-u009}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C04-U009` · §12.1; p. 83 · after [`FC16-C04-U004`](#fc16-c04-u004), [`FC16-C04-U003`](#fc16-c04-u003)
:::

For closed H≤G, embed H as a line stabilizer and take the orbit Y=G·[L]; the canonical map π:G→Y is surjective with fibres the left cosets of H.

### Theorem: universal mapping property of G/H {#fc16-c04-u010}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C04-U010` · §12.1; pp. 83–84 · after [`FC16-C04-U009`](#fc16-c04-u009), [`FC16-C01-U046`](c01.html#fc16-c01-u046)
:::

The pair (π,Y) is universal for morphisms from G constant on H-cosets: each such morphism factors uniquely through Y.

### Result: topology of the homogeneous-space quotient {#fc16-c04-u011}

The canonical quotient map π:G→G/H is open; the topology on G/H is the quotient topology for π.
