### Definition: abstract root system and abstract Weyl group {#fc16-c10-u001}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C10-U001` · §27.1; p. 163 · after [`FC16-C09-U031`](c09.html#fc16-c09-u031)
:::

A finite spanning subset Φ of a real vector space E, not containing 0, is a root system when only ±α are scalar multiples in Φ, each root has an associated reflection preserving Φ, and every reflection difference s_α(β)−β is an integral multiple of α. W(Φ) is generated by the root reflections.

### Theorem: root system of a semisimple algebraic group {#fc16-c10-u002}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C10-U002` · §27.1–27.2; pp. 163–165 · after [`FC16-C10-U001`](#fc16-c10-u001), [`FC16-C09-U031`](c09.html#fc16-c09-u031), [`FC16-C09-U006`](c09.html#fc16-c09-u006)
:::

For semisimple G and E=R⊗_Z X(T), Φ(G,T) is an abstract root system of rank rank G, and its abstract Weyl group identifies with W(G,T).

### Proposition: root subgroup shifts weights along root strings {#fc16-c10-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C10-U003` · §27.2; p. 165 · after [`FC16-C09-U033`](c09.html#fc16-c09-u033), [`FC16-C06-U026`](c06.html#fc16-c06-u026), [`FC16-C10-U002`](#fc16-c10-u002)

For a rational representation ρ:G→GL(V) with kernel contained in Z(G), if V_λ is a T-weight space then ρ(U_α)V_λ lies in the sum of V_{λ+kα} over integral k.

### Definition/result: base, simple roots and positive roots {#fc16-c10-u004}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C10-U004` · §27.3; pp. 165–166 · after [`FC16-C10-U002`](#fc16-c10-u002)
:::

A base Δ is a basis of the ambient root space such that every root has a unique integral expansion with coefficients all of one sign; its elements are simple roots and determine the positive roots. Bases exist and W acts simply transitively on them.

### Comparison: bases, abstract chambers, cocharacter chambers and Borel subgroups {#fc16-c10-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C10-U005` · §27.3; p. 166 · after [`FC16-C10-U004`](#fc16-c10-u004), [`FC16-C09-U026`](c09.html#fc16-c09-u026), [`FC16-C09-U004`](c09.html#fc16-c09-u004)
:::

For fixed maximal T, bases of Φ, Weyl chambers in the root and cocharacter spaces, and Borel subgroups containing T correspond naturally and are each permuted simply transitively by W.

### Theorem: generation by simple root rank-one subgroups {#fc16-c10-u006}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C10-U006` · §27.3; p. 166 · after [`FC16-C10-U004`](#fc16-c10-u004), [`FC16-C09-U031`](c09.html#fc16-c09-u031), [`FC16-C09-U033`](c09.html#fc16-c09-u033)

### Definition: diagram-stabilizing automorphism subgroup {#fc16-c10-u007}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C10-U007` · §27.4; p. 166 · after [`FC16-C10-U004`](#fc16-c10-u004), [`FC16-C08-U004`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u004), [`FC16-C08-U006`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u006)
:::

For semisimple G with fixed T⊆B, D≤Aut G consists of automorphisms stabilizing T and B; each induces an automorphism of the based root system, hence a Dynkin-diagram automorphism.

### Theorem: automorphisms of a semisimple group modulo inner automorphisms {#fc16-c10-u008}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C10-U008` · §27.4; pp. 166–167 · after [`FC16-C10-U007`](#fc16-c10-u007), [`FC16-C10-U006`](#fc16-c10-u006), [`FC16-C08-U005`](c08.html#fc16-c08-u005)

Aut G=(Int G)D, and the natural map from D to diagram automorphisms induces an injection Aut G/Int G into the finite diagram-automorphism group.

### Theorem: minimal normal factors of a semisimple group {#fc16-c10-u009}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC16-C10-U009` · §27.5; pp. 167–168 · after [`FC16-C05-U012`](c05.html#fc16-c05-u012), [`FC16-C07-U004`](c07.html#fc16-c07-u004), [`FC16-C10-U008`](#fc16-c10-u008), [`FC16-C10-U006`](#fc16-c10-u006)
:::

The minimal positive-dimensional closed connected normal subgroups G_i of semisimple G are finite in number, centralize one another, multiply onto G with finite kernel, and every closed connected normal subgroup is the product of the G_i it contains; moreover G=(G,G) and is generated by its root groups.

### Corollary: simple factors and irreducible root-system components {#fc16-c10-u010}

The almost-direct decomposition of semisimple G into its minimal connected normal factors corresponds exactly to the decomposition of Φ into irreducible root systems.

### Proposition: Bruhat decomposition in semisimple rank one {#fc16-c10-u016}

If G has semisimple rank one and s is the nontrivial Weyl element, then G=B ⊔ BsB. After fixing a representative n(s), every element of the nontrivial cell has a unique factorization u n(s)t u′ with the indicated rank-one unipotent factors and t∈T.

### Definition: Tits system (BN-pair) {#fc16-c10-u024}

::: {.unit-meta}

A Tits system (G,B,N,S) has G generated by B,N; T=B∩N normal in N; W=N/T generated by involutions S; and axioms (T1) sBw⊆BwB∪BswB and (T2) sBs is not contained in B. W is its Weyl group and |S| its rank.

Length ℓ(w) is minimal word length in S; for I⊆S let W_I=⟨I⟩ and P_I=BW_IB.

For every I⊆S, P_I is a subgroup; in particular P_S=G. The double cosets BwB are pairwise distinct.

For s∈S,w∈W, ℓ(sw)=ℓ(w)±1. The sign determines whether sBw lies in BswB alone or meets BwB, giving the basic double-coset length calculus.
