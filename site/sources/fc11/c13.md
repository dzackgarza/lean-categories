### Definition / construction / notation {#fc11-c13-u001}

The spin group of a quadratic vector space $( V , q )$ over a field $k$ is defined by means of its Clifford algebra ${ \mathsf { C } } ( q )$ . As a vector space this is just the exterior algebra $\Lambda ( V )$ and it contains $V$ . The algebra structure comes from $q$ . For us its importance lies in the action of the algebra conjugation. Firstly, conjugation with a non-isotropic vector $u \in V$ preserves $V$ and, secondly, in $V$ this gives the hyperplane reflection $\sigma _ { u }$ up to a sign. This property for $u$ can be extended to a ”large” subgroup of ${ \mathsf { C } } ( q )$ . To do this properly, one makes use of a canonical splitting of ${ \mathsf { C } } ( q )$ into even and odd elements. The even elements form a subalgebra, the even Clifford algebra. The conjugation with $u$ receives a sign according to whether $u$ is odd or even. The Clifford group consists of the invertible elements $u$ of the Clifford algebra which have the property that twisted conjugation with $u$ preserves $V$ .
