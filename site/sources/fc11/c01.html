Remark 1.1.5

FC11-C01-U015 · Chapter 1, source L458 · not formalized

Remark 1.1.5. The technique used in the proof of the preceding proposition shows that every quadratic form in characteristic different from 2 is diagonalizable; if there is a non-zero radical, say of dimension \(n\) , all diagonalized forms have precisely \(n\) zeros on the diagonal. If \(k\) is a field of characteristic 2, this is no longer true. Consider for example the so-called hyperbolic plane over \(k\) , \(U = k \cdot e \oplus k \cdot f\) with bilinear form given by \(b ( e , e ) = b ( f , f ) = 0\) , \(b ( e , f ) = 1\) . Since \(b ( x , x ) = 0\) for all \(x \in U\) , this form is not diagonalizable.

Not formalized. Degenerate diagonalization is available. The characteristic-two hyperbolic example is defined and proved alternating/nonzero, but its non-diagonalizability and the complete nullity comparison are not packaged by the inspected counterexample theorem (which instead disproves injectivity of polarization).

Definition / construction / notation

FC11-C01-U016 · Chapter 1, source L460 · not formalized

Finally a word about the comparison of forms. A metric linear map between two \(k\) -vector spaces \(V , V ^ { \prime }\) equipped with symmetric bilinear forms, say \(( V , b )\) and \(( V ^ { \prime } , b ^ { \prime } )\) , is a linear map \(f\) for which \(b ^ { \prime } ( f ( x ) , f ( y ) ) = b ( x , y )\) for all \(x , y \in V\) . If \(b\) is non-degenerate, \(f\) is injective. If, moreover, \(f\) is bijective, then \(b ^ { \prime }\) is also nondegenerate and \(f\) is called an isometry. Two inner product spaces are said to be isometric if there exists an isometry between them.

Not formalized. Form-preserving linear maps and categorical isomorphisms exist. This bundled paragraph additionally proves injectivity from nondegeneracy of the source and transfers nondegeneracy through a bijection; the map constructor alone is not that result.
