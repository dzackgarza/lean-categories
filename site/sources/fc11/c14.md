As explained in Section 14.1, the appropriate way to compare integral lattices in a given genus is to consider these as a full rank lattice of a fixed (quadratic) inner product space over the rational numbers, say $V$ . Observe that this fixes the signature so that the place at infinity does not play a role. Isometric lattices $L , L ^ { \prime }$ have isometric localizations $L _ { v } \simeq L _ { v } ^ { \prime }$ and so by definition belong to the same genus and belong to the localization $V _ { v }$ of $V$ .

When lattices in one genus are identified inside a fixed rational quadratic space $V$, two sublattices are called equivalent if a rational isometry of $V$ sends one to the other; they are genus-equivalent when this holds locally at every prime.

If we follow the preceding convention, first of all, two sublattices $L ^ { \prime } , L ^ { \prime \prime }$ of $V$ are isometric precisely if there is a vector space isometry sending $L ^ { \prime }$ to $L ^ { \prime \prime }$ in which case we call $L ^ { \prime }$ and $L ^ { \prime \prime }$ equivalent lattices.
