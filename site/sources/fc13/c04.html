Definition: valuation ring

FC13-C04-U001 · §10, pp. 71–72 · in Mathlib

A domain R with fraction field K is a valuation ring if for every x∈K, x∈R or x⁻¹∈R. Its ideals are totally ordered by inclusion, so R is local; the field itself is the trivial valuation ring.

Formalized. Mathlib/RingTheory/Valuation/ValuationRing.lean::ValuationRing, iff_ideal_total, isInteger_or_isInteger, isLocalRing — Mathlib’s valuation-ring class is equivalent to total divisibility/total ordering of ideals; in a fraction field every x or x⁻¹ is integral over the ring interface, and valuation rings are local. A field carries the trivial valuation-ring instance.

Searched: P=db584cd6d46c92f209a44c0f1c829460d327499d

Theorem: Theorem 10.1

FC13-C04-U002 · §10, p. 72 · after FC13-C04-U001 · not formalized

If R⊊R′⊆K are valuation rings with maximal ideals m,p, then p⊂m⊂R⊂R′ and p≠m; p is prime in R and R′=R_p; R/p is a valuation ring of Frac(R′/p); and inverse images of valuation subrings of R′/p give valuation rings of K contained in R′.

Not formalized. Pinned Mathlib classifies valuation-subring coarsenings by prime ideals (ValuationSubring.primeSpectrumOrderEquiv) and realizes the overring as localization, but no checked declaration packages the quotient valuation ring and inverse-image/composite clauses of Matsumura 10.1 in one exact bundle.

Definition: composite of valuation rings

FC13-C04-U003 · §10, p. 72 · after FC13-C04-U002 · not formalized

The inverse-image construction in Theorem 10.1(iv) is the composite valuation ring; every valuation ring of K contained in R′ arises as such a composite.

Not formalized. The inverse-image ingredients and coarsening order are present, but no pinned/current/open/external declaration defines Matsumura’s composite valuation ring and proves that every contained valuation ring arises that way.

Definition: domination of local rings

FC13-C04-U004 · §10, p. 72 · after FC13-C01-U017 · in Mathlib

For local rings R⊇S, R dominates S if m_R∩S=m_S.

Formalized. Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/LocalSubring.lean::LocalSubring, le_def — LocalSubring is ordered precisely by inclusion together with IsLocalHom; the file documents this as domination, equivalently contraction of the dominating maximal ideal to the dominated maximal ideal.

Searched: P=db584cd6d46c92f209a44c0f1c829460d327499d

Theorem: Theorem 10.2

FC13-C04-U005 · §10, pp. 72–73 · after FC13-C02-U021, FC13-C04-U001 · in Mathlib

For a field K, subring A⊆K and p∈Spec A, there exists a valuation ring R of K containing A whose centre on A is p, i.e. m_R∩A=p.

Formalized. Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/LocalSubring.lean::LocalSubring.ofPrime; Mathlib/RingTheory/Valuation/LocalSubring.lean::LocalSubring.exists_le_valuationSubring — Localizing A at p gives a local subring with center p, and every local subring of a field is dominated by a valuation subring. Its maximal ideal therefore contracts to p, exactly Matsumura 10.2.

Searched: P=db584cd6d46c92f209a44c0f1c829460d327499d

Theorem: Theorem 10.3

FC13-C04-U006 · §10, p. 73 · after FC13-C03-U060, FC13-C04-U001 · in Mathlib

Every valuation ring is integrally closed.

Formalized. Mathlib/RingTheory/Valuation/LocalSubring.lean::IsIntegrallyClosed — Every valuation subring of a field has an IsIntegrallyClosed instance; transporting along the fraction-field embedding gives the source theorem for valuation rings.

Searched: P=db584cd6d46c92f209a44c0f1c829460d327499d

Theorem: Theorem 10.4

FC13-C04-U007 · §10, p. 73 · after FC13-C03-U060, FC13-C04-U005, FC13-C04-U006 · in Mathlib

The integral closure of A in a field K is the intersection of all valuation rings of K containing A.

Formalized. Mathlib/RingTheory/Valuation/LocalSubring.lean::Subring.eq_iInf_of_isIntegrallyClosedIn — The pinned theorem states that an integrally closed subring of a field is exactly the infimum/intersection of all valuation subrings containing it. Applied to the integral closure of A in K, this is Matsumura 10.4.

Definition: centre and Zariski space of valuations

FC13-C04-U008 · §10, pp. 73–74 · after FC13-C04-U005 · not formalized

A valuation ring R of K containing A has centre m_R∩A on A. Zar(K,A) is the set of such valuation rings, called the Zariski space or Zariski Riemann surface of K over A.

Not formalized. Valuation subrings and centers are formalized, but no checked declaration packages the set Zar(K,A) of valuation overrings with center map as a named source-shaped object.

Definition: ordered abelian group

FC13-C04-U011 · §10, p. 75 · in Mathlib

An abelian group H with a total order is ordered when translation preserves the order; adjoin ∞ larger than every element, with ∞+a=∞.

Formalized. Mathlib/Algebra/Order/Group/Defs.lean::AddCommGroup, IsOrderedAddMonoid; Mathlib/Algebra/Order/Monoid/Unbundled/WithTop.lean::WithTop — Mathlib separates the additive commutative group, linear order, and translation-invariance typeclasses; WithTop adjoins the absorbing top element ∞ with the expected ordered-additive operations.

Searched: P=db584cd6d46c92f209a44c0f1c829460d327499d

Definition: additive valuation and value group

FC13-C04-U012 · §10, p. 75 · in Mathlib

An additive valuation v:K→H∪{∞} satisfies v(xy)=v(x)+v(y), v(x+y)≥min(v(x),v(y)), and v(x)=∞ iff x=0. The image of K* is its value group.

Formalized. Mathlib/RingTheory/Valuation/Basic.lean::AddValuation, ValueGroup, ValueGroup₀ — AddValuation is the additive presentation of valuations with top, using min for the ultrametric inequality. ValueGroup/ValueGroup₀ restrict a valuation to the subgroup generated by its nonzero image, exactly the intrinsic value-group construction.

Searched: P=db584cd6d46c92f209a44c0f1c829460d327499d

Construction: valuation ring attached to an additive valuation

FC13-C04-U013 · §10, p. 75 · after FC13-C04-U001, FC13-C04-U012 · in Mathlib

For v, R_v={x | v(x)≥0} is a valuation ring and m_v={x | v(x)>0} its maximal ideal. Conversely every valuation ring yields such a valuation with value group K/R; valuations with the same valuation ring differ by an order-isomorphism of value groups.

Formalized. Mathlib/RingTheory/Valuation/ValuationSubring.lean::ValuationSubring.valuation, integer_valuation, isEquiv_iff_valuationSubring; Mathlib/RingTheory/Valuation/Basic.lean::Valuation.IsEquiv.orderMonoidIso — A valuation subring has a canonical valuation whose integer ring is exactly the subring; equal valuation subrings are equivalent valuations, and equivalent valuations induce an ordered isomorphism of their intrinsic value groups.

Searched: P=db584cd6d46c92f209a44c0f1c829460d327499d

Definition: Archimedean ordered group

FC13-C04-U014 · §10, p. 76 · after FC13-C04-U011 · not formalized

An ordered group is Archimedean if it is order-isomorphic to a subgroup of the additive real numbers.

Not formalized. Mathlib has an Archimedean typeclass using the multiples/cofinality condition, but no checked theorem identifies an arbitrary linearly ordered abelian group satisfying it with a subgroup of (ℝ,+) as Matsumura uses for the definition.

Theorem: Theorem 10.6

FC13-C04-U015 · §10, p. 76 · after FC13-C04-U014 · not formalized

An ordered group G is Archimedean iff for all a>0 and b∈G there exists n∈N with na>b.

Not formalized. Archimedean/exists_lt_nsmul formalize the right-hand multiples condition, but without the Hölder embedding theorem of U014 there is no checked iff with Matsumura’s subgroup-of-ℝ definition.

Definition: rank and rational rank of an ordered group

FC13-C04-U016 · §10, p. 77 · after FC13-C04-U014 · not formalized

A nonzero ordered group has rank one if it is order-isomorphic to a subgroup of R. Its rational rank is the maximal number of elements linearly independent over Z after such an embedding.

Not formalized. Valuation rank-one classes exist, but no compatible declaration defines Matsumura’s ordered-group rank together with rational rank as ℤ-linear rank after an embedding in ℝ.

Example: rank one need not rational rank one

FC13-C04-U017 · §10, p. 77 · after FC13-C04-U016 · not formalized

Z+Z√2⊂R has ordered-group rank one and rational rank two.

Not formalized. No checked declaration owns the ordered subgroup ℤ+ℤ√2 ⊂ ℝ with the source rank and rational-rank computations.

Theorem: Theorem 10.7

FC13-C04-U018 · §10, p. 77 · after FC13-C02-U039, FC13-C04-U013, FC13-C04-U016 · not formalized

For a valuation ring R with value group G, G has rank one iff R has Krull dimension one.

Definition: discrete valuation ring

FC13-C04-U019 · §11, p. 78 · after FC13-C04-U001, FC13-C04-U013 · not formalized

A valuation ring whose value group is isomorphic to Z is a discrete valuation ring (DVR).

Not formalized. Mathlib defines IsDiscreteValuationRing as a local PID that is not a field and proves its associated valuation is rank-one discrete with value group order-isomorphic to ℤ. No checked converse starts from Matsumura’s valuation-ring/value-group-ℤ definition, so the source definition is not directly represented.

Definition: normalised valuation of a DVR

FC13-C04-U020 · §11, p. 78 · after FC13-C04-U019 · in Mathlib

For a DVR R, the additive valuation with value group Z is its normalised additive valuation.

Formalized. Mathlib/RingTheory/Valuation/Discrete/IsDiscreteValuationRing.lean::IsDiscreteValuationRing.isRankOneDiscrete, IsDiscreteValuationRing.maximalIdeal; Mathlib/RingTheory/Valuation/Discrete/RankOne.lean::valueGroup₀_equiv_withZeroMulInt; Mathlib/RingTheory/DedekindDomain/AdicValuation.lean::intValuation, valuation — A DVR’s maximal ideal gives a discrete rank-one valuation; its intrinsic value group is canonically order-isomorphic to integers (with zero added), and the adic/int valuation realizes the normalized integer exponent convention.

Theorem: Theorem 11.1

FC13-C04-U021 · §11, pp. 78–79 · after FC13-C01-U027, FC13-C01-U052, FC13-C04-U019 · in Mathlib
