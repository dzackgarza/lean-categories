### Definition: bilinear map {#fc13-ca-u001}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U001` · Appendix A, p. 266
:::

For A-modules M,N,L, a map M×N→L is bilinear when it is A-linear in each argument separately; T_A(M,N;L) denotes the A-module of bilinear maps.

### Definition/construction: tensor product {#fc13-ca-u002}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U002` · Appendix A, p. 266 · after [`FC13-CA-U001`](#fc13-ca-u001)
:::

The tensor product M⊗_A N is characterized by a universal bilinear map M×N→M⊗_A N. It exists as the quotient of the free A-module on M×N by the bilinearity relations.

### Formula: Formula 1 (tensor universal property) {#fc13-ca-u003}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U003` · Appendix A, p. 266 · after [`FC13-CA-U002`](#fc13-ca-u002)
:::

Hom_A(M⊗_A N,L)≅T_A(M,N;L) naturally.

### Construction/formula: multilinear tensor products and Formula 2 {#fc13-ca-u004}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U004` · Appendix A, pp. 266–267 · after [`FC13-CA-U002`](#fc13-ca-u002)
:::

The r-fold tensor product represents r-multilinear maps. Tensor product is associative: (M⊗M′)⊗M″≅M⊗M′⊗M″≅M⊗(M′⊗M″).

### Formulas: Formulas 3–5 {#fc13-ca-u005}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U005` · Appendix A, p. 267 · after [`FC13-CA-U002`](#fc13-ca-u002)
:::

There are canonical isomorphisms M⊗N≅N⊗M, M⊗A≅M, and (⊕_λ M_λ)⊗N≅⊕_λ(M_λ⊗N).

### Formula: Formula 6 (tensor product of maps) {#fc13-ca-u006}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U006` · Appendix A, p. 267 · after [`FC13-CA-U002`](#fc13-ca-u002)
:::

Linear maps f:M→M′ and g:N→N′ induce f⊗g:M⊗N→M′⊗N′ with (f⊗g)(Σx_i⊗y_i)=Σf(x_i)⊗g(y_i).

### Formula: Formula 7 (tensor quotient for two surjections) {#fc13-ca-u007}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U007` · Appendix A, p. 267 · after [`FC13-CA-U006`](#fc13-ca-u006)
:::

For exact 0→K→M→M′→0 and 0→L→N→N′→0, M′⊗N′ is (M⊗N) modulo the sum of the images K⊗N and M⊗L.

### Formula: Formula 8 (right exactness of tensor product) {#fc13-ca-u008}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U008` · Appendix A, pp. 267–268 · after [`FC13-CA-U007`](#fc13-ca-u007)
:::

If M₁→M₂→M₃→0 is exact, then M₁⊗N→M₂⊗N→M₃⊗N→0 is exact.

### Definition/lemma: split exactness and flat modules {#fc13-ca-u009}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U009` · Appendix A, p. 268 · after [`FC13-CA-U008`](#fc13-ca-u008)
:::

Tensoring preserves split injections. An A-module N is flat precisely when −⊗_A N is exact.

### Definition: two-sided A–B module and scalar action on tensor products {#fc13-ca-u010}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U010` · Appendix A, p. 268
:::

For an A–B-bimodule P, M⊗_A P is naturally a B-module by (Σy_i⊗x_i)b=Σy_i⊗(x_i b).

### Formula: Formula 9 (Hom–tensor adjunction for bimodules) {#fc13-ca-u011}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U011` · Appendix A, p. 268 · after [`FC13-CA-U010`](#fc13-ca-u010)
:::

For A–B-bimodule P, A-module M and B-module N, Hom_A(M,Hom_B(P,N))≅Hom_B(M⊗_A P,N).

### Formula: Formula 10 (associativity under change of rings) {#fc13-ca-u012}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U012` · Appendix A, p. 268 · after [`FC13-CA-U010`](#fc13-ca-u010)
:::

For compatible bimodules, (M⊗_A P)⊗_B N≅M⊗_A(P⊗_B N).

### Definition: extension of scalars {#fc13-ca-u013}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U013` · Appendix A, p. 268 · after [`FC13-CA-U010`](#fc13-ca-u010)
:::

For a ring homomorphism A→B and A-module M, M⊗_A B is the B-module obtained by extension of scalars, also denoted M_B.

### Formula: Formula 11 (tensor product commutes with scalar extension) {#fc13-ca-u014}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U014` · Appendix A, p. 268 · after [`FC13-CA-U012`](#fc13-ca-u012), [`FC13-CA-U013`](#fc13-ca-u013)
:::

For A-modules M,M′ and A→B, (M⊗_A B)⊗_B(M′⊗_A B)≅(M⊗_A M′)⊗_A B.

### Construction: tensor product of A-algebras {#fc13-ca-u015}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U015` · Appendix A, p. 269 · after [`FC13-CA-U002`](#fc13-ca-u002)
:::

For A-algebras B,C, the A-module B⊗_A C is an A-algebra with (b⊗c)(b′⊗c′)=bb′⊗cc′; it is generated by B⊗1 and 1⊗C.

### Examples: quotient and polynomial base change {#fc13-ca-u016}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U016` · Appendix A, p. 269 · after [`FC13-CA-U015`](#fc13-ca-u015)
:::

For an ideal a⊲A, B⊗_A(A/a)≅B/aB. Also B⊗_A A[X]≅B[X], and similarly in several variables.

### Definition: directed set {#fc13-ca-u017}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U017` · Appendix A, p. 269
:::

A poset Λ is directed if any two elements admit a common upper bound; finite subsets ordered by inclusion are the standard non-total example.

### Definition: direct system {#fc13-ca-u018}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U018` · Appendix A, pp. 269–270 · after [`FC13-CA-U017`](#fc13-ca-u017)
:::

A direct system indexed by a directed set consists of objects M_λ and transition maps f_{μλ}:M_λ→M_μ for λ≤μ satisfying identity and composition laws; the definition applies to sets, modules, rings, and categories.

### Definition: morphism of direct systems {#fc13-ca-u019}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U019` · Appendix A, p. 270 · after [`FC13-CA-U018`](#fc13-ca-u018)
:::

A morphism of direct systems with the same index set is a compatible family of maps commuting with all transition maps.

### Definition/construction: direct limit {#fc13-ca-u020}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U020` · Appendix A, p. 270 · after [`FC13-CA-U018`](#fc13-ca-u018)
:::

The direct limit lim→M_λ is characterized by the universal property for compatible maps out of the system. It exists as the quotient of the disjoint union by eventual equality; module and ring structures descend when present.

### Theorem: Theorem A1 {#fc13-ca-u021}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U021` · Appendix A, pp. 270–271 · after [`FC13-CA-U020`](#fc13-ca-u020)
:::

Tensor product commutes with direct limits: lim→(M_λ⊗_A N)≅(lim→M_λ)⊗_A N.

### Theorem: Theorem A2 {#fc13-ca-u022}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U022` · Appendix A, p. 271 · after [`FC13-CA-U020`](#fc13-ca-u020)
:::

Direct limits of A-modules are exact: a levelwise exact sequence of three direct systems yields an exact sequence of their direct limits.

### Construction: module as direct limit of finitely generated submodules {#fc13-ca-u023}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U023` · Appendix A, p. 271 · after [`FC13-CA-U020`](#fc13-ca-u020)
:::

Every A-module is the direct limit of its finitely generated submodules ordered by inclusion.

### Construction: ring as direct limit of Noetherian subrings {#fc13-ca-u024}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U024` · Appendix A, p. 271 · after [`FC13-CA-U020`](#fc13-ca-u020)
:::

Every ring is a direct limit of subrings finitely generated over its minimal subring; these subrings are Noetherian.

### Definition/construction: inverse system and inverse limit {#fc13-ca-u025}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U025` · Appendix A, pp. 271–272 · after [`FC13-CA-U017`](#fc13-ca-u017)
:::

Inverse systems and inverse limits are the arrow-reversed notions. The inverse limit is the compatible-subset of the direct product ∏M_λ and inherits module/ring structure.

### Example: p-adic integers and I-adic completion {#fc13-ca-u026}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U026` · Appendix A, p. 272 · after [`FC13-CA-U025`](#fc13-ca-u025)
:::

The inverse limit lim← Z/(p^n) is the ring of p-adic integers. More generally lim← A/I^n is the I-adic completion of A.

### Counterexample: inverse limit is left exact but not exact {#fc13-ca-u027}

::: {.unit-meta}
`FC13-CA-U027` · Appendix A, pp. 272–273 · after [`FC13-CA-U025`](#fc13-ca-u025)
:::

Inverse limit of modules is left exact in general but not right exact; the source gives an inverse system of exact rows whose inverse-limit map fails surjectivity.
