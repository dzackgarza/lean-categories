Chapter 3. Limits and Colimits (pp. 73–114)

FC03 — Riehl, Category Theory in Context.

This chapter contains 19 definitions, 10 theorems, 12 propositions, 7 lemmas, 3 corollaries, 31 examples, 2 constructions, 6 remarks, 1 convention and 2 other statements.

Nobody has checked this chapter against Lean yet, so the statements below say nothing about whether they are formalized.

Definition: For c∈C, the constant functor c:J→C is constant on c and 1c

FC03-C03-U001 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2409 · after FC03-C01-U028, FC03-C01-U102

For c∈C, the constant functor c:J→C is constant on c and 1_c; these assemble into the diagonal embedding Δ:C→C^J.

Definition: A cone over F:J→C with summit c is a natural transformation c⇒F with legs c→Fj

FC03-C03-U002 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2411 · after FC03-C03-U001, FC03-C01-U049

A cone over F:J→C with summit c is a natural transformation c⇒F with legs c→Fj; a cocone/cone under F with nadir c is F⇒c.

Definition: A limit of F represents Cone(-,F):C^op→Set, with universal cone lim F⇒F;…

FC03-C03-U003 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2441 · after FC03-C02-U003, FC03-C03-U002, FC03-C02-U010

A limit of F represents Cone(-,F):C^op→Set, with universal cone lim F⇒F; dually a colimit represents Cone(F,-), with universal cocone F⇒colim F.

Definition: Equivalently, a limit is terminal in the category of cones over F and a…

FC03-C03-U004 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2467 · after FC03-C03-U003, FC03-C02-U036, FC03-C01-U092

Equivalently, a limit is terminal in the category of cones over F and a colimit initial in the category of cocones under F; cone morphisms are maps of summits/nadirs commuting with legs.

Convention/notation

FC03-C03-U005 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2475 · after FC03-C03-U003

Write lim_J F and colim_J F when the indexing shape matters; “limit cone” or “colimit cone” includes the universal cone data.

Proposition: Any two limit cones over one diagram have a unique isomorphism between…

FC03-C03-U006 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2479 · after FC03-C03-U004, FC03-C02-U018

Any two limit cones over one diagram have a unique isomorphism between summits commuting with all legs; dually for colimit cones.

Remark: A limit diagram is F together with its limit cone and is indexed by J with a…

FC03-C03-U007 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2485 · after FC03-C03-U002, FC03-C03-U003

A limit diagram is F together with its limit cone and is indexed by J with a freely adjoined initial object; dually a colimit diagram adjoins a terminal object.

Definition: A product is a limit of a discrete diagram

FC03-C03-U008 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2489 · after FC03-C03-U003

A product is a limit of a discrete diagram; projections π_k:∏_jF_j→F_k satisfy C(c,∏F_j)≅∏_k C(c,F_k).

Example: Topological products are categorical products with the coarsest topology…

FC03-C03-U009 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2501 · after FC03-C03-U008, FC03-C01-U003

Topological products are categorical products with the coarsest topology making projections continuous; for infinite families the box topology generally fails the universal property.

Definition: A terminal object is the nullary product, i.e

FC03-C03-U010 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2525 · after FC03-C03-U008, FC03-C01-U092

A terminal object is the nullary product, i.e. the limit of the empty diagram.

Example: The category 1 is terminal in Cat and CAT.

FC03-C03-U011 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2527 · after FC03-C03-U010, FC03-C01-U043

The category 1 is terminal in Cat and CAT.

Definition: The equalizer of f,g:A⇉B is the universal h:E→A with fh=gh

FC03-C03-U012 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2529 · after FC03-C03-U003

The equalizer of f,g:A⇉B is the universal h:E→A with fh=gh; every other equalizing a:C→A factors uniquely through h.

Example: For groups, ker φ is the equalizer of φ and the trivial homomorphism

FC03-C03-U013 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2543 · after FC03-C03-U012

For groups, ker φ is the equalizer of φ and the trivial homomorphism; Eq(φ,ψ)={g|φg=ψg}, and for abelian H it is ker(φ-ψ).

Definition: A pullback/fiber product B×A C is the universal commutative square over a…

FC03-C03-U014 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2549 · after FC03-C03-U003, FC03-C03-U002

A pullback/fiber product B×_A C is the universal commutative square over a cospan B→A←C; pulling back a generalized element defines a fiber.

Example: For ρ:R→S¹, t↦e^{2πit}, the fiber/pullback over 1∈S¹ is the discrete…

FC03-C03-U015 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2579 · after FC03-C03-U014

For ρ:R→S¹, t↦e^{2πit}, the fiber/pullback over 1∈S¹ is the discrete subspace Z⊂R.

Example: In Ab, the pullback of multiplication-by-m and multiplication-by-n on Z…

FC03-C03-U016 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2589 · after FC03-C03-U014

In Ab, the pullback of multiplication-by-m and multiplication-by-n on Z consists of (x,y) with nx=my and is Z with legs determined by an lcm when m,n are not both zero.

Definition: An inverse limit is the limit of a tower indexed by ω^op (more generally a…

FC03-C03-U017 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2601 · after FC03-C03-U003, FC03-C01-U016

An inverse limit is the limit of a tower indexed by ω^op (more generally a limit ordinal opposite); “inverse limit” is sometimes used for arbitrary limits.

Example: The p-adic integers are the inverse limit Zp=limn Z/p^n along canonical…

FC03-C03-U018 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2613 · after FC03-C03-U017

The p-adic integers are the inverse limit Z_p=lim_n Z/p^n along canonical quotient maps.

Definition: Dual limit notions

FC03-C03-U019 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2621 · after FC03-C03-U008, FC03-C03-U010, FC03-C03-U012, FC03-C03-U014, FC03-C03-U017, FC03-C01-U019

Dual limit notions: coproduct (discrete colimit), initial object (empty colimit), coequalizer, pushout, and sequential/direct colimit (ω-indexed colimit), with coproduct injections and the stated universal properties.

Example: S¹∨S¹ is a pushout in Top (and coproduct in based spaces)

FC03-C03-U020 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2653 · after FC03-C03-U019

S¹∨S¹ is a pushout in Top (and coproduct in based spaces); attaching D² by the commutator loop via pushout yields the torus.

Example: The cokernel of a group homomorphism φ:G→H is the coequalizer of φ and the…

FC03-C03-U021 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2661 · after FC03-C03-U019

The cokernel of a group homomorphism φ:G→H is the coequalizer of φ and the trivial map, i.e. H modulo the normal closure of im φ.

Example: A sequential colimit of nested sets is their union

FC03-C03-U022 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2663 · after FC03-C03-U019

A sequential colimit of nested sets is their union; a CW complex is the colimit of its skeleta.

Remark: Maps out of a coproduct and into a product are naturally families of…

FC03-C03-U023 · §3.1, pp. 74–83; L2673 · after FC03-C03-U008, FC03-C03-U019

Maps out of a coproduct and into a product are naturally families of component maps; hence a map coproduct→product is a matrix of component maps. In Ab/Mod_R/Ch_R finite coproducts and products are canonically isomorphic direct sums, with composition by matrix multiplication.

Definition: A diagram is small when its indexing category is small

FC03-C03-U024 · §3.2, pp. 84–89; L2761 · after FC03-C01-U005, FC03-C03-U003

A diagram is small when its indexing category is small; C is complete if it has all small limits and cocomplete if it has all small colimits.

Definition: For small F:J→Set, define lim F=Cone(1,F)

FC03-C03-U025 · §3.2, pp. 84–89; L2777 · after FC03-C03-U002, FC03-C03-U003, FC03-C03-U024

For small F:J→Set, define lim F=Cone(1,F); its j-th projection sends a cone μ:1⇒F to its leg μ_j.

Remark: Smallness of J ensures Cone(1,F) is a set because Set^J is locally small.

FC03-C03-U026 · §3.2, pp. 84–89; L2791 · after FC03-C03-U025, FC03-C01-U103

Smallness of J ensures Cone(1,F) is a set because Set^J is locally small.

Theorem: Set is complete

FC03-C03-U027 · §3.2, pp. 84–89; L2795 · after FC03-C03-U025

Set is complete: the construction lim F=Cone(1,F) is universal for every small set-valued diagram.

Example: Products in Set are ordinary cartesian products of indexed families.

FC03-C03-U028 · §3.2, pp. 84–89; L2819 · after FC03-C03-U027, FC03-C03-U008

Products in Set are ordinary cartesian products of indexed families.

Example: The terminal object in Set is any singleton set.

FC03-C03-U029 · §3.2, pp. 84–89; L2825 · after FC03-C03-U027, FC03-C03-U010

The terminal object in Set is any singleton set.

Example: The equalizer of f,g:X⇉Y in Set is {x∈X | f(x)=g(x)}.

FC03-C03-U030 · §3.2, pp. 84–89; L2827 · after FC03-C03-U027, FC03-C03-U012

Example: The inverse limit of a tower of sets is the set of coherent sequences (xn)…

FC03-C03-U031 · §3.2, pp. 84–89; L2833 · after FC03-C03-U027, FC03-C03-U017

The inverse limit of a tower of sets is the set of coherent sequences (x_n) with transition equations f_{n,n-1}(x_n)=x_{n-1}.

Example: The pullback in Set is B×A C={(b,c)∈B×C | f(b)=g(c)}.

FC03-C03-U032 · §3.2, pp. 84–89; L2843 · after FC03-C03-U027, FC03-C03-U014

Example: For a left G-set X:BG→Set, lim X is the fixed-point set X^G.

FC03-C03-U033 · §3.2, pp. 84–89; L2855 · after FC03-C03-U027, FC03-C01-U036

For a left G-set X:BG→Set, lim X is the fixed-point set X^G.

Construction/comparison

FC03-C03-U034 · §3.2, pp. 84–89; L2861 · after FC03-C03-U028, FC03-C03-U030, FC03-C03-U032

A pullback B×_A C in Set is the equalizer of the two maps B×C⇉A induced by f and g.

For idempotent e:A→A in Set, lim e=A^e=Eq(1,e); the factorization e=sr splits e with rs=1, and conversely every retract yields a split idempotent.

Definition: For a specified class of diagrams, F:C→D preserves limits by sending limit…

For a specified class of diagrams, F:C→D preserves limits by sending limit cones to limit cones, reflects them by detecting limit cones, and creates them by lifting codomain limits to domain limits while reflecting the class; dual definitions apply to colimits.

If F creates a class of limits and D has them, then C has them and F preserves them; dually for colimits.

Definition: A presheaf F:O(X)^op→Set is a sheaf when it carries every open-cover colimit…

Lemma: Every full and faithful functor reflects all limits and colimits that exist…

Every full and faithful functor reflects all limits and colimits that exist in its codomain.

Lemma: Every equivalence of categories preserves, reflects, and creates all limits…

FC03-C03-U042 · §3.3, pp. 90–92; L2999 · after FC03-C01-U057, FC03-C03-U038

Every equivalence of categories preserves, reflects, and creates all limits and colimits present in either category.

Definition: F strictly creates a class of limits when every limit cone over FK has a…

FC03-C03-U043 · §3.3, pp. 90–92; L3005 · after FC03-C03-U038

F strictly creates a class of limits when every limit cone over FK has a unique lifted cone over K and that lift is limiting; dual for colimits.

Definition: An indexing category is connected when nonempty and every two objects are…
