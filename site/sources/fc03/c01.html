Chapter 1. Categories, Functors, and Natural Transformations (pp. 1–48)

FC03 — Riehl, Category Theory in Context.

This chapter contains 27 definitions, 2 theorems, 2 propositions, 18 lemmas, 4 corollaries, 26 examples, 9 constructions, 9 remarks, 6 conventions, 1 criterion and 3 other statements.

Nobody has checked this chapter against Lean yet, so the statements below say nothing about whether they are formalized.

Definition: Category

FC03-C01-U001 · §1.1, pp. 3–8; L295

Category: objects and arrows with specified domains/codomains, identity arrows, and associative unital composition.

Remark: Objects are recoverable from identity arrows

FC03-C01-U002 · §1.1, pp. 3–8; L325 · after FC03-C01-U001

Objects are recoverable from identity arrows; equivalently a category may be specified by its arrows, partial composition, and identities.

Example: Concrete categories

FC03-C01-U003 · §1.1, pp. 3–8; L329 · after FC03-C01-U001

Concrete categories: Set, Top and pointed variants, Group/Ring/Field, Mod_R/Vect_k/Ab, Graph/DirGraph, Man, Meas, Poset, Ch_R, and Model_T, with their standard structure-preserving maps.

Example: Abstract examples

FC03-C01-U004 · §1.1, pp. 3–8; L355 · after FC03-C01-U001

Abstract examples: Mat_R; one-object categories BG of monoids/groups; preorders/posets and ordinal categories; discrete categories; Htpy; and Measure.

Definition: A category is small when its collection of arrows is a set.

FC03-C01-U005 · §1.1, pp. 3–8; L397 · after FC03-C01-U001

A category is small when its collection of arrows is a set.

Convention/notation

FC03-C01-U006 · §1.1, pp. 3–8; L399 · after FC03-C01-U005

For a small category C, write ob C and mor C for its sets of objects and morphisms; domain, codomain, and identity are set maps.

Definition: A category is locally small when each collection of morphisms between a…

FC03-C01-U007 · §1.1, pp. 3–8; L407 · after FC03-C01-U001

A category is locally small when each collection of morphisms between a fixed pair of objects is a set.

Convention/notation

FC03-C01-U008 · §1.1, pp. 3–8; L409 · after FC03-C01-U007

For locally small C, the hom-set from X to Y is C(X,Y), also written Hom(X,Y).

Definition: An isomorphism f:X→Y has a two-sided inverse

FC03-C01-U009 · §1.1, pp. 3–8; L419 · after FC03-C01-U001

An isomorphism f:X→Y has a two-sided inverse; write X≅Y.

Definition/notation

FC03-C01-U010 · §1.1, pp. 3–8; L421 · after FC03-C01-U009

An endomorphism has equal domain and codomain; an automorphism is an invertible endomorphism.

Example: Isomorphisms specialize to bijections in Set, bijective algebraic…

FC03-C01-U011 · §1.1, pp. 3–8; L423 · after FC03-C01-U009, FC03-C01-U003

Isomorphisms specialize to bijections in Set, bijective algebraic homomorphisms, homeomorphisms in Top, homotopy equivalences in Htpy, and only identities in posets.

Definition: A groupoid is a category in which every morphism is an isomorphism.

FC03-C01-U012 · §1.1, pp. 3–8; L437 · after FC03-C01-U009

A groupoid is a category in which every morphism is an isomorphism.

Example: A group is a one-object groupoid

FC03-C01-U013 · §1.1, pp. 3–8; L437 · after FC03-C01-U012, FC03-C01-U004

A group is a one-object groupoid; the fundamental groupoid Π₁(X) has points of X as objects and endpoint-preserving homotopy classes of paths as morphisms.

Definition: A subcategory has selected objects and morphisms closed under identities and…

FC03-C01-U014 · §1.1, pp. 3–8; L443 · after FC03-C01-U001

A subcategory has selected objects and morphisms closed under identities and composition; e.g. CRing ⊂ Ring ⊂ Rng.

Lemma: Every category has a maximal subgroupoid

FC03-C01-U015 · §1.1, pp. 3–8; L445 · after FC03-C01-U009, FC03-C01-U012

Every category has a maximal subgroupoid: all objects and exactly the isomorphisms.

Definition: The opposite category C^op has the same objects and reversed arrows, with…

FC03-C01-U016 · §1.2, pp. 9–12; L489 · after FC03-C01-U001

The opposite category C^op has the same objects and reversed arrows, with reversed composition.

Construction/comparison

FC03-C01-U017 · §1.2, pp. 9–12; L509 · after FC03-C01-U016

Passing to C^op is involutive information-wise and exhibits the syntactic self-duality of the category axioms.

Example: Opposites of MatR reverse matrix handedness

FC03-C01-U018 · §1.2, pp. 9–12; L511 · after FC03-C01-U016, FC03-C01-U004

Opposites of Mat_R reverse matrix handedness; opposite preorders reverse order; (BG)op≅B(Gop), with G^op governing right actions.

Convention/principle

FC03-C01-U019 · §1.2, pp. 9–12; L525 · after FC03-C01-U016, FC03-C01-U017

Duality principle: a theorem universally quantified over categories yields a dual theorem by reversing every arrow.

Lemma: For f:x→y, the following are equivalent

FC03-C01-U020 · §1.2, pp. 9–12; L529 · after FC03-C01-U009, FC03-C01-U008

For f:x→y, the following are equivalent: f is an isomorphism; postcomposition C(c,x)→C(c,y) is bijective for every c; precomposition C(y,c)→C(x,c) is bijective for every c.

Remark: In a locally small category, isomorphisms are detected representably by…

FC03-C01-U021 · §1.2, pp. 9–12; L545 · after FC03-C01-U020, FC03-C01-U007

In a locally small category, isomorphisms are detected representably by isomorphisms of all postcomposition (equivalently precomposition) hom-set maps.

Definition: A monomorphism is left-cancellable and an epimorphism is right-cancellable

FC03-C01-U022 · §1.2, pp. 9–12; L585 · after FC03-C01-U001, FC03-C01-U019

A monomorphism is left-cancellable and an epimorphism is right-cancellable; the notions are dual.

Comparison

FC03-C01-U023 · §1.2, pp. 9–12; L593 · after FC03-C01-U022, FC03-C01-U008

Equivalently, f is monic iff every postcomposition map on hom-sets is injective; f is epic iff every precomposition map is injective.

Example: In Set, monomorphisms are exactly injections and epimorphisms exactly…

FC03-C01-U024 · §1.2, pp. 9–12; L599 · after FC03-C01-U022, FC03-C01-U003

In Set, monomorphisms are exactly injections and epimorphisms exactly surjections.

Example: If rs=1, then s is a section/split mono, r a retraction/split epi, and x is…

FC03-C01-U025 · §1.2, pp. 9–12; L605 · after FC03-C01-U022

If rs=1, then s is a section/split mono, r a retraction/split epi, and x is a retract of y.

Example: Every isomorphism is monic and epic, but the converse fails

FC03-C01-U026 · §1.2, pp. 9–12; L609 · after FC03-C01-U009, FC03-C01-U022, FC03-C01-U025

Every isomorphism is monic and epic, but the converse fails: Z→Q is monic and epic in Ring without being an isomorphism.

Lemma: Composites of monos are mono and monicity of gf implies monicity of f

FC03-C01-U027 · §1.2, pp. 9–12; L613 · after FC03-C01-U022, FC03-C01-U019

Composites of monos are mono and monicity of gf implies monicity of f; dually composites of epis are epi and epicity of gf implies epicity of g.

Definition: A functor F:C→D assigns objects and morphisms preserving domains/codomains,…

FC03-C01-U028 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L657 · after FC03-C01-U001

A functor F:C→D assigns objects and morphisms preserving domains/codomains, identities, and composition.

Example: Functor examples include covariant power set/direct image, forgetful and…

FC03-C01-U029 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L671 · after FC03-C01-U028, FC03-C01-U003, FC03-C01-U013

Functor examples include covariant power set/direct image, forgetful and inclusion functors, homotopy quotients, π₁ and Π₁, cycle/boundary/homology, free groups, the derivative/Jacobian, and the Fin_* construction attached to a commutative monoid.

Theorem: Brouwer fixed point theorem

FC03-C01-U030 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L699 · after FC03-C01-U028, FC03-C01-U029, FC03-C01-U025

Brouwer fixed point theorem: every continuous endomorphism of D² has a fixed point.

Example: Clustering as functorial data

FC03-C01-U031 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L719 · after FC03-C01-U028

Clustering as functorial data: FinMetric→Cluster admits only trivial scale-invariant clusterings under the stated conditions; persistent clusters form PCluster, supporting a distinguished persistent-clustering functor.

Definition: A contravariant functor C→D is a covariant functor C^op→D; it reverses…

FC03-C01-U032 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L731 · after FC03-C01-U016, FC03-C01-U028

A contravariant functor C→D is a covariant functor C^op→D; it reverses arrows and the order of composition.

Convention/notation

FC03-C01-U033 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L743 · after FC03-C01-U032

For contravariant functors, arrows are drawn in the original direction in C and their images in the reversed direction; composites retain the usual C-order.

Example: Contravariant examples

FC03-C01-U034 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L751 · after FC03-C01-U032, FC03-C01-U033

Contravariant examples: inverse-image power set, vector-space dual, open/closed-subset functors, Spec on commutative rings, presheaves, and simplicial sets.

Lemma: Functors preserve isomorphisms.

FC03-C01-U035 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L769 · after FC03-C01-U028, FC03-C01-U009

Functors preserve isomorphisms.

Example: A functor BG→C is a left G-action on an object of C; BG^op→C is a right action

FC03-C01-U036 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L779 · after FC03-C01-U028, FC03-C01-U004, FC03-C01-U018

A functor BG→C is a left G-action on an object of C; BG^op→C is a right action. In Set, Vect_k, Top these are G-sets, representations, and G-spaces.

Corollary: A functorial G-action acts by automorphisms and (g){-1}=(g{-1}).

FC03-C01-U037 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L793 · after FC03-C01-U036, FC03-C01-U035

A functorial G-action acts by automorphisms and (g_*){-1}=(g{-1})_*.

Lemma/construction

FC03-C01-U038 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L795 · after FC03-C01-U028, FC03-C01-U025

Every functor preserves split monomorphisms and split epimorphisms.

Definition: For locally small C and c∈C, C(c,-):C→Set and C(-,c):C^op→Set are the…

FC03-C01-U039 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L797 · after FC03-C01-U007, FC03-C01-U020, FC03-C01-U032

For locally small C and c∈C, C(c,-):C→Set and C(-,c):C^op→Set are the covariant and contravariant functors represented by c, acting by post- and precomposition.

Terminology

FC03-C01-U040 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L809 · after FC03-C01-U028

A bifunctor is a functor of two variables.

Definition: The product category C×D has object and morphism pairs, with identities and…

FC03-C01-U041 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L811 · after FC03-C01-U001

The product category C×D has object and morphism pairs, with identities and composition componentwise.

Definition: For locally small C, the two-sided represented bifunctor C(-,-):C^op×C→Set…

FC03-C01-U042 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L819 · after FC03-C01-U039, FC03-C01-U041

For locally small C, the two-sided represented bifunctor C(-,-):C^op×C→Set sends (f,h) to g↦hgf.

Convention/notation

FC03-C01-U043 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L837 · after FC03-C01-U005, FC03-C01-U007, FC03-C01-U028

Cat is the locally small category of small categories and functors; CAT has locally small categories and functors as objects, with Cat↪CAT.

Definition: An isomorphism of categories is a pair of strictly inverse functors.

FC03-C01-U044 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L841 · after FC03-C01-U043, FC03-C01-U009

An isomorphism of categories is a pair of strictly inverse functors.

Example: Canonical category isomorphisms

FC03-C01-U045 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L843 · after FC03-C01-U044, FC03-C01-U016, FC03-C01-U012

Canonical category isomorphisms: C↦C^op; BG≅BG^op via inversion; any groupoid≅its opposite; left R-modules≅right R^op-modules; opens≅closed^op by complement; Mat_R≅Mat_R^op by transpose.

Example: For a finite Galois extension E/F with G=Aut(E/F), the fixed-field functor…

FC03-C01-U046 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L859 · after FC03-C01-U044, FC03-C01-U032

For a finite Galois extension E/F with G=Aut(E/F), the fixed-field functor yields an isomorphism O_Gop≅Field_FE (categorical fundamental theorem of Galois theory).

Construction: Set^∂ has sets as objects and partially defined functions as morphisms, with…

FC03-C01-U047 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L867 · after FC03-C01-U001

Set^∂ has sets as objects and partially defined functions as morphisms, with composition on the maximal compatible domain.

Construction/comparison

FC03-C01-U048 · §1.3, pp. 13–22; L869 · after FC03-C01-U047, FC03-C01-U028

Adding a disjoint basepoint defines (-)*+:Set^∂→Set**; deleting the basepoint defines U:Set_*→Set^∂; U(-)_+ is strictly identity while (U-)₊ is only objectwise isomorphic to identity.

Definition: A natural transformation α:F⇒G consists of components αc:Fc→Gc satisfying…

FC03-C01-U049 · §1.4, pp. 23–28; L947 · after FC03-C01-U028, FC03-C01-U009

A natural transformation α:F⇒G consists of components α_c:Fc→Gc satisfying the naturality square for every f:c→c′; it is a natural isomorphism when every component is invertible.

Example: Examples/comparisons
