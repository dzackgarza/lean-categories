Chapter 2. Representability and the Yoneda Lemma (pp. 49–72)

FC03 — Riehl, Category Theory in Context.

This chapter contains 6 definitions, 1 theorem, 5 propositions, 1 lemma, 4 corollaries, 17 examples, 2 remarks, 3 conventions and 1 other statements.

Nobody has checked this chapter against Lean yet, so the statements below say nothing about whether they are formalized.

Example: Discrete dynamical systems

FC03-C02-U001 · §2.1, pp. 50–54; L1745 · after FC03-C01-U092

Discrete dynamical systems: (N,s,0) is universal—given (X,f,x₀), there is a unique r:N→X with r(0)=x₀ and r∘s=f∘r.

Definition: Representable characterization

FC03-C02-U002 · §2.1, pp. 50–54; L1761 · after FC03-C01-U092, FC03-C01-U039, FC03-C01-U049

Representable characterization: c is initial iff C(c,-) is naturally the constant singleton functor; c is terminal iff C(-,c) is.

Definition: A set-valued covariant/contravariant functor is representable when naturally…

FC03-C02-U003 · §2.1, pp. 50–54; L1769 · after FC03-C01-U039, FC03-C01-U049

A set-valued covariant/contravariant functor is representable when naturally isomorphic to C(c,-)/C(-,c); a representation includes the chosen c and chosen natural isomorphism.

Example: Covariant representables

FC03-C02-U004 · §2.1, pp. 50–54; L1779 · after FC03-C02-U003, FC03-C01-U056

Covariant representables: identity Set by 1; underlying Group/Mod_R/Ring by Z/R/Z[x]; tuples by free groups/modules; units of rings by Z[x,x^{-1}]; points/objects/arrows/isomorphisms/paths by walking objects; based-set underlying functor; path and loop functors.

Terminology

FC03-C02-U005 · §2.1, pp. 50–54; L1825 · after FC03-C02-U003

Covariant representable universal constructions are commonly called free; the dual term is cofree.

Example: Contravariant representables

FC03-C02-U006 · §2.1, pp. 50–54; L1827 · after FC03-C02-U003, FC03-C01-U032

Contravariant representables: power set by Ω={⊤,⊥}; opens/closed subsets by the Sierpiński space; Hom(-×A,B) by B^A; vector dual by k; cohomology by K(A,n); principal G-bundles by BG.

Example: For ordinal ω and F:ω→Set, natural transformations ω(k,-)⇒F are in bijection…

FC03-C02-U007 · §2.2, pp. 55–61; L1891 · after FC03-C02-U003, FC03-C01-U049

For ordinal ω and F:ω→Set, natural transformations ω(k,-)⇒F are in bijection with Fk, determined by the image of id_k.

Example: For BG, the covariant and contravariant representables are the regular…

FC03-C02-U008 · §2.2, pp. 55–61; L1899 · after FC03-C02-U003, FC03-C01-U036

For BG, the covariant and contravariant representables are the regular left/right G-set G; equivariant maps G→X are determined by the identity element.

Proposition: For a left G-set X, G-equivariant maps G→X correspond bijectively to…

FC03-C02-U009 · §2.2, pp. 55–61; L1905 · after FC03-C02-U008

For a left G-set X, G-equivariant maps G→X correspond bijectively to elements of X via evaluation at e∈G.

Theorem: Yoneda lemma

FC03-C02-U010 · §2.2, pp. 55–61; L1909 · after FC03-C02-U003, FC03-C01-U007, FC03-C01-U049

Yoneda lemma: for locally small C, F:C→Set, c∈C, Nat(C(c,-),F)≅Fc via α↦α_c(id_c), naturally in c and F; dually for presheaves.

Remark: Yoneda is a natural isomorphism between the natural-transformation bifunctor…

FC03-C02-U011 · §2.2, pp. 55–61; L1993 · after FC03-C02-U010, FC03-C01-U042

Yoneda is a natural isomorphism between the natural-transformation bifunctor Nat(y(-),-) and evaluation; it also shows the relevant transformation collection is a set.

Corollary: The covariant and contravariant Yoneda embeddings Cop→SetC and…

FC03-C02-U012 · §2.2, pp. 55–61; L2017 · after FC03-C02-U010, FC03-C01-U061

The covariant and contravariant Yoneda embeddings Cop→SetC and C→Set{Cop} are full and faithful.

Convention: Common convention calls both Theorem 2.2.4 and its full-faithfulness…

FC03-C02-U013 · §2.2, pp. 55–61; L2047 · after FC03-C02-U010, FC03-C02-U012

Common convention calls both Theorem 2.2.4 and its full-faithfulness Corollary 2.2.8 “the Yoneda lemma.”

Corollary: Every row operation on matrices with n rows is left multiplication by a…

FC03-C02-U014 · §2.2, pp. 55–61; L2051 · after FC03-C02-U012

Every row operation on matrices with n rows is left multiplication by a unique n×n matrix, namely its value on the identity matrix.

Corollary: Cayley theorem

FC03-C02-U015 · §2.2, pp. 55–61; L2057 · after FC03-C02-U012, FC03-C01-U036

Cayley theorem: every group is isomorphic to a subgroup of a permutation group.

Proposition: Objects x,y are isomorphic iff their covariant representables are naturally…

FC03-C02-U016 · §2.3, pp. 62–65; L2091 · after FC03-C02-U012, FC03-C01-U009

Objects x,y are isomorphic iff their covariant representables are naturally isomorphic (equivalently for contravariant representables); hence any two representing objects of one functor are isomorphic.

Convention: A representing object is unique up to the unique isomorphism compatible with…

FC03-C02-U017 · §2.3, pp. 62–65; L2099 · after FC03-C02-U016

A representing object is unique up to the unique isomorphism compatible with the chosen representations; “the” is used in this canonical-isomorphism sense.

Corollary: The full subcategory of terminal objects is empty or a contractible groupoid

FC03-C02-U018 · §2.3, pp. 62–65; L2101 · after FC03-C02-U016, FC03-C02-U002

The full subcategory of terminal objects is empty or a contractible groupoid; in particular any two terminal objects are uniquely isomorphic.

Definition: A contractible groupoid is equivalent to 1

FC03-C02-U019 · §2.3, pp. 62–65; L2103 · after FC03-C01-U012, FC03-C01-U057

A contractible groupoid is equivalent to 1; explicitly it is nonempty with exactly one morphism in every hom-set.

Definition: A universal property of c is a representable functor F together with a…

FC03-C02-U020 · §2.3, pp. 62–65; L2109 · after FC03-C02-U003, FC03-C02-U010

A universal property of c is a representable functor F together with a universal element x∈Fc that, via Yoneda, classifies the representing natural isomorphism.

Z[x] represents U:Ring→Set with universal element x; Ring(Z[x],R)≅UR by evaluation at x.

A left G-set is representable iff it is a nonempty free transitive G-set, i.e. a G-torsor; a representation is selected by a universal element.

FC03-C02-U024 · §2.3, pp. 62–65; L2123 · after FC03-C02-U020

Tensor product V⊗W represents U↦Bilin(V,W;U); its universal element is the universal bilinear map, through which every bilinear map factors uniquely.

Proposition: For vector spaces V,W, there is a canonical isomorphism V⊗W≅W⊗V, induced by…

The tensor symmetry is the unique map induced by the universal bilinear map (v,w)↦w⊗v; its inverse is induced by swapping back.

The group ring Z[G] represents R↦Group(G,R^×); its universal element is the canonical homomorphism G→Z[G]^×.
