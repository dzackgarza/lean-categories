# Feature Specification: The algebraic categories of the catalogue

**Feature Branch**: `009-algebraic-category-catalogue` (work lands on `main`)

**Created**: 2026-11-25

**Status**: Planned

**Input**: The algebraic phases of the catalogue: the integral-domain tower (#55); free-plus-torsion
modules over integral domains (#101); rings, ideals, fractional ideals, localizations and
completions (#102); the category of groups (#103); group actions on modules (#104); finitely
generated groups and their subgroup constructions (#106); chain complexes and cohomology (#107);
graded and differential graded algebras (#108). Every open point is settled in
[research.md](research.md).

The feature registers the algebraic categories that the catalogue owes, at their generating
level, with their structural functors, universal properties and standard objects, over their
Mathlib owners. The audits of Sage's interfaces named in these issues are not requirements of
this repository (research D8).

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - A consumer reads the divisibility tower (Priority: P1)

A consumer reads $\mathbb Z$ as a Euclidean domain, a principal ideal domain, a unique
factorization domain and an integral domain through registered structural functors; $\mathbb Z[x]$
as a unique factorization domain that is not a principal ideal domain; $\mathbb Q[x]$ and
$\mathbb C[x]$ as Euclidean domains.

**Why this priority**: factorization, gcd and primality are methods of these categories (#55;
feature 001 FR-036, FR-037).

**Independent Test**: the tower test module elaborates the objects and the functors; $\mathbb Z[x]$
is refused as a principal ideal domain by the ideal $(2, x)$.

---

### User Story 2 - A mathematician uses rings, ideals and their constructions (Priority: P1)

The mathematician forms $R/I$ with its universal property, a fractional ideal as an $R$-submodule of
$\operatorname{Frac}(R)$, a localization $S^{-1}R$ and $S^{-1}M$, an $I$-adic completion, and the
regular module $R$ in $\operatorname{Mod}(R)$.

**Why this priority**: these are the scalar constructions of the module and lattice spine (#102).

**Independent Test**: $\mathbb Z/(2)$ has the universal property of the quotient; the fractional
ideal $\tfrac12\mathbb Z$ is invertible; $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ is the localization at $p$.

---

### User Story 3 - A mathematician uses groups, actions and presentations (Priority: P2)

The mathematician forms $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal C}(X)$, a $G$-set and its permutation
representation, a presented group $\langle S \mid R \rangle$, the invariant factors of a finite
abelian group, a finitely generated group, a stabilizer, centralizer, normalizer or intersection,
and the invariants and coinvariants of a $G$-module.

**Why this priority**: orthogonal groups, their stabilizers and their actions on discriminant
forms are core vocabulary of the lattice programme (#103, #104, #106).

**Independent Test**: $\mathbb Z/4 \times \mathbb Z/6$ has invariant factors $(2, 12)$; a
finite-index subgroup of a finitely generated group is finitely generated by a theorem; the
invariants of the swap on $\mathbb Z^2$ are the diagonal.

---

### User Story 4 - A mathematician uses complexes and algebras (Priority: P2)

The mathematician forms a chain complex of $R$-modules, a chain map, a homotopy, a cone, a shift,
a truncation, tensor and Hom complexes, the homology modules and the long exact sequence of a
short exact sequence; a graded algebra, a differential graded algebra, a Clifford algebra and an
exterior algebra.

**Why this priority**: cohomology theories and their rings share one interface (#107, #108).

**Independent Test**: a proposed differential with $d^2 \neq 0$ is refused; the long exact sequence
of $0 \to \mathbb Z \xrightarrow{2} \mathbb Z \to \mathbb Z/2 \to 0$ elaborates; the Clifford
algebra of a quadratic form has its universal property.

---

### User Story 5 - A mathematician uses certified free-plus-torsion modules (Priority: P3)

Over an integral domain $R$, the mathematician forms a module with a chosen splitting
$M \cong F \oplus T$, $F$ finite free and $T$ torsion, and the operations that preserve such a
splitting under stated hypotheses.

**Why this priority**: the presentation tooling of modules extends past principal ideal domains
where it honestly can (#101).

**Independent Test**: over $\mathbb Z$ every finitely generated module has a splitting; over
$\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]$ the ideal $(2, 1 + \sqrt{-5})$ is torsion-free and not free, so it has no
splitting with $T = 0$.

---

### Edge Cases

- A Euclidean structure is data; a domain can carry several.
- Over a domain, torsion means annihilation by a nonzero element; arbitrary commutative rings are
  outside this feature (research D3).
- Finite generation of a group does not pass to an arbitrary subgroup.
- A presented group and a group isomorphic to it are different objects related by a chosen
  isomorphism.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001 (#55)**: Integral domains, unique factorization domains with a chosen normalization,
  principal ideal domains and Euclidean domains MUST be registered: integral domains, UFDs and
  PIDs as property classifiers, the Euclidean structure and the normalization as structure, with
  the structural functors $\mathbf{Euc} \to \mathbf{PID} \to \mathbf{UFD} \to \mathbf{Dom} \to \mathbf{CRing}$,
  the category of elements of the underlying set of UFDs, and the objects $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Z[x]$,
  $\mathbb Q[x]$, $\mathbb C[x]$ at their entry categories (research D2).
- **FR-002 (#101)**: Over an integral domain $R$, the category of modules with a chosen splitting
  $\varphi : F \oplus T \cong M$ ($F$ finite free, $T$ torsion) MUST be registered with its
  forgetful functor to $\operatorname{Mod}(R)$, whose essential image is the property category,
  and direct sums MUST carry the induced splitting (research D3).
- **FR-003 (#101)**: Kernels, images, cokernels, tensor products, Hom, localization and base change
  MUST each be classified by a theorem: under its stated hypotheses it returns a splitting, or it
  is not an operation of the category.
- **FR-004 (#102)**: The quotient $R/I$ with its universal property, fractional ideals as
  $R$-submodules of $\operatorname{Frac}(R)$ with their product, the localization functors
  $S^{-1}$ on rings and modules with their universal properties, the $I$-adic completion functor and
  the regular-module section $R \mapsto {}_R R$ MUST be registered
  ([FOUNDATIONS.md](../../FOUNDATIONS.md) Def. 13.3, §49).
- **FR-005 (#103)**: Groups and abelian groups, the generic $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal C}$, $G$-sets
  as functors $BG \to \mathbf{Set}$ with their permutation representations, presented groups with
  their comparison to groups, and the invariant factors of finite abelian groups as an invariant
  on $\operatorname{Core}(\mathbf{FinAb})$ MUST be registered (research D4).
- **FR-006 (#104)**: $G$-modules over $R$ as functors $BG \to \operatorname{Mod}(R)$, invariants as
  the right adjoint and coinvariants as the left adjoint of the trivial-action functor, and the
  saturation theorem $M^G = M \cap (M \otimes \mathbb Q)^G$ over $\mathbb Z$ MUST be registered
  (research D5). The lattice case is feature 003 N10.
- **FR-007 (#106)**: Finitely generated groups as a property of groups; stabilizers,
  centralizers, normalizers and intersections as subgroup constructions; the theorem that a
  finite-index subgroup of a finitely generated group is finitely generated; generating families
  as `choice` operations (feature 001 FR-017) MUST be registered.
- **FR-008 (#107)**: Chain complexes of $R$-modules with chain maps, homotopies, cones, shifts,
  truncations, tensor and Hom complexes, homology functors and the long exact sequence MUST be
  registered at their corpus owners, and every registered cohomology theory MUST factor through
  them (research D6).
- **FR-009 (#108)**: Graded algebras, differential graded algebras with the Leibniz rule under the
  Koszul sign, Lie algebras, Clifford and exterior algebras as functors from quadratic and plain
  modules, and the cohomology ring of a multiplicative complex MUST be registered (research D7).
- **FR-010**: Each category of this feature MUST have its registry row with its structural
  functors (constitution M7).
- **FR-011 (process)**: Every requirement MUST pass constitution M2, M3, M8 and E1.
